## The problem, plainly

- You pitch brands you should have walked away from, because nobody had time to research them properly.
- First emails sound like everyone else's, because the AI does not know which of your projects actually fit that client.
- Calls end without the budget, the decision maker, or the deadline, because nobody asked the awkward questions.
- Quotes go out as one bare number, so every pushback becomes a discount instead of a conversation about scope.
- Proposals quietly give away free creative, because showing ideas feels like the only way to win.
- Finished projects end the relationship, because nobody makes the ongoing-work offer at the moment the client is happiest.

## How it solves it

Eight instruction files, one per selling job. Each one carries the full playbook for that job: what to ask, what to check, what never to offer, and how to handle the unusual cases. You bring the material you already have: a client email, messy call notes, your price list. The assistant does the disciplined part and hands you a draft.

Two rules are built into every file. The assistant is instructed to never invent a fact, a result, a price, or a term: anything missing is marked for you to confirm instead. And it never sends anything on its own. It drafts, you check, you send.

## See the difference

## How it works

1. **Tell it about your studio, once.** A one-page fact sheet: your work, your prices, your typical terms. About 15 minutes, with a filled-in sample to copy from. The assistant can even interview you to fill it in.
2. **Add the files to your AI.** Upload them into a project or a chat. That is the whole setup. No software to install, nothing technical to configure.
3. **Ask for the job you need.** "Research this brand." "Price this project." "They say we are too expensive, help me reply." You get a draft that follows the playbook and flags its own gaps, ready for your review.

## The eight jobs

1. **Research a brand before you pitch.** Real reasons they might buy now, with sources and dates where your assistant can browse the web, whether your work fits, and a straight answer: pursue, watch, or walk away.
2. **Write a first email that fits.** Built on the one or two of your projects that genuinely match that client. If none match, it tells you instead of writing fluff.
3. **Run a first call properly.** Preparation beforehand; afterwards, your messy notes become a clear brief, with a verdict on what to do next.
4. **Turn notes into a proposal.** Structured, priced from your scope, and with zero free design concepts of the client's actual product.
5. **Price the job without guessing.** Three options from your rates, with rounds, extras, and exclusions written down, so the price can defend itself.
6. **Answer "too expensive" and procurement.** Discount demands, free pitch requests, forms and terms. Trade scope, keep your rates, offer nothing unapproved.
7. **Turn a finished project into monthly work.** A retainer offer built on what the client keeps needing, priced from your rates, with a fair exit for both sides.
8. **Restart a stalled deal, or earn an introduction.** Follow-ups based on something that really changed, and introduction requests asked at the right moment, when a client is happy with finished work. If a client ever said "do not contact us," the assistant will not draft a single word for them.

Plus an industry file for your world: packaging, brand and product design, fashion, or agencies. Its buyers, its calendar, its pricing habits.

## Your tools, without the technical part

The normal way to use the suite is copy and paste: the client email, the call notes, the price list. It works fully that way.

If your studio is more technical, the assistant can also read from your design app, your CRM, or your documents directly once connected. That is an optional extra with its own setup guide. It is not needed, and it is not the pitch.

## Honest limits

The assistant follows these files carefully, and they are built to show their working and flag anything unchecked. It can still make mistakes, so you read every draft before a client sees it. If your AI cannot browse the web on your plan, research works from what you paste in, and the files instruct it to say so. Contract wording in the templates is a starting draft for your own adviser, not legal advice.

## FAQ

**I am not technical. Is this for me?** Yes, it was written for you. If you can upload a file to a chat and paste an email, you can use the whole suite. The long files are for the AI to read, not you. Your reading starts and ends with the five-minute Start Here page.

**What exactly is a "skill"?** One instruction file that teaches your assistant one job, in depth: the steps, the judgement calls, the templates, and the mistakes to avoid. You upload it once per project, then just ask.

**Which AI do I need?** Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini, on a plan that lets you upload files. Projects (Claude Projects, ChatGPT projects) are the smoothest way to keep the files in one place, but a plain chat works.

**Will it make things up?** It is instructed not to, and built to mark gaps instead of filling them: [TO CONFIRM] where a decision or number is yours, [UNVERIFIED] where a fact could not be checked. You still give every draft a final read.

**Is my client information safe?** The files themselves send nothing anywhere; they are text. What your assistant sees is only what you paste or connect, and you approve every draft before anything leaves your desk.

**Why not just write my own prompts?** A prompt is one paragraph of good intentions. These files carry the whole job: the steps, the awkward questions, the pricing structure, the unusual cases, and a self-check, every session, without you re-explaining your studio each time.

**What does it cost?** Nothing. It is free under the MIT license. Use it, adapt it, share it.
