# Example 8: One-Page Proposal for a Small Job

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative, taken from a fictional scope output and reproduced exactly.

## Inputs

- Situation: an existing client, a regional coffee roaster (fictional), needs artwork adaptation for 3 new bag sizes on an existing, studio-designed range, plus an updated back-of-pack panel for a blend change. Known dieline, known system, four week window, no deadline pressure. Decision maker: the operations manager the studio already works with.
- Scope output: single fixed fee built from the per-SKU adaptation rate plus a half day of artworking for the panel change; one revision round included (proportionate for adaptation work on an approved system); standard terms apply from the standing client relationship.
- Format call: Tree 1 modifier. Small job, existing relationship, no meeting, no forwarding expected. The one-page proposal template in action: actually the proposal document template compressed onto one page, which is how the skill handles this size of job rather than reaching for a flagship structure that would embarrass the fee.

## The one-pager (complete)

---

**Hartwell Studio · Artwork: three new bag sizes and blend panel update · [Date] · v1**

**The job.** Adapt the approved range design to three new bag sizes (250g, 500g, 1kg whole bean) on the existing dieline family, and update the back-of-pack panel on the house blend for the new blend composition. Your system, your printer, no design changes.

**What you get.** Print-ready artwork for 3 SKUs; updated back panel artwork for 1 SKU; one consolidated revision round; files to your printer's specification, as before.

**Not included.** Design changes to the range system, new photography or licences, structural or dieline changes, print costs. Anything beyond this list is quoted before it is done, as usual.

**Timeline.** Two weeks from go-ahead for first artwork, inside your four week window, with your panel copy confirmed at the start. If the blend copy changes after artwork, it is a small change order, priced before we touch it.

**Investment.** [Fixed fee exactly as the scope output states it], plus VAT [tax treatment per rate sheet]. Invoiced on file release under our standing terms; deposit waived per the existing relationship terms recorded in the scope output.

**Next step.** Reply "go" and we start Monday. [Drafter's note: terms summary and the drafting aid flag are carried in the standing client SOW referenced by the scope output; for a new client at this size, the one-pager adds the five-line terms summary instead of relying on a standing document.]

---

## Critique

Strengths: proportionality is the entire skill here. A nine-section proposal for an adaptation job would signal overhead the client will (correctly) expect to be billed for, and would delay a job whose value is its speed. Yet nothing protective was dropped to get to one page: deliverables are countable, exclusions are present, the change trigger on blend copy is named before it happens (it is the single most likely event on this exact job), and the fee is the scope output's fee, not a remembered one.
Watch outs: the waived deposit and standing terms are relationship facts recorded in the scope output, not defaults the skill assumes; for any client without that paper trail, the one-pager carries the standard deposit and the short terms summary, and self-check 10 applies in full. Small documents fail self-checks at the same rate as big ones when drafters relax; the checks do not scale down with the page count.
