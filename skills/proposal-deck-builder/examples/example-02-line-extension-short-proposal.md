# Example 2: Line Extension, Short Proposal

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative, taken from a fictional scope output and reproduced exactly.

## Inputs

- Discovery brief: Bramblewick Kitchen (fictional), existing client. Winter limited edition, 4 SKUs on the existing seasonal family and dieline. Shelf date early November; copy freeze and artwork lock dates already agreed in discovery. Decision maker: founder. No meeting requested; decision from the document.
- Scope output: Good 3,562.50 / Better (recommended) 5,537.50 / Best 7,062.50, built from the seasonal concept direction phase plus per-SKU artwork at 387.50 per adaptation, a late-copy change order term agreed up front, no rush needed because the calendar was planned.
- Library: the studio designed the client's existing seasonal family (named use, outcome citable as recorded), plus one other seasonal case, imagery only.
- Format: Tree 1 modifier applies (small, existing relationship, document decision): short proposal, four pages, no deck.

## The proposal (condensed in full)

**Page 1. The job.** The extension in one paragraph: four winter SKUs into the family the studio built last year, on the same dieline, to the November date, with the copy freeze the client already agreed. One line on the risk extensions carry: dilution. The existing family stays the hero; the edition reads as its winter chapter, not a new range competing with it.

**Page 2. Approach and evidence.** Three phases: concept direction for the edition (one round of routes inside the paid scope, which is where routes belong), master artwork, adaptations. The client's own family shown once, full-bleed, as the architecture the edition extends. The second case appears as a single image with a deliverables line only; no outcome language, per its permission.

**Page 3. Scope and investment.** Tiers transcribed exactly. Per-SKU logic explained in two lines: adaptations at 387.50, so a fifth SKU prices itself if the range grows, by change order. Exclusions: copy, print costs, licences. The late-copy term stated where the client will see it now, not at SOW stage: regulated content supplied or changed after the freeze is a change order and dates move day for day unless rush is elected in writing.

**Page 4. Timeline and next step.** Backwards from shelf: files to printer late September, artwork lock mid September, copy freeze early September, kickoff on deposit in early August. Next step: approve Better by reply; deposit invoice same day. Terms summary in five lines with the drafting aid flag.

## Critique

Strengths: proportion. Four pages for a four-SKU extension from an existing system signals the speed the client is actually buying, and the per-SKU arithmetic lets the client extend the job without a new negotiation. The late-copy term appears in the proposal because last year's project was burned by exactly that, and both sides remember.
Watch outs: short proposals tempt drafters to drop sections; this one keeps exclusions and terms summary at full plainness. The imagery-only case nearly acquired the sentence "sold through in six weeks" in drafting; the library records no such permission, so the sentence died at self-check 4, as it should.
