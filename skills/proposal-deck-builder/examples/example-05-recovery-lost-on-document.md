# Example 5: Recovery, Lost on Document Quality, Rebuilt

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Figures in the rebuild come from a fictional scope output and are reproduced exactly.

## What happened

Hartwell Studio (fictional) pitched the Tern and Co pet food range redesign the previous quarter and lost. The debrief, obtained by the founder on a call the client agreed to, was specific and uncomfortable: "Your work was the strongest we saw. Your document was the worst we received. The price appeared before we understood the scope, the team page showed two people who never appeared again, the timeline ignored our printer completely, and our finance lead could not find the payment terms. We chose the studio whose paper we could approve."

The client has now reopened the conversation for a second range. The scope work has been redone properly through the Scope and Pricing Architect. This example is the document rebuild, section by section, against the debrief.

## The audit of the lost document (what the skill produced first)

| Debrief complaint | Root cause in the old document | Fix in the rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| Price before scope | Price sat on page 2 as a single number, no tiers, no deliverables beside it | Price in Section 8, tiers transcribed from the scope output with deliverables attached; deck reaches price at minute 25 |
| Ghost team page | Stock team page recycled from a credentials deck, including a designer who had left | Team rebuilt from the actual project staffing; every name carries the role they will play and one of their own past projects |
| Timeline ignored the printer | Timeline showed studio phases only, ending at "files delivered" | Three-lane timeline: studio, client obligations (copy freeze, consolidated feedback, regulatory sign-off), and the printer's proofing window, built back from the shelf date |
| Finance could not find terms | Terms existed only in an email thread, softer than the eventual SOW | Terms summary as Section 9, reconciled line by line with the scope output, flagged as drafting aid |
| (Found in audit, not in debrief) | Old document quoted the range at "around 6,000" from a verbal conversation, with no tiers | Every figure now traces to the signed-off scope output; the verbal number appears nowhere |

## The rebuilt proposal (shape)

Nine sections per the template. Mirror written from the new discovery conversation, including the client's own sentence about why they reopened the conversation, used with their agreement in the call. Proof: the pet food case from another client is imagery only (permission does not cover this use), so the lead case is a household range redesign with a citable outcome, chosen for architecture rhyme. Timeline page built on the timeline template with the printer's standard proof cycle written in as a named supplier lane, flagged [TO CONFIRM] against the client's actual printer dates rather than assumed. Cover email names the version (v1 of the new engagement), states plainly that the studio rebuilt its document process after the debrief, in one line, without grovelling.

## Critique

Strengths: the recovery treats the debrief as a specification. Every complaint maps to a self-check item that now exists (checks 2, 6, 8, 9, 10), which is the real lesson: the lost document would have failed this skill's self-check in five places, and the founder's audit table says so, to the user, without softening. The one line acknowledging the rebuild in the cover email converts a weakness into evidence the studio listens, which is the same faculty the mirror section exists to prove.
Watch outs: a rebuilt document does not win by itself; the client also needs the meeting run well, which is outside this skill. And the audit found the old verbal price still living in the CRM note; the skill flagged it, because a stale "around 6,000" surfacing in contracting would undo the entire rebuild (see edge case 20).
