# Example 7: Brand Identity Proposal (with Packaging to Follow)

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative, taken from a fictional scope output and reproduced exactly.

## Inputs

- Discovery brief: Fernlight Renewal (fictional), a skincare brand with two founders, launching with one formulated product (a serum) and three more in development, unformulated. She needs identity now and packaging as products land. Her words in discovery: "I need a brand that makes the second product look planned, not bolted on." Decision maker: the founder quoted here; both founders sign off identity. No committee, no procurement.
- Scope output: Good 6,975 / Better (recommended) 12,975 / Best 17,987.50. Strategy phase in Better and Best; identity system; packaging master for the hero SKU; future SKUs priced at a locked adaptation rate of 387.50 per adaptation on the existing master format, stated in the proposal. Source files excluded (1,500 per family). Trademark clearance excluded, flagged for her attorney.
- Library: two identity cases (one named with a citable outcome, one anonymised by permission), one identity-plus-packaging case showing a system extending across later SKUs.
- Format: founder, document decision, no meeting set. Tree 1: DOCUMENT ONLY, with the one-page summary as a call aid if she books one.

## The proposal (shape)

1. **Understanding.** The real brief is not a logo; it is a system that survives products that do not exist yet. Written from her sentence, quoted back to her: the second product must look planned.
2. **What we see.** The category's pattern: founders buy a beautiful mark for product one and pay for it again at product three, when the layout principles, type system, and colour logic were never built to stretch. The insight is architectural, not aesthetic, which is why it can be written without designing anything.
3. **Approach.** Strategy, then identity system (mark, colour, type, layout principles, voice), then the hero SKU as the system's first citizen, with the adaptation logic documented so future products are applications, not reinventions.
4. **Relevant work.** The identity-plus-packaging case leads because it proves the stretch: launched with one product, extended to five on the same system. The named identity case supports on craft. The anonymised case is used exactly as its permission allows: descriptor, no name, no outcome.
5. **Scope.** Tiers transcribed. The future-SKU adaptation rate appears in this section, verbatim from the scope output, because for this client the rate is a headline benefit: growth is already priced. Exclusions plain: copywriting, photography, font and image licences, print and production costs, trademark clearance (her attorney; the IP language carries the drafting aid flag).
6. **Team.** Founder-led strategy, one senior designer, stated truthfully; she is buying a small senior team and the proposal says that is the point.
7. **Timeline.** Built from her launch date backwards, with her obligations named: naming decision gate, claims copy, regulatory review of product claims, which is hers and is not a design task.
8. **Investment.** Tiers exact. Best includes naming architecture and guidelines, the pieces that make the system self-serviceable later.
9. **Terms summary and next step.** Approve Better by reply; deposit invoice same day; strategy workshop date offered in the same paragraph.

## Critique

Strengths: the proposal sells the system and the locked adaptation rate, not the reveal. Identity proposals are the genre most vulnerable to spec temptation ("just show her a direction"), and this one contains zero identity work for Fernlight: not a typeface suggestion, not a colour palette, not a "territory". The thinking is demonstrated entirely through the category pattern and a permitted past case. The unformulated products are handled the scope's way: not priced as fiction, but carried at a stated adaptation rate.
Watch outs: founder documents get forwarded to advisors, partners, and occasionally investors; the price and terms survive that read because they were written for it. The trademark exclusion needed the drafting aid flag and got it; in an earlier draft it read like legal advice about clearance likelihood, which this skill does not give.
