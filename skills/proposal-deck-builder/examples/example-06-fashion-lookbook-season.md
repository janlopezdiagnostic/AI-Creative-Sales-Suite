# Example 6: Fashion Label, Lookbook-Season Proposal

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative, taken from a fictional scope output and reproduced exactly.

## Inputs

- Discovery brief: Atelier Vost (fictional), an independent fashion label, two collections a year. The job: lookbook and campaign imagery direction, showroom and line-sheet materials, and the seasonal packaging suite (boxes, tissue, tags, carrier) for the autumn/winter season. Fixed points: market week in early October, lookbook needed for buyer appointments during it, retail delivery in August for the early drop. Decision maker: the founder-designer; her commercial director handles terms.
- Scope output: Good / Better (recommended) / Best tiers covering art direction days, lookbook design, line sheets, packaging suite artwork on existing structures; photography and casting excluded as client-managed; seasonal retainer option priced as an alternative in Best.
- Library: one full season for another label (named, imagery permitted, no sales outcome recorded for it), one packaging-only case for a fashion retailer (named, deliverables only).
- Vertical note: fashion layer applies. The document itself is a taste sample; restraint is the credential.

## Arc chosen

Seasonal arc: the season does not move. Beats: the calendar first (market week and delivery dates, working backwards), one season of past work shown large and mostly wordless, the deliverables by collection moment, tiers. The proposal is six pages. The document stays short on purpose: six pages, large work, little text.

## The proposal (shape)

**Page 1.** Cover: label name, season, studio, one image from the studio's past season. Nothing else.
**Page 2.** The season, working backwards: retail delivery August, lookbook print deadline three weeks before market week, shoot window, packaging artwork lock against the printer's summer schedule. One line naming the crunch everyone in fashion knows: everything lands in the same fortnight, so the plan front-loads decisions while the collection is still being finished.
**Page 3.** Understanding, in the founder's language from discovery: buyers judge the range in the lookbook before they touch a garment; the packaging suite is what the customer actually keeps. No claims about the studio yet.
**Page 4.** Proof: the past season, three spreads, captions limited to deliverable facts (lookbook, 32 pages; line sheets; packaging suite on existing structures). No outcome sentence exists for this case and none is implied; the imagery is the argument, which in this vertical is honest rather than weak.
**Page 5.** Approach and team: shoot-day art direction, the designer's role, the production hand who runs printers in August when half the trade is on holiday, named, with her past-season credit.
**Page 6.** Scope, investment, terms summary, next step. Tiers transcribed; Best carries the seasonal retainer alternative so the founder can choose a rhythm rather than a project. Photography and casting exclusions stated plainly because fashion clients routinely assume both are "part of the shoot". Next step: approve a tier before the designer's fabric deadline week, after which the shoot window narrows; the date is real, from the brief, not manufactured pressure.

## Critique

Strengths: the document performs the judgement it is selling. Calendar fluency (market week, delivery, the August printer problem) does the credibility work that outcome claims do in CPG, which matters because this library, like most fashion libraries, has almost no citable outcomes. The retainer inside Best plants the second season without a separate pitch.
Watch outs: imagery-led documents are where permission discipline quietly dies, because a beautiful spread begs a caption. Self-check 4 caught one caption in drafting that named a retailer the past client had not permitted for this use; the name came out, the image stayed. Also note the deliberate absence of any concept for Atelier Vost's own season: no moodboard of "where we might take it", however tempting with a founder-designer audience. The past season is the moodboard, and it is already paid for.
