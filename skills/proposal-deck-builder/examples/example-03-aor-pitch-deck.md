# Example 3: Agency of Record Pitch, Deck Outline

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative, taken from a fictional scope output and reproduced exactly.

## Inputs

- Discovery brief: Northfield Pantry (fictional), the granola bar brand whose 12 SKU redesign the Scope and Pricing Architect module scoped (its Example 1) and whose range guardianship retainer it priced (its Example 8). They want the retained relationship formalised as an AOR-style engagement. Decision group: VP brand, head of procurement (joins at terms), founder in the final meeting.
- Scope output: retainer at 5,350 per month (8.5 studio days across roles, itemised), overage at standard day rates, quarterly review with a usage trigger, 30 day exit on a quarter boundary, fees payable through the notice period.
- Library: one three-year guardianship case (named, outcome citable), one redesign case, one launch case carried by a designer who has since left (flagged in Step 2 of the workflow; not used as a team credential).
- Format: presented pitch to the decision group, forwarded afterwards to the founder. Tree 1: BOTH. Deck 18 pages, proposal document as leave-behind.

## Critique

Strengths: the argument is operational, not creative. AOR buyers are purchasing the absence of emergencies, so the deck sells pattern, governance, and named people, and the exit clause is presented as confidence rather than hidden as a risk. Every figure traces to the client's own supplied history or the scope output; the studio invented nothing about the client's costs, which the VP brand noticed, per the (fictional) outcome note: shortlisted, then won at the scope output's figures without a rate negotiation.
Watch outs: the founder receives the forwarded deck cold; pages 3 and 11 carry their meaning on-page for exactly that reason. The launch case by the departed designer was tempting proof of range breadth and was cut; a team page implying she would do the work would have failed self-check 8 and, worse, been found out in week one.
