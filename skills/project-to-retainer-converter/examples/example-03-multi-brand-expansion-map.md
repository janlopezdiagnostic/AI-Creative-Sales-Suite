# Example 3: Expansion Map for a Multi-Brand Client

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Minimum booking unit: half day. Rates are never discounted.
- Retainer terms for this example: monthly fee invoiced in advance, unused days do not roll over, quarterly review with a 70 percent usage trigger.

## Messy input (account lead notes)
"Northgate Foods is a group. We only did the project for Brand A, their chilled soup brand, Millbrook. The group also has Brand B, a pantry staples brand called Copperline, and Brand C, a frozen brand called Frost and Field, neither of which we have touched. Their group marketing director hinted at 'one arrangement across the group'. There is also a Canada launch discussed for Millbrook, meaning bilingual panels, a seasonal programme every year, and guidelines nobody owns, three years out of date. I do not want to pitch a giant group retainer we cannot staff, or price brands we have never seen the files for."

## Reasoning
1. The expansion map is not the retainer. The map shows the whole territory, brand by brand, so the client can see what exists. The retainer then covers one phased slice, sized from work the studio understands.
2. Only Brand A, Millbrook, was the project. The studio knows its design families, printer, copy approval route, and quarterly volume. Brands B and C are map entries, not scoped work: their files, volumes, and approval routes are unknown, and pricing them now would be guessing dressed as confidence.
3. Canada is real but unstarted. Bilingual panels are a defined job type once the client supplies approved English and French copy, but timing, SKU count, and regulatory copy ownership are unconfirmed, so Canada is a phased addition, quoted or folded in at a quarterly review, not buried in phase 1.
4. Seasonal work and guidelines guardianship are the strongest recurring threads. Seasonal versions repeat yearly, and guardianship, meaning the studio maintains the guidelines and checks artwork against them, only works if one party owns it.
5. Phasing protects both sides. Phase 1 covers Millbrook only, from its known forecast. Expansion is a standing item at each quarterly review, where usage, the 70 percent trigger, and the forecast decide the next block size with evidence in front of both parties.

## The maths (internal, shown here for training)
Phase 1 block, Millbrook only, sized from its known pattern of variants, copy updates, and seasonal preparation:

Monthly block, by role:
- Creative director: 0.5 day x 950 = 475
- Strategist: 1 day x 800 = 800
- Senior designer: 1.5 days x 750 = 1,125
- Designer: 2 days x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 2.5 days x 450 = 1,125
- Project manager: 1 day x 500 = 500
- Monthly total: 8.5 days; 475 + 800 + 1,125 + 1,100 + 1,125 + 500 = 5,125
- Quarterly total: 5,125 x 3 = 15,375

No phase 2 fee is stated. Sister brand and Canada volumes are unknown, so any number invented here would not recompute from evidence. Phase 2 is priced at the first quarterly review, from confirmed volumes, at the same rates.

## Output excerpt

### Expansion map (summary, as presented)
**Brand A, Millbrook (project completed):** variants, retailer versions, copy updates, seasonal programme, guidelines guardianship. Ready to scope now.
**Brand B, Copperline (sister brand, not yet worked on):** believed to need a guidelines audit first, then artwork support. Volume and file condition unknown. Audit quoted separately before any retainer coverage.
**Brand C, Frost and Field (sister brand, not yet worked on):** no current work identified. Map entry only, revisited at quarterly reviews.
**Second market, Canada (Millbrook):** bilingual English and French panels for the launch SKUs, using client supplied, client approved copy in both languages. SKU count and timing to be confirmed. Priced at the review once confirmed, or quoted as a separate project.
**Seasonal programme (group):** annual seasonal versions, Millbrook first, Copperline only after its audit. Produced within the existing systems, drawn from the block where covered.
**Guidelines guardianship (group ambition):** one maintained guidelines document per covered brand, held by the studio, with third party artwork checked against it and drift flagged in writing. Starts with Millbrook, extends brand by brand.

### Phased retainer recommendation (excerpt)
**Phase 1 retainer: 5,125 per month**, invoiced monthly in advance. A reserved block of 8.5 studio days per month (creative director 0.5, strategist 1, senior designer 1.5, designer 2, artworker 2.5, project manager 1), covering Millbrook only, on the standard retainer terms: rates unchanged, unused days do not roll over, overage quoted and approved in writing before it starts.
**Quarterly review:** usage, the 70 percent trigger, and the expansion map are standing items. If usage runs under 70 percent for two consecutive months, we will raise resizing ourselves. Expansion to Copperline, Canada, or a wider seasonal programme is decided at a review, priced from confirmed volumes at standard rates, and recorded in a written note signed by both parties.

## Critique
Strengths: the map is generous and the contract is narrow, which is the right way round. The studio commits only to the brand it can staff and price honestly, and each expansion becomes a review decision with usage evidence behind it.
Watch outs: group conversations drift. A Brand B request arriving informally "under the group arrangement" is the predictable failure mode, and the answer is routing, not refusal: the work is welcome, it is simply not covered until a review adds it, and an audit quote can be issued the same week. Second, if Canada accelerates mid quarter, quote it as a separate project at standard rates rather than cannibalise the Millbrook block and argue about overage.
