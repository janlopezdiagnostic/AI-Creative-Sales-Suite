# Example 4: Mixed Outcome, Addressed Honestly, Converted Smaller

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Minimum booking unit: half day. Rates are never discounted.
- Retainer terms for this example: monthly fee invoiced in advance, unused days do not roll over, quarterly review with a 70 percent usage trigger.

## Messy input (founder voice note, after the wash up meeting)
"Juniper Lane Snacks launched two weeks late, and that was us. Our artworker rebuilt a dieline wrong, two SKUs had to be reprinted, I owned it to their founder in writing, and we covered the reprint cost. The work is good, but I will not walk in with a glossy recap pretending none of that happened. They still need artwork help: constant retailer file versions and copy tweaks, and their in house person is drowning. They do not need our strategy, and they are not ready to hand us guardianship. Pitch what fits, at the real rates, or do not pitch at all."

## Reasoning
1. The recap addresses the failure first. The delay was the studio's artwork error, owned in writing when it happened, and the recap repeats that ownership. A recap leading with studio praise after a studio caused delay reads as dishonest.
2. The mixed results are stated as mixed. The range launched and the system works, and the launch was two weeks late for a reason that sits with the studio. No sales claims are made, because none were given with permission.
3. The retainer matches the trust that exists. The client has a recurring artwork workload and a stretched in house resource. They have no reason to buy strategy now, and guardianship, trusting the studio to judge other people's artwork, is precisely what the error damaged. So the offer is artwork operations only: file versions, copy updates, and print ready supply.
4. Smaller is not discounted. The block is smaller in scope and days, at the same standard rates. Trust is rebuilt by honesty and delivery, not by cutting prices.
5. The standard protections still apply: unused days do not roll over, overage is quoted and approved in writing, requests route through the project manager, and the quarterly review with the 70 percent trigger decides whether the block is ready to grow.

## The maths (internal, shown here for training)
Artwork operations block, sized from the client's workload, estimated at 5 to 7 small jobs a month. Roles not listed are not included: no creative director, no strategist, no senior designer.

Monthly block, by role:
- Designer: 1 day x 550 = 550
- Artworker: 4 days x 450 = 1,800
- Project manager: 1 day x 500 = 500
- Monthly total: 6 days; 550 + 1,800 + 500 = 2,850
- Quarterly total: 2,850 x 3 = 8,550

Every day above is at the full standard rate. The reduction is scope, not price: a genuine smaller offer, not a disguised discount.

## Output excerpt

### Results recap (opening, as sent)
First, the part that sits with us. This range launched two weeks late because of a studio artwork error: a dieline was rebuilt incorrectly and two SKUs had to be reprinted. We owned this in writing at the time, we covered the reprint cost, and we are not rewriting it here. The full range did launch, the system held across every SKU, and the range is now in store. Both things are true, in that order.

### Smaller retainer proposal (excerpt)
**Artwork operations retainer: 2,850 per month**, invoiced monthly in advance. A reserved block of 6 studio days per month (designer 1, artworker 4, project manager 1), covering Juniper Lane Snacks artwork operations only.
**In scope, drawn from the block:** retailer specific file versions of existing artwork, pack copy updates using your supplied approved copy, format adaptations of existing SKUs within the existing system, and print ready file supply.
**Out of scope, quoted separately:** new SKUs needing design beyond adaptation, any design family change, strategy, naming, and guardianship or checking of third party work. We are not proposing those now.
**How the block works:** requests route through our project manager, who confirms estimated days before work starts, with a second pair of eyes check on every dieline, a control added after the launch error. A usage note accompanies each invoice. Unused days do not roll over. Overage is quoted at standard rates, half day minimum, and starts only on your written approval.
**Quarterly review:** usage and output are reviewed each quarter. If usage runs under 70 percent for two consecutive months, we will raise resizing ourselves. Whether the scope should ever widen is a review question.

### Outcome (fictional, part of this example)
The client accepted the smaller retainer for two quarters. Their founder's reply, paraphrased in this fictional example, said the recap opening was why the conversation happened at all.

## Critique
Why pitching big would have failed: a guardianship pitch asks the client to trust the studio's judgement about artwork quality immediately after the studio's own artwork error cost them two weeks. The ask and the evidence point in opposite directions, and a glossy retelling would have made it worse.
Strengths of the smaller conversion: the scope fits an evidenced need, the block contains only the roles that need requires, and the dieline double check converts an apology into a visible control. It also creates the one thing that can later justify a wider scope: a clean, on time delivery record, reviewed quarterly.
Watch outs: artwork operations must not quietly grow strategy or guardianship inside the block, however flattering the requests. Widening happens at a review, in writing, or it does not happen.
