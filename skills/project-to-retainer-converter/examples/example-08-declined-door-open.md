# Example 8: Declined Retainer, Door Left Open

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio, USD, tax excluded. Standard rates, never discounted: creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day. Half day minimum; overage at standard rates, half day minimum, quoted and approved in writing first.

## Messy input (forwarded email and a note from the founder)
"Alder and Vine, the home fragrance brand we rebranded in spring, replied to the retainer proposal today. Their marketing director wrote: 'We love the work and the proposal makes sense, but we were acquired in June and all discretionary spend is frozen until the new owners finish their review. It is not a no to the idea, it is a cannot right now.' Founder's note: 'I believe her. The acquisition is public. I do not want to chase or vanish, and I do not want us pitching again in six months with no record of where we left it. Write the decline properly.'"

## Reasoning
1. Believe the stated reason when evidence supports it. The acquisition is public, a spend freeze is a normal consequence, and the reply named a mechanism, new owner review, not a vague brush off. Treating a real freeze as an objection to overcome would burn the relationship for a signature the client cannot give.
2. A decline for a real reason is not the end of a pipeline; it is a pipeline with a date problem. The work here is converting "cannot right now" into a specific, dated re entry trigger, so follow up happens because the client's situation changed, not because the studio got anxious.
3. The trigger is new fiscal year planning at the acquiring group, in [month, TO CONFIRM from the client], ahead of a new fiscal year. That is when a retainer line can enter a budget. Re entry is set a few weeks before that round, with the proposal refreshed against any range changes since.
4. Keep warm contact runs on the client's calendar, not the studio's sales rhythm: one short check in per quarter, tied to something real in their world, such as their seasonal launch, and only ever containing something useful: a relevant result, a requested file, an introduction. No newsletters disguised as check ins.
5. Record everything internally now, while it is fresh: the proposal as sent, the exact reason given, the named trigger, the contact's words on timing, and who else supported the idea.
6. Rates and terms, when the conversation reopens, are the proposal as sent: standard rates, never discounted for the wait, overage at standard rates, half day minimum, rollover marked [TO CONFIRM], quarterly review with a usage trigger, exit at a quarter end on written notice. The door stays open; the price does not drift open with it.

## The maths (the proposal as sent, kept on record)
- Creative director: 0.5 x 950 = 475
- Strategist: 0.5 x 800 = 400
- Senior designer: 1.5 x 750 = 1,125
- Designer: 2 x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 1 x 450 = 450
- Project manager: 0.5 x 500 = 250
- Monthly total: 6 days, 3,800
- Quarterly total: 3,800 x 3 = 11,400

## Output: the decline handling pack
**1. Short reply (sent the same day):**
"Thank you for telling us straight, and congratulations on the acquisition. We completely understand the freeze, and there is no need to keep the proposal warm at your end. We will hold our notes here. If it helps, when your new planning round comes up we can send a refreshed one page version, updated for whatever the range looks like by then, so you have something current for the budget conversation. Until then we are here if anything urgent comes up, on the usual basis."

**2. Dated re entry trigger (internal):**
Re entry point: four weeks before the client's new fiscal year planning round, [planning month and year TO CONFIRM from the client]. Action: send the refreshed one page proposal, restate the block at standard rates, note any range or SKU changes, and offer a 30 minute call. If the freeze is still in place, set the next trigger at the following planning round and return to quarterly check ins.

**3. Light touch keep warm plan:**
One contact per quarter, maximum, initiated only with something of use. Quarter one: send the final rebrand results summary they requested for the new owners, with citation permissions as recorded. Quarter two: note their seasonal launch, briefly, no pitch attached. Quarter three: the refreshed proposal at the re entry trigger.

**4. Internal record (filed with the proposal):**
Client and contact; proposal date, block, and fee (6 days, 3,800 per month, standard rates); reason for decline in the client's own words ("all discretionary spend is frozen until the new owners finish their review"); internal champion and role; re entry trigger and date; keep warm actions, with dates; permissions held for results and quotes; status: Dormant, not Closed lost.

## Critique
Strengths: the reply costs the client nothing and asks for nothing, which is why it will be remembered when the freeze lifts. Re entry is tied to the client's budget mechanism rather than a generic "check in next quarter", so the studio returns as help with a deadline the client actually has. The internal record means the thread survives staff changes on either side.
Watch outs: the keep warm plan fails by enthusiasm. One extra message, a greeting with a proposal attached, or a "quick idea" that is really a pitch, and the discipline that made the reply credible is gone. Second: when the conversation reopens, do not reward the wait or punish it. The proposal returns at the same standard rates and structure, refreshed only for real changes in the range.
