# Example 1: Packaging Guardianship Conversion, the Full Run

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Minimum booking unit: half day. Rates are never discounted.
- Retainer terms: monthly fee invoiced in advance, unused days do not roll over, quarterly review with a 70 percent usage trigger.

## Messy input (founder notes after the final project meeting)
"Harbor and Vine Soup Co. redesign is done, 18 SKUs, all on shelf. Their operations director, Priya Nair, said in writing we can cite the distribution gain: listings in 2 additional retailers. She gave no permission for any sales figure, so sales are not to be mentioned. Next year they expect 4 to 6 limited editions, retailer specific sizes, pack copy changes, and constant small artwork updates. She asked, 'Can you just look after the range now?' Do the full conversion properly: recap first, expansion map second, then a retainer sized from their forecast."

## Reasoning
1. The recap leads, because the pitch has no weight without evidence. The only results claim available with permission is the distribution gain, and it is strong on its own: listings in 2 additional retailers means buyers gave the redesigned range shelf space.
2. No sales figure appears anywhere. Informal mention is not permission and is not a sourced figure, and an unpermitted claim would risk the relationship the retainer depends on. Inside this fictional example, all results are part of the fiction.
3. The expansion map turns "look after the range" into countable work: limited editions within the existing design families, retailer specific sizes, pack copy updates from client supplied approved copy, seasonal sleeves, artwork checks on third party files, and one range review call per month.
4. The block is sized bottom up from that forecast. Artwork and design carry most of the load, strategy is a small allocation so range decisions are priced rather than absorbed, and project management is explicit.
5. The terms follow the retainer template: rates unchanged, unused days do not roll over, overage quoted and approved in writing before it starts, and a quarterly review where either side can propose resizing. If usage runs under 70 percent for two consecutive months, the studio raises resizing itself.

## The maths (internal, shown here for training)
Forecast per month: 1 limited edition or variant in progress, 2 to 3 artwork updates, 1 copy change round, plus guardianship checks.

Monthly block, by role:
- Creative director: 1 day x 950 = 950
- Strategist: 0.5 day x 800 = 400
- Senior designer: 2 days x 750 = 1,500
- Designer: 2 days x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 3 days x 450 = 1,350
- Project manager: 1 day x 500 = 500
- Monthly total: 9.5 days; 950 + 400 + 1,500 + 1,100 + 1,350 + 500 = 5,800
- Quarterly total: 5,800 x 3 = 17,400

## Output excerpt

### Results recap (summary sent to the client)
The 18 SKU Harbor and Vine redesign is complete and on shelf. Following the redesign, the range secured listings in 2 additional retailers. Source: client operations director, written permission to cite the distribution gain given. No sales figures are cited, because permission was not given.

### Expansion map (summary)
Year one, inside the existing system: 4 to 6 limited editions, retailer specific sizes, ingredient and pack copy updates, seasonal sleeves, and guardianship checks on new and third party artwork. Other brands, a new design family, or a redesign sit outside this map and would be quoted separately.

### Retainer pitch (excerpt)
**Range guardianship retainer: 5,800 per month**, invoiced monthly in advance. A reserved block of 9.5 studio days per month (creative director 1, strategist 0.5, senior designer 2, designer 2, artworker 3, project manager 1), booked against the Harbor and Vine range.
**In scope, drawn from the block:** limited editions and variants within the existing design families, retailer specific artwork, pack copy updates using your supplied approved copy, seasonal sleeves, artwork checks on third party files with drift flagged in writing, one range review call per month, and print ready file supply.
**Out of scope, quoted separately:** a new design family or redesign, naming and strategy programmes, photography, illustration, copywriting, native source files, print costs, and other brands.
**How the block works:** requests come through your named lead to our project manager, who confirms estimated days before work starts. A usage note accompanies each invoice. Unused days do not roll over; the fee reserves capacity we cannot sell elsewhere. Work beyond the block is quoted at the standard day rates, half day minimum, and starts only on your written approval.
**Quarterly review:** at each quarter end we review usage, output, and the forecast together, and either side may propose resizing. If usage runs under 70 percent for two consecutive months, we will raise resizing ourselves.

### Signed outcome (fictional, part of this example)
The client signed for an initial two quarters, with the 30 day quarter end exit from the template, the in and out lists unchanged, and the first review date booked at signing.

## Critique
Strengths: the sequence is right, evidence first, map second, price third, so the fee reads as the cost of a defined workload, not a loyalty charge. The permission discipline is the quiet strength: one clean, permitted, specific claim persuades without needing a sales figure, and sizing from the forecast gives the review something real to test against.
Watch outs: if the limited edition programme slips, usage will fall and the 70 percent trigger will fire. The studio must then actually propose a smaller block, rather than quietly keep the larger one. That moment, handled honestly, is what makes the second year signable.
