# Example 7: "We Will Just Call You" Rescue

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio, USD, tax excluded. Standard rates, never discounted: creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day. Half day minimum; overage at standard rates, half day minimum, quoted and approved in writing first.

## Messy input (studio diary summary, three months on)
"Copperline Coffee Roasters finished their packaging project in February. We proposed a small retainer. Their operations manager said no thanks: 'We will just call you when we need you, it will not be that often.' Since then: March, a rush label fix on a Friday because a retailer found a weight error. April, four requests in one week, two marked urgent. May, a new blend launch pack done in pieces, three separate invoices, and a complaint that nothing matches the launch timeline they never shared. Nobody kept the work away; we just never wrote it down as one picture. Now the founder wants to reopen the retainer conversation, without it sounding like a lecture."

## Reasoning
1. Do not argue from the studio's prediction. Argue from the client's own log. As one table, the log makes the case without a single claim about what the client should have done.
2. Present the log neutrally: date, what was asked, who asked, days used, fee, notice given. Let the pattern show itself. The moment the studio adds commentary like "as warned", the client stops reading numbers and starts defending a decision.
3. The rescue offer is deliberately small and short: a starter block for one quarter only. One quarter proves routing, response, and the usage note, and saying yes costs little courage.
4. The block is sized from the log, not hope. The log shows 7 days a month on average, spiky and badly routed. The starter block sits slightly below that, at 6 days, because some logged time was waste from rush and fragmentation, which the queue should reduce. If demand is higher, overage at standard rates, half day minimum, handles it in the open.
5. The rate does not move, in either direction. No penalty pricing for having declined, no discount for returning. The same standard rates priced the ad hoc work and price the block. What changes is order: a named route, estimates before work, capacity reserved, one invoice, a quarterly review with a usage trigger, exit at the quarter end on written notice. Rollover is marked [TO CONFIRM].
6. Tone is a deliverable. The covering message thanks them for the work, shows the log as housekeeping, never as an accusation: the client did nothing wrong by calling; the studio did something wrong by never offering a structure.

## The maths
Request log, March to May:
- Creative director: 3 x 950 = 2,850
- Senior designer: 3 x 750 = 2,250
- Designer: 8 x 550 = 4,400
- Artworker: 5 x 450 = 2,250
- Project manager: 2 x 500 = 1,000
- Three month total: 21 days, 12,750
- Monthly average: 12,750 / 3 = 4,250, on 7 days a month, across 11 requests and 9 invoices

Proposed starter block, one quarter:
- Creative director: 0.5 x 950 = 475
- Strategist: 0.5 x 800 = 400
- Senior designer: 1 x 750 = 750
- Designer: 2 x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 1.5 x 450 = 675
- Project manager: 0.5 x 500 = 250
- Monthly total: 6 days, 3,650
- One quarter total: 3,650 x 3 = 10,950
The block fee is below the recent average because the block is smaller than the spiky average, not because any rate changed. Demand above the block appears as pre approved overage at the same rates.

## Output excerpt (covering message and proposal summary)
"Thanks for all of the work since February. While closing our files for the quarter we put the requests since March into one list, attached. It added up to more than either of us expected: 21 days, 11 requests, 12,750 in fees, and much of it at short notice.
If it would help, we can run next quarter differently. A starter block of 6 days a month, 3,650 per month for one quarter: requests through one route, an estimate before anything starts, capacity held for you, one invoice with a usage note. Same day rates as the work you have already paid for, nothing repriced. At the end of the quarter we review usage together and you decide whether to continue, resize, or stop, with exit at the quarter end on written notice as set out in the contractual scope."
Proposal notes attached: in scope (label and pack updates within the existing design families, retailer versions, launch pack adaptations, one planning call per month); out of scope, quoted separately (new blends needing design work beyond adaptation, photography, copywriting, new design families); overage at standard rates, half day minimum, quoted and approved in writing first; rollover [TO CONFIRM]; quarterly review with the trigger that if usage runs under 70 percent of the block for two consecutive months, resizing is raised at the review. Contractual version: the retainer scope from the Scope and Pricing Architect module. Drafting aid, have counsel review before use.

## Critique
Strengths: the evidence is unimpeachable because it is the client's own history, invoiced and paid. Nobody has to concede an argument to accept it, which is why it can be accepted.
Watch outs: the log must be accurate to the day and the dollar before it is sent. One wrong date and the meeting becomes about the error. Check it against invoices twice. Second, the most common failure here: any hint of "we told you so", in the meeting or in a joke afterwards. The client declined in good faith with a forecast that turned out low. Studios that punish a client for returning do not get a second return.
