# Example 5: Fashion Seasonal Retainer

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet)
Hartwell Studio, USD, tax excluded. Standard rates, never discounted: creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day. Half day minimum; overage at standard rates, half day minimum, quoted and approved in writing first.

## Messy input (notes from the account lead)
"Maren Vale, the womenswear label, loved the spring summer lookbook and campaign packaging we did: hangtags, swing tickets, gift boxes, tissue. Their founder says every season is the same scramble: design starts late, artwork is rushed, the lookbook slips, and their small in house team chases printers at midnight. She asked for 'a retainer, but not the same every month, because fashion is not the same every month'. Two main seasons, spring summer and autumn winter, plus a holiday capsule in November. Can we build something that breathes with that calendar? The sell through review for the launch season is in three weeks, and she wants to talk then."

## Reasoning
1. A flat block would fail twice. In quiet months she would pay for capacity she cannot use, and in the two months before each drop the block would be exhausted in the first fortnight, pushing the most important work into overage arguments.
2. The solution is two blocks, agreed in advance: a base block every month, and a larger peak block only in the pre drop months. Both use the same standard rates. What changes by season is days, never price per day.
3. The base block covers work that never stops: replenishment packaging tweaks, crops of existing imagery, retailer requests, label and hangtag updates from client supplied approved copy, forward planning.
4. The peak block covers the surge: lookbook layout, new season packaging artwork, campaign adaptations, printer ready files. New photography, a new identity, and new packaging structures are out of scope, quoted separately. A bigger block is not a wider scope.
5. Timing matters as much as structure. The pitch is set just after the sell through review of the launch season, when the client has their own numbers in front of them: what sold, what reordered, what the scramble cost. Pitching earlier would argue from studio enthusiasm instead of client evidence.
6. Rollover, review, and exit follow the standard model. Rollover choice is marked [TO CONFIRM]. There is a quarterly review with a usage trigger, and exit falls at a quarter end on written notice, so neither side can end mid drop.

## The maths
Base block, every month:
- Creative director: 0.5 x 950 = 475
- Strategist: 0.5 x 800 = 400
- Senior designer: 1 x 750 = 750
- Designer: 2 x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 1 x 450 = 450
- Project manager: 0.5 x 500 = 250
- Base total: 5.5 days, 3,425 per month

Peak block, in each of the two months before a drop:
- Creative director: 1 x 950 = 950
- Strategist: 1 x 800 = 800
- Senior designer: 2 x 750 = 1,500
- Designer: 3 x 550 = 1,650
- Artworker: 2 x 450 = 900
- Project manager: 1 x 500 = 500
- Peak total: 10 days, 6,300 per month

Year view: three drops, two peak months each, so six peak months and six base months: 6 x 3,425 = 20,550, plus 6 x 6,300 = 37,800, annual total 58,350.

## Output excerpt (proposal)
**Seasonal retainer for Maren Vale: 3,425 per month in base months, 6,300 per month in peak months**, invoiced monthly in advance. Peak months are the two months before each agreed drop date: spring summer, autumn winter, and the holiday capsule. The drop calendar is attached and forms part of this proposal; if a drop date moves, peak months move with it by written agreement.
**In the base block:** replenishment packaging artwork, retailer versions, crops of existing imagery, label and hangtag updates from your supplied approved copy, one planning call per month. **In the peak block, additionally:** lookbook layout from the agreed page plan, new season packaging artwork within the existing system, campaign adaptations, printer ready files.
**Out of scope, quoted separately:** photography, new packaging structures, a new identity or campaign concept, copywriting, print costs.
**How the block works:** requests come through your named lead to our project manager, who confirms estimated days before work starts. A usage note accompanies each invoice. Overage is at standard rates, half day minimum, quoted and approved in writing first. Rollover: [TO CONFIRM, record the chosen rule in the contractual scope, which is the retainer scope from the Scope and Pricing Architect module].
**Quarterly review and exit:** each quarter we review usage and the coming season, and either side may propose resizing a block. If usage runs under 70 percent of the block for two consecutive months, we will raise resizing at the review ourselves. Either party may end the retainer at the end of a quarter by giving written notice as set out in the contractual scope. Drafting aid, have counsel review before use.

## Critique
Strengths: the seasonal shape is the whole pitch. The client said fashion is not the same every month, and the proposal agrees in structure, not words only, while holding the rate line: days flex, prices do not.
Watch outs: the drop calendar is now a commercial document. If the client moves a drop by six weeks without telling the studio, peak capacity lands in the wrong months, so peak months are tied to agreed drop dates and move only by written agreement. Second: the holiday capsule will try to borrow full season treatment. Its cap on lookbook pages and packaging items must be in the contractual scope, or a capsule quietly becomes a third main season.
