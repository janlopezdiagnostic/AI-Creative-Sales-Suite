# Example 2: Budget-Frozen Deal Revived on the Next Cycle

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** Kestrel Home Goods (fictional) approved a packaging refresh in principle last spring. In June, the brand manager wrote: "Finance has frozen all non-essential spend until the new fiscal year. Our planning opens in January. Talk then, please, we do want to do this." The studio logged it, sent nothing in between, and the tracker review date is now 20 December.

## Raw input (as received)

> "Kestrel window is coming up. January planning, like they said. Can you draft the return? Original scope was the full 18-SKU refresh at the fee in the proposal. They hinted the budget will be smaller than last year's number."

## Reasoning

- Tree 4: CLEAR. The client invited the return and named its timing; this is consent to exactly one well-timed conversation, not a general licence.
- Diagnosis: budget frozen, textbook. The unblock condition was the cycle turning, and the tracker carried the date from the day the deal stalled, which is why nobody had to reconstruct the history in December.
- Play: Template B, budget-cycle return. Arrive just before planning opens, reference their words and their date, and answer the budget hint honestly: re-cut the scope (core 10 SKUs plus the system, remaining SKUs as a priced phase two) rather than discounting the same scope. Cutting scope to fit a real budget is legitimate; cutting the rate on identical work teaches the market your prices are fiction.

## Final output (draft, for the user's confirmation)

> Subject: Kestrel refresh: picking up for January planning
>
> [Name], you told me in June that the refresh was frozen until the new fiscal year and to talk in January, so here it is.
>
> I have re-cut the original proposal to the shape we discussed: the full system and the core 10 SKUs first, with the remaining 8 as a priced second phase when the range review lands. The system work is untouched, because that is the part everything else hangs from. The original full scope still exists if the budget picture has improved instead.
>
> Worth 20 minutes before planning locks to check the shape? I can do any morning this week or next.

## Critique

What this example does right: the seven silent months were the strategy, not neglect, and the email says so implicitly by arriving exactly when promised. What to watch: the phase split must be real (the 10 SKUs must be a coherent shelf set), and the phase-two price must be the studio's standard rate from its own rate sheet, stated in the proposal that follows, not improvised in this email.
