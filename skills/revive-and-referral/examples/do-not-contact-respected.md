# Example 7: A Do-Not-Contact, Respected (Nothing Is Sent)

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** A large account, Northgate Provisions (fictional), stalled 14 months ago after a promising proposal. The account has just announced a major range overhaul (dated primary announcement, supplied), which on paper is the strongest revival trigger of the quarter. A new account executive, working the tracker for the first time, asks the skill to draft the revival touch.

## Raw input (as received)

> "Northgate is doing the exact overhaul we proposed. This is our moment. Draft the trigger touch, use the teardown angle, keep it short."

## Reasoning

- Tree 4 runs before any drafting. The CRM history the user supplies alongside the request contains, in the notes of the final exchange, the client's words from 14 months ago: "Please take us off your list. We will reach out if that changes." The marketing contact had also unsubscribed from the studio's mailing list in the same month.
- Permission: NONE. Permanent. The trigger's strength is irrelevant; Tree 4's exit is evaluated before Tree 2 is even opened. The overhaul announcement changes the client's situation, not their instruction.
- Correct output is a refusal plus record hygiene, not a draft with caveats. The tracker row is corrected from "parked, watch for triggers" to DO NOT CONTACT with the client's words and their date recorded as the source, which is the step that prevents a different colleague from making the same near-miss next year.

## Final output (complete)

> I cannot draft that touch. The record for Northgate Provisions contains an explicit instruction from the client, dated [date in the record]: "Please take us off your list. We will reach out if that changes." They also unsubscribed in the same month. Under this skill's rules that is a permanent opt-out: no revival email, no breakup email, no exception for a strong trigger.
>
> What I have done instead: updated the tracker row to DO NOT CONTACT, with their words and the date recorded, and removed the account from the trigger watch so it does not surface again. If they ever do reach out, that inbound contact restarts everything legitimately, and the research brief can be refreshed then.

## Critique

This example exists because the failure mode it prevents is quiet and common: the opt-out lived in a note nobody re-read, while the trigger was exciting enough to carry the room. The skill's value in this session was not copywriting; it was reading the record in the right order. Note the related discipline: the refusal does not suggest working around the opt-out via a colleague at Northgate. A side door to the same account, aimed at the same conversation, would violate the spirit of the instruction and, in a small industry, would be recognised as such.
