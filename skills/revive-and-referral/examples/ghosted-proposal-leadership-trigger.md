# Example 1: Ghosted Proposal Revived by a Leadership-Change Trigger

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio proposed a range redesign to Bramble & Vine Preserves (fictional) seven months ago. Two good meetings, a full proposal, then silence. One value-add touch three months ago went unanswered. The deal sits in the tracker as no-decision, permission CLEAR.

## Raw input (as received)

> "Bramble & Vine again. Just saw a trade piece (Packaging Monthly, dated 12 March, fictional) saying they have a new head of brand, started this month, ex-CPG. Our champion there was the marketing manager who went quiet. Should I write to the new person? Tracker and the trade snippet attached."

## Reasoning

- Tree 4 first: no opt-out, no stated no, no conflict in the record. Permission CLEAR.
- Diagnosis: the original stall reads no-decision, but the new fact changes the primary cause to champion gone in effect: the person who owned the conversation has been superseded by a new head of brand with her own mandate.
- Trigger: leadership change, confirmed by a dated trade source, inside the early window (the first 90 days, strongest roughly days 14 to 90, rule of thumb). Tree 2: ACT, because it directly changes the blocking condition: there is now a decision maker whose job is to review exactly this kind of open question.
- Play: Template C, new-champion introduction. Not a re-pitch. The proposal goes as an attachment for context, the ask is deliberately minimal, and the breakup option is not used because the counterparty has changed; a breakup email to a person who never received the original thread would be theatre.

## Final output (draft, for the user's confirmation)

> Subject: Background on the Bramble & Vine range redesign
>
> Hello [Name], congratulations on the role. Brief background so you do not have to dig: last autumn we worked through a proposed redesign of the preserves range with your marketing team. It paused without a decision either way, and the summary is attached if it is useful context.
>
> If the range is on your list for this year, I am glad to walk through where it stood whenever suits. If it is not, the summary is yours to file and there will be no chase from me.

Tracker update prepared alongside: stall cause updated to champion gone (superseded), trigger logged with source and date, owner and next review date set 30 days out.

## Critique

What this example does right: it treats the leadership change as a new conversation, not a louder repetition of the old one, and it never blames the silence. What to watch: the draft says "no decision either way" because the record supports that; if the record had contained an actual rejection, Tree 4 would have parked this account and the same trade article would not have justified contact.
