# Example 3: Lost to Incumbent, Played as a Long Game

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** A studio lost the Alderbrook Dairy (fictional) range redesign to the incumbent nine months ago. The client communicated the decision kindly, citing the incumbent's knowledge of their print supply chain. Since then: a gracious close was sent, the deal was parked with roster-review and leadership triggers on watch, and one teardown touch was sent at month five (a genuine observation about their seasonal range crowding), which got a warm two-line reply and no work.

## Raw input (as received)

> "Alderbrook again. Retailer trade note (Grocery Week, dated 2 February, fictional) says they are launching a plant-based line in autumn, new format, pouches, which Studio North (the incumbent) has never shown in their portfolio that I know. Our strongest category too. Is this the moment?"

## Reasoning

- Tree 4: CLEAR. A communicated loss to an incumbent is a closure of that decision, not of the account, and no negative permission signals exist.
- Trigger check: the new format is confirmed and dated, and crucially it is work outside the incumbent's demonstrated scope, which is exactly Tree 1's narrow-scope exception for lost-to-incumbent deals. The unverified part ("Studio North has never done pouches") is inference about a third party's portfolio: usable as a reason to write, never as a claim to make to the client.
- Play: Template D, narrow-scope return. The email does not reopen the redesign decision, does not suggest the incumbent cannot do the work, and offers a useful artefact before any proposal. The proof claim in it (three ranges shipped in the format) comes from the studio's case study library, which the user supplied with the account history; it is stated only because those three entries are complete and citable. One touch only; if it does not land, the account returns to the quarterly rhythm.

## Final output (draft, for the user's confirmation)

> Subject: The plant-based pouch line
>
> [Name], when the redesign went ahead last year we were sorry to miss it and glad to see it land well. This is about something adjacent rather than that decision: the announcement that the range moves into pouches this autumn.
>
> Pouch formats are the centre of our recent work: the structure, the reseal, and the shelf behaviour are a different problem from pots, and we have shipped three ranges in the format in the last two years. If it would help, I can send two pages on what we learned about standout in the chilled fixture, with no proposal attached.

## Critique

What this example does right: nine months of discipline (one gracious close, one useful touch, no campaign) bought the credibility this email spends, and it spends it on a narrow, provable claim. What to watch: the "three ranges shipped" line must match the fact sheet exactly; inflating proof in a long-game touch, where the whole asset is trust, is the costliest fabrication in the suite.
