# Example 8: A Referral That Became a Pipeline Entry, With the CRM Log

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** Following the introduction ask in Example 6, the Marlow Botanics founder (fictional) made the introduction to Halden & Grey (fictional) four days later. This example shows the follow-through: the same-day reply, the report back, and the records created, because the referral engine is judged on this half as much as on the ask.

## Raw input (as received)

> "She did it, intro email landed this morning, Halden's founder replied 'happy to talk, next week?' already. What do I do now, and what needs logging? I do not want to mess up the follow-through."

## Reasoning

- Speed and shape of the reply: same day, warm, no pitch in the first message, one low-friction scheduling step. The giver is copied only as the thread already includes her; no separate performance for her benefit.
- Pipeline discipline: the introduction is not a deal. The CRM entry is created as referral-sourced, at the earliest stage, with the giver named, and the next action is a research brief (Skill 1) before any proposal thinking, because the studio knows the account only through dated trade coverage and the giver's framing.
- The giver loop: she gets a brief update once the meeting is booked, and a final one when there is an outcome, whichever way it goes (edge case 4 discipline). The referral ask log records the full chain so the studio can see, months later, exactly what this referral produced.

## Final outputs

**Reply to the introduction thread (draft, sent same day):**

> [Name], thank you for the introduction. [Halden founder], glad to meet you. Next week works well on my side: I will send two or three times today and we can take it from there. Looking forward to hearing about the listing and what it means for the range.

**Message to the giver, once the meeting is booked (draft):**

> Quick update so you are not left wondering: we are talking next week. Thank you again for putting your name to it. I will let you know how it lands.

**CRM log (as written, on the user's confirmation):**

> Deal created: Halden & Grey (fictional), stage: first conversation, source: referral introduction. Giver: [Name], Founder, Marlow Botanics (client, phase two in progress). Path: testimonial granted [date]; introduction asked [date] on repeat-commission evidence; introduction made [date]; first reply same day; meeting booked [date]. Next step: Account Research Brief before the meeting. Referral log updated; giver outcome report sent [date]; final outcome report owed on result.

**Tracker update:** referral table row completed for the ask and outcome; Marlow account flagged as asked-out until a new satisfaction peak (next expected: phase-two launch).

## Critique

What this example does right: every promise the ask created was discharged in order (response, booking, report, record), and the referred prospect was treated as a research task rather than a prize. What to watch: the temptation at exactly this moment is to reciprocate with something large or to ask the giver for a second introduction while goodwill is warm. Both are refused by the method: gratitude is expressed, the outcome is reported, and the account rests until a new peak.
