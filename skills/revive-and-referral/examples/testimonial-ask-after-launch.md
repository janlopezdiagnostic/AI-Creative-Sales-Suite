# Example 5: Testimonial Ask After a Launch

All brands, people, and events in this example are fictional. Facts are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** A studio redesigned the range for Marlow Botanics (fictional), a skincare brand. The range launched into a national retailer two weeks ago. In an email last Tuesday (supplied by the user), the founder wrote: "Seeing it on shelf for the first time was the best moment of my year. Your team made a complicated range feel simple, and the retailer buyer said it is the clearest hierarchy in the category." No asks have ever been made to this client.

## Raw input (as received)

> "Marlow launched and the founder is over the moon, see her email below. Time for a testimonial ask? Draft it if so. Do not overdo it."

## Reasoning

- Evidence check: the satisfaction is in the client's own words, dated, and attached to a live peak (launch plus a third-party compliment she chose to relay). Strongest available evidence class.
- Tree 3: no prior asks, no open issues, peak live now. Exit: ASK NOW.
- Ask selection: the ladder offers review, testimonial, case study, introduction. The evidence supports a testimonial today. A case study involving retailer results would need separate outcome permissions, and an introduction request now would spend the peak twice. One ask: testimonial, drafted strictly from her words ("made a complicated range feel simple", "clearest hierarchy in the category" is the buyer's phrase as she relayed it, so the draft attributes carefully or leaves it out; the draft below uses only her own direct words about the studio).

## Final output (draft, for the user's confirmation)

> Subject: A favour, only if it is easy
>
> [Name], seeing the range on shelf was a moment here too. You said in your note last week that the team "made a complicated range feel simple", which is exactly what we hope a client says at the end of a project like this.
>
> Would you be comfortable with us quoting you on it? Draft, using your words: "They made a complicated range feel simple." Attribution would be [Name], Founder, Marlow Botanics, on our website and in proposals. Edit it however you like, or say not this time and I will not raise it again.

Log entry prepared: evidence (her email, dated), ask (testimonial), attribution elements requested itemised, outcome to follow.

## Critique

What this example does right: the ask arrived inside the peak, asked for one thing, and made declining costless in a way the follow-up behaviour will honour. What to watch: the buyer compliment is tempting copy, but it is second-hand and belongs to a third party; the draft correctly leaves it out. If she volunteers it in her reply, it still needs care, not automatic publication.
