# Template: Revival Email Set

Four touches, one per situation. Pick by stall cause (Tree 1), never send more than one per trigger. Every bracketed field must be filled from the deal record or a sourced trigger: an unfilled or invented field means the email is not ready. All drafts require the user's confirmation before sending.

---

## A. The trigger touch (no-decision or timing stall, trigger has fired)

**Subject:** [Specific noun from the trigger, e.g. "Your spring range extension"]

> [Name], when we last spoke in [month of last real exchange, from the record], [the project / the range redesign] was paused because [blocking condition, in neutral words].
>
> [Trigger, one sentence, with its source and date in plain language: "Your announcement last week that [event]..."] changes that picture, so I wanted to share one thing that may be useful: [value-add item in two or three lines: the observation, the teardown finding, or the permitted case result].
>
> If [the work] is moving again, I am glad to pick up from where we left it: [lowest-friction next step, e.g. "a 20-minute call, and I will bring the original scope updated for what has changed"]. If the timing is still wrong, tell me when to aim for and I will aim for it.

Rules: no reference to the silence, no "just checking in", the trigger is the reason for writing and appears in the first two lines.

---

## B. The budget-cycle return (budget frozen, their window has arrived)

**Subject:** [Project name]: picking up for [cycle they named]

> [Name], you told me in [month, from the record] that [the project] was frozen until [the cycle they named], and to talk [now / this month]. I believed you, so here it is.
>
> I have re-cut the original scope to the budget reality we discussed: [what changed in the scope, in one or two lines, no invented figures]. The full original version still exists if the picture has improved.
>
> Worth a short call to check the shape before your planning locks? [Specific low-friction offer.]

Rules: use their words for the reason and the date. If they never named a date, this template does not apply; use Template A on a genuine trigger, or stay parked.

---

## C. The new-champion introduction (champion gone, successor named)

**Subject:** Background on [project name], for your first weeks

> [Name], congratulations on the role. Brief background so you do not have to dig: [predecessor's name or "your predecessor"] and I worked through [scope summary] for [project] last [season/year]. It paused when [neutral statement of the change: "the role changed hands"], with no decision taken either way.
>
> Attached is the one-page summary of where it stood. No ask attached to it; if [the work] is on your list, I am glad to walk through it whenever suits. If it is not, the summary is yours to file and there will be no chase from me.

Rules: never criticise the stall, the predecessor, or the handover. Source and date for the successor's appointment must exist in the record before this is drafted (Tree 1).

---

## D. The narrow-scope return (lost to incumbent, uncovered work appears)

**Subject:** [The uncovered scope, named specifically]

> [Name], when [project] went ahead last [season], we were sorry to miss it and glad to see it land well. I am writing about something adjacent rather than that decision: [trigger, sourced and dated: e.g. "the announcement that the range enters (retailer/format) in (month)"].
>
> That work sits outside what [incumbent arrangement, described neutrally] typically covers, and it is the centre of what we do: [one named proof point from the fact sheet]. If it would help, I can share [specific useful artefact, e.g. "two pages on how we handled the same format for (permitted client example)"] with no proposal attached.

Rules: zero criticism of the incumbent, zero attempt to reopen the original decision. If permission to cite the proof client is not on record, describe the work type without naming them.
