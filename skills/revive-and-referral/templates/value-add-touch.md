# Template: Value-Add Touch

The wrapper for the playbook's touch library (teardown, trend note, permitted case result, introduction or resource). The value item is the email; this structure only frames it. If the value item would not be useful to the recipient with the deal permanently dead, do not send the touch.

**Subject:** [The useful thing, named plainly: "Two notes on your spring range" / "Label deadline worth knowing"]

> [Name], [one line connecting to the record: the project you discussed, or the trigger that prompted this, sourced and dated where it is news].
>
> [THE VALUE ITEM, in full. For a teardown: two or three specific observations about public material, one implication, no disparagement of whoever produced it. For a trend note: the movement, the source and date, and the one-line relevance to their range. For a case result: the approved wording only. For a resource or introduction: what it is and the one-line reason it fits them specifically.]
>
> No response needed to this. If [the underlying work] becomes live again on your side, you know where we are: [lowest-friction next step].

## Drafting notes (delete before sending)

- Under 200 words in total. If the value item needs more room, attach it or link it and keep the email to the summary.
- One value item per touch. Bundling three favours into one email reads as a campaign, not a gesture.
- "No response needed" must be true. If the sender plans to chase a reply, the line is dishonest and the whole touch is reclassified as a nudge.
- Permission check for any cited result: the satisfaction log must show the cited client approved external use of that result, in that wording. Otherwise describe the work type without numbers or names.
- Log the touch in the tracker before sending: date, trigger, value item used. Unlogged touches are how cadence limits get breached.
