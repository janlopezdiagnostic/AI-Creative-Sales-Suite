# Example 6: Packaging Sustainability and Material Change (Strong)

All brands in this example are fictional. The facts below are invented for training, including the pledge and the dates.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio asks for a brief on Casa Verde Foods (fictional), a chilled ready-meals and soups brand selling through two national grocery retailers.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Casa Verde - didn't they say something about getting rid of plastic trays? Also new regs coming on packaging in their markets I think?? Our studio: ready-meal packaging redesign last year (full range, tray to paperboard for a deli brand), strong structural partners. Check it out."

Plus pasted material: the brand's sustainability report dated 2026-03-15 (fictional) committing to move its chilled range from plastic trays to fibre-based trays by the end of 2027, with a named programme lead; a trade article dated 2026-09-08 (fictional) reporting that one of its two retailers will require recyclability scoring for chilled own-brand and branded suppliers from its spring 2027 reset; the brand's current range page showing plastic tray formats across 18 SKUs.

## Reasoning

- Intake: origin is the seller's half-remembered news. No relationship. Conflict check: clear; the studio's deli tray-to-paperboard project is in an adjacent category and the client is not a competitor.
- Sweep, packaging layer first: this is a material change account, so the three layers from the playbook get checked separately. Brand layer: a dated pledge with a deadline (end of 2027), a named material direction (fibre-based trays), and a named programme lead. That is the strong version of signal 7, not marketing language. Retailer layer: a recyclability scoring requirement tied to a specific reset (spring 2027), reported in trade press. Regulatory layer: the user "thought" regulations were coming; nothing specific to this brand's markets and formats could be sourced in the session, so regulation is recorded as UNVERIFIED and excluded from the hypothesis. The brief does not need it; two layers are enough.
- Why this buys design, not just engineering: a tray material change alters print surfaces, colour reproduction, barrier and sealing, shelf standout, and photography across 18 SKUs. Every SKU gets redesigned or at minimum re-artworked, and the range must hold together through a phased transition where old and new formats sit side by side. That is a programme, not a project.
- Timing: end of 2027 completion with a spring 2027 retailer milestone means design work is briefed now. Material testing and retailer approval sit between design and shelf, so the design window is the front of the programme. Timing 3.
- Stakeholders: the named programme lead is the entry point (role sourced from the sustainability report). The brand director still owns look and hierarchy; procurement will run process on a programme this size, so the brief flags procurement-led risk honestly and notes the studio does accept programme tenders per its fact sheet.
- Incumbent check: the current packaging is credited to an in-house team plus a production agency for artwork. No design studio of record found. Recorded as reported, two sources (pack credits and a supplier article), and flagged: in-house teams often keep concept work and brief out the system and rollout, which still fits the studio's structural strength.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: PURSUE NOW.** Next step: outreach planning aimed at the sustainability programme lead and the brand director in parallel, angle: phased material transition design, keeping a range coherent while formats change underneath it. Review in 30 days.

**Hypothesis:** Because Casa Verde Foods (fictional) has committed in its March 2026 sustainability report to move 18 chilled SKUs from plastic to fibre-based trays by the end of 2027, and a key retailer adds recyclability scoring at its spring 2027 reset, it needs a range redesign programme designed for phased material transition, briefed this year. The studio can credibly win a system and rollout role because it shipped a full-range tray-to-paperboard redesign in an adjacent category last year. Confidence: HIGH on the need, MEDIUM on the shape of the win (in-house may retain concept work). Falsifier: the pledge is withdrawn or the deadline slips publicly.

| Fit | Score | Basis |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Portfolio match | 3 | Direct material-transition range proof, adjacent category |
| Deal size fit | 3 | Multi-SKU programme in typical range (estimate) |
| Timing | 3 | Design window open now, hard milestones ahead |

## Critique

The discipline in this example is layer separation. The seller's vaguest input ("new regs coming, I think") was the most dramatic claim and the least supported, and it was excluded rather than blended in to make the case feel bigger. The case was already strong: a dated pledge with a named lead plus a retailer deadline is one of the most fundable patterns in packaging. Note the confidence split (HIGH on need, MEDIUM on shape of win): collapsing those into one score would have hidden the in-house risk, which is exactly the fact that shapes how the studio should approach.
