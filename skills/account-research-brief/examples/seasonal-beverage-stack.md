# Example 8: Seasonal Beverage Brand, Three Moderate Signals Stacking (Pursue)

All brands in this example are fictional. The facts below are invented for training, including the reset, the appointment, and the dates.

**Scenario:** A brand and packaging studio asks for a brief on Solstice Drinks Co. (fictional), a seasonal sparkling drinks brand built around summer and winter limited editions, sold through national grocery and convenience retailers.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Solstice, the seasonal seltzer people. A few bits: I think their main grocer resets the drinks fixture in spring and Solstice might have to resubmit the range. They hired a new brand manager over the summer, saw it on a people-moves roundup. And their range keeps growing, every time I look there are more flavours and another seasonal edition. None of it feels huge on its own. Our fact sheet: seasonal beverage ranges for two drinks brands, limited edition packaging systems, 12-SKU core range redesign in 2024."

Plus pasted material: a retail trade article dated 2026-10-05 (fictional) reporting that a national grocery retailer will reset its functional and flavoured sparkling drinks set in March 2027, with range submissions from brands due in December 2026; a trade people-moves item dated 2026-09-08 (fictional) reporting a new Brand Manager started at Solstice Drinks Co. in August 2026, an external hire from another drinks brand; retailer listing screenshots dated 2026-10-18 (fictional) showing the range at 14 SKUs, against 8 SKUs in listings from October 2025, including two seasonal editions layered on the core range.

## Reasoning

- Intake: origin is the seller's accumulated observations, now partially sourced. No prior relationship. Conflict check against the fact sheet client categories: clear.
- Sweep: three signals, each one moderate on its own, and the brief's job is to grade them individually before letting them stack.
  - Retailer reset (signal 6): a named retailer type, a dated reset (March 2027), and a brand submission deadline (December 2026), reported in retail trade press, 2026-10-05. Grade: Reported, one credible source. Moderate because Solstice is not named in the article; its participation is inferred from its current listing at that retailer, which the listing screenshots support.
  - New brand manager (signal 2): external hire, started August 2026, reported in a trade people-moves item, 2026-09-08. Grade: Reported. Moderate, not strong, for two reasons: brand manager is below the VP level where roster decisions usually sit, and the appointment is a single-source report. Still inside the early-tenure window when a new hire reviews suppliers and needs visible wins.
  - SKU growth (signal 4): the range grew from 8 to 14 SKUs in roughly a year, with seasonal editions layered on top, evidenced by dated retailer listings. Grade: Reported, from listing evidence the user supplied. Moderate because growth alone can be managed growth; what matters here is the pattern, core range plus rotating seasonal editions, which is exactly the structure that strains a packaging system.
- Stack check: this is the whole brief. Test each signal alone: the reset alone gives a deadline but no named need at this brand. The hire alone gives a person but no project. The SKU growth alone gives strain but no date. Together they point at the same work in the same window: Solstice must submit a range for the March 2027 reset that now runs to 14 SKUs including seasonal editions, its packaging system has grown by accretion rather than design, and a new brand manager owns that submission in their first year and will be judged on how the range performs at the reset. Three moderate, independent signals, one work type, one dated window. Per the playbook, stacks beat singles: Tree 1 strength upgrades from MODERATE to STRONG on the stack, with each component still graded honestly as Reported underneath it.
- Trees: Tree 1 STRONG (stack, as above). Tree 2 portfolio match 3: the studio has shipped seasonal beverage ranges and limited edition packaging systems for two drinks brands, same deliverable, same category, named proof. Tree 3 timing NOW with a hard edge: brand submissions are due December 2026 and reset artwork follows, which puts design decisions in the next 4 to 8 weeks. This window does not drift; miss it and the next comparable window is the following year's reset. Tree 4 buyer IDENTIFIED (BRAND): the new brand manager is the working owner of the submission, with the caveat recorded that a head of brand or founder above them is [UNVERIFIED] and may hold the final call.
- Deal size: a range tidy and seasonal system job across 14 SKUs is plausibly at the lower half of the studio's typical range rather than the middle. Score 2, labelled an estimate. It does not block pursuit: the reset relationship and the recurring seasonal cycle make the account worth more than the first project.
- What would weaken this: if the March 2027 reset slips, or the listings growth turns out to be retailer-specific variants rather than brand-led range growth. Both are checkable, and both are named below as falsifiers rather than left as doubts.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: PURSUE.** Next step: outreach planning aimed at the new brand manager, angle: submitting a 14-SKU range with a packaging system built for 8, ahead of a dated reset. First action within two weeks; the window closes in stages: submission deadline December 2026, reset March 2027. Review in 30 days, or immediately if the reset date moves.

**Hypothesis:** Because Solstice Drinks Co. (fictional) faces a March 2027 retailer reset with range submissions due December 2026, has grown from 8 to 14 SKUs including seasonal editions within a year, and hired a new brand manager in August 2026, it likely needs a packaging system tidy and seasonal edition framework before it resubmits the range. The studio can credibly win it because it has shipped seasonal beverage ranges and limited edition systems for two drinks brands. Confidence: MEDIUM. The need and the window rest on a stack of three Reported signals rather than any single Confirmed one; the stack is the evidence, and it would be proven wrong if the reset slips publicly or if a range rationalisation by another studio is announced.

| Fit | Score | Basis |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Portfolio match | 3 | Seasonal beverage ranges and limited edition systems, named proof |
| Deal size fit | 2 | 14-SKU system job, lower half of typical range (estimate) |
| Timing | 3 | Dated window: submissions December 2026, reset March 2027 |

## Critique

The discipline in this example is grading before stacking. A lazy brief reads three moderate signals, feels the accumulation, and writes "strong demand, high confidence". This brief does the opposite in the open: every signal keeps its Reported grade and its weakness in writing (the brand is not named in the reset article, the hire is one level junior and single-sourced, the SKU count comes from listings), and the upgrade to a pursue recommendation is justified by the one thing that actually justifies it, three independent signals converging on the same work inside the same dated window. Confidence stays MEDIUM, which is what sends the seller in prepared rather than cocky. Note also what the stack does to timing: a seasonal brand with a hard reset gives this brief something most medium briefs lack, a window with edges. The recommendation carries the dates, not a generic 90-day review.
