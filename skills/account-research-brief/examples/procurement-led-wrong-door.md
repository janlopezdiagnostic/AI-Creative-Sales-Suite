# Example 7: Strong Signal, Procurement-Led. Correct Output: PURSUE (Route: Taste Buyer First, Not Procurement)

All brands in this example are fictional. The facts below are invented for training, including the announcements and the dates.

**Scenario:** A packaging design studio asks for a brief on Tarnmoor Snacks (fictional), a savoury snacks brand owned by a mid-size food group, after hearing the brand is redesigning its whole range.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Tarnmoor are redoing EVERYTHING apparently, full range redesign for next year. Someone sent me a link, their procurement is setting up a rate panel for design services and applications close soon, like late November. Should we just get on the panel?? Our fact sheet: we did the 28-SKU crisps range redesign in 2025, also a nuts range, 20 SKUs. Note: our fact sheet says we do NOT join rate panels or tenders without a brand-side sponsor, owner decision back in 2024 after a bad panel experience."

Plus pasted material: a trade article dated 2026-09-22 (fictional) reporting Tarnmoor Snacks will redesign its full 24-SKU core range for a shelf relaunch in autumn 2027; a company announcement dated 2026-09-15 (fictional) confirming a range-wide packaging redesign programme; a supplier notice dated 2026-10-06 (fictional) from the parent group's procurement team inviting design studios to apply for a creative services rate panel, applications closing 2026-11-20, naming a procurement category manager as the contact; no brand-side owner named in any of the three sources.

## Reasoning

- Intake: origin is trade press plus a forwarded supplier notice. No prior relationship with the brand or the group. Conflict check against the fact sheet client categories: clear.
- Sweep: one signal, and it is genuinely strong. A full 24-SKU range redesign with a dated shelf relaunch (autumn 2027), confirmed by two independent sources (trade press, 2026-09-22, and the brand's own announcement, 2026-09-15). This is the packaging refresh and range redesign pattern at programme scale. Nothing else surfaces: no leadership news, no line extension, no material change. One strong signal is enough for Tree 1 if it points at specific, scopable work, and this one does.
- Trees: Tree 1 STRONG on the single confirmed programme signal. Tree 2 portfolio match 3: the studio shipped a 28-SKU crisps range redesign in 2025 and a 20-SKU nuts range, same deliverable, same category, named proof. Tree 3 timing NOW: an autumn 2027 shelf date for a 24-SKU range means design decisions land across the next two quarters, so the window is open.
- Tree 4 is where this brief is decided. Buyer: the only visible buyer in any source is procurement. The supplier notice names a procurement category manager running a rate panel with a closing date. No head of brand, brand director, or marketing lead is named anywhere. Buyer = IDENTIFIED (PROCUREMENT-LED).
- The collision: the fact sheet says the studio does not join rate panels or tenders without a brand-side sponsor. Tree 4's procurement exit says pursue only if the studio plays procurement-led accounts per the fact sheet, otherwise watchlist pending a brand-side sponsor appearing. The seller's instinct ("applications close soon, just get on the panel") treats the panel deadline as the opportunity. It is not. The redesign is the opportunity. The panel is a process for buying design by the hour once someone brand-side has already decided what good looks like.
- Why entering through procurement is the wrong door, stated plainly: a rate panel application is judged on rate card, insurance, and comparability before any portfolio is seen by anyone who judges taste. Rates get locked before scope exists. Panel membership does not equal a place on the redesign shortlist; panels routinely hold dozens of studios, most of whom never receive a brief. And a seller who enters as a commodity supplier first has a much harder time being heard as a creative partner later.
- The missing fact that would change the route: the name of the brand-side owner of the redesign programme. That person exists; a 24-SKU redesign has an owner. They are simply not visible in the sources checked. Identifying them is a research task with a deadline, not a reason to default to procurement.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: PURSUE (Route: Taste Buyer First, Not Procurement). Do not enter through procurement.** Do not submit the rate panel application. Next step: a focused research sprint to identify the brand-side owner of the redesign programme (brand or marketing leadership) from sourced material, to be completed before the panel closing date of 2026-11-20 so the studio still has options. If no brand-side owner can be identified from sources by 2026-11-13, the account downgrades to WATCHLIST with "identify the buying centre" as the standing action, and the panel decision is revisited only if a sponsor appears. Review date: 2026-11-13.

**Hypothesis:** Because Tarnmoor Snacks (fictional) has announced a full 24-SKU range redesign for an autumn 2027 shelf relaunch (company announcement, 2026-09-15; trade press, 2026-09-22), it will need a lead packaging studio for range system design across the next two quarters. The studio can credibly win it because it shipped a 28-SKU crisps range redesign in 2025. The conversation should start with the brand-side programme owner once identified, not with the procurement panel. Confidence: HIGH on the need and the studio's right to win it, LOW on route until the taste buyer is identified. Falsifier: the redesign is awarded to an incumbent or a group roster studio before a brand-side owner is found.

| Fit | Score | Basis |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Portfolio match | 3 | 28-SKU crisps range redesign, same category and deliverable |
| Deal size fit | 3 | 24-SKU range programme in typical range (estimate) |
| Timing | 3 | Window open now, autumn 2027 shelf date |

Note on the scores: the fit total is 9 of 9 and the recommendation is still not PURSUE NOW. Tree 4 gates the route. Scores measure whether the work is worth winning; the buyer tree measures whether the visible door leads anywhere worth going.

## Critique

Two failure modes were available here and the brief avoids both. The first is treating urgency as evidence: the panel closing date is real, but it is procurement's deadline, not the buying window, and letting it set the studio's behaviour hands the process to the one party that does not judge the work. The second failure mode is the opposite overcorrection: writing the account off as procurement-led and walking away from a confirmed, in-category, 24-SKU programme that scores 9 of 9 on fit. The correct output holds both facts at once. The signal is pursued, the route is refused, and the brief converts "we cannot see the buyer" into a named research action with its own deadline and a stated downgrade if it fails. What to watch: if the research sprint finds that the group genuinely has no brand-side owner for design and procurement truly runs selection end to end, that is new information, and the honest output at review is a deliberate owner-level decision about whether the no-panel rule bends for this account, made in the open, not a quiet exception made by a seller against a deadline.
