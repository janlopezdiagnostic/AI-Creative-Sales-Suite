# Example 1: CPG Packaging Line Extension (Strong)

All brands in this example are fictional. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended; the facts below are invented for training so the method can be judged without real company data.

**Scenario:** A packaging design studio asks for a brief on Harbor & Rye Pantry Co. (fictional), a mid-size sauce and condiments brand.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Heard Harbor & Rye might be doing dips?? Someone at the Natural Products show said they got into a big grocery chain for spring. Also their head of brand is new I think, started a few months ago, ex big agency. Our fact sheet: we've done sauce packaging (two ranges), dips for a deli brand, 40+ SKU systems. Do the brief."

Plus pasted material: a trade snippet dated 2026-10-20 (fictional) reporting Harbor & Rye will launch a 6-SKU refrigerated dips range in spring 2027 through a national grocery retailer; a company announcement dated 2026-08-14 (fictional) naming a new Head of Brand who joined from an agency background; a retailer listing screenshot showing the current sauce range unchanged since a 2023 redesign.

## Reasoning

- Intake: origin is trade-show conversation plus trade press; no prior relationship; conflict check against the fact sheet client categories: clear.
- Sweep: three signals surface. Line extension with a date and a retailer (trade press, 2026-10-20, confirmed). New head of brand, external hire, started about 10 weeks ago (company announcement, 2026-08-14, confirmed), squarely inside the day 30 to 150 window. Refresh cycle context: core sauce packs date from 2023, mid-cycle, not a trigger on its own.
- Stack check: the extension and the leadership change point at the same window. The new leader inherits a launch that needs packaging designed to stretch the sauce system into a refrigerated fixture the brand has never occupied. That is a stack, not two coincidences.
- Trees: Tree 1 STRONG (two confirmed signals, same work, same window). Tree 2 portfolio match 3: the studio has shipped dips packaging and large sauce SKU systems, named in the fact sheet. Tree 3 timing NOW: spring 2027 shelf date means design decisions land this quarter. Tree 4 buyer IDENTIFIED (BRAND): the new Head of Brand.
- Deal size: a 6-SKU launch system with launch materials sits inside the studio's typical range. Score 3, labelled an estimate.
- Risk: the design may already be briefed to an incumbent. Nothing found names an incumbent for dips; the sauce redesign was credited to a studio that has since closed its packaging practice (noted as reported, single source). This uncertainty goes in Risks, it does not block the recommendation.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: PURSUE NOW.** Next step: outreach planning aimed at the Head of Brand, angle: refrigerated fixture is a different shelf problem than ambient sauces. Review in 30 days.

**Hypothesis:** Because Harbor & Rye (fictional) launches a 6-SKU dips range into national grocery in spring 2027, it needs a packaging system extension into refrigerated by roughly the end of this quarter. The studio can credibly win it because it has shipped both dips packaging and 40+ SKU sauce systems. Confidence: HIGH. Falsifier: news that the launch design is complete or awarded.

| Fit | Score | Basis |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Portfolio match | 3 | Dips plus large sauce systems, named proof |
| Deal size fit | 3 | Launch system in typical range (estimate) |
| Timing | 3 | Window open now |

## Critique

What this example does right: the hypothesis rests on a stack, every signal carries a source and a date, and the incumbent risk is stated rather than buried. What to watch: the "incumbent studio closed its packaging practice" claim is single-source and was graded Reported, not Confirmed; a weaker analyst would have upgraded it silently and overstated confidence. Confidence stays HIGH only because the hypothesis survives without that claim.
