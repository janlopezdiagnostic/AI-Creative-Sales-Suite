# Example 5: Recovery. First Hypothesis Wrong, Corrected by a Second Signal

All brands in this example are fictional. The facts below are invented for training.

**Scenario:** A brand and packaging studio asks for a brief on Bluepine Beverages (fictional), a sparkling water and soda brand. The first research pass goes wrong; this example shows the correction, which is the skill working as designed.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Bluepine is rebranding, 100%. New logo spotted on their Instagram last week, totally different wordmark. They raised money last year too. Big rebrand opportunity, our beverage work is our best category. Brief it as a rebrand play."

Plus pasted material, first pass: three social screenshots showing a different wordmark on cans; a business press article dated 2025-11-04 (fictional) reporting a growth funding round.

## First pass reasoning (the wrong turn)

- The analyst anchored on the user's framing. Social screenshots showed a genuinely different wordmark, the funding article was real, and the playbook says funded brands spend on brand. First-pass hypothesis: "Bluepine is mid-rebrand; pitch identity and system work." Portfolio match 3, timing scored 3. A pursue-now brief was nearly issued.
- What the first pass did wrong: it treated social screenshots as a confirmed signal without a dateable primary source, and it never asked the obvious question: a different wordmark on which product, exactly?

## The second signal (what corrected it)

During the verify step, the analyst checked the brand's current range on a retailer site and found: the core sparkling water range still carries the existing identity, unchanged. The new wordmark appears only on a newly listed sub-range: a caffeinated line under a sub-brand name, launched quietly six weeks earlier. A trademark filing for the sub-brand name, dated five months before the launch (fictional), confirms this was a planned line architecture move, not an identity change. The core brand is not rebranding. The "new logo" is a sub-brand lockup, deliberately distinct.

## Corrected reasoning

- Rebuilt hypothesis: Bluepine (fictional) has launched a sub-brand into an adjacent occasion (energy) and, if it performs, will extend it: more flavours, possibly formats, plus trade and launch support. That is a line extension and SKU proliferation pattern, not a rebrand. The funding round, dated last November, supports exactly this kind of expansion and is context, not the trigger.
- The corrected opportunity is smaller than a rebrand but real, and it changes the pitch completely: extension system design, architecture discipline, speed to shelf. Pitching "we can rebrand you" to a brand that is not rebranding would have marked the studio as careless in the first meeting.
- Trees, rerun: Tree 1 MODERATE to STRONG (confirmed sub-brand launch via retailer listings plus a dated trademark filing; two signals pointing at extension work). Tree 2 portfolio match 3 still holds (beverage systems work includes a sub-brand architecture project). Tree 3 timing: the first wave has launched; extension waves typically follow within 2 to 3 quarters if sell-through holds, so timing 2, window opens next. Tree 4 buyer UNKNOWN: no brand lead named; recorded as the first research action.
- Deal size revised down: extension design for a sub-range, scored 2, labelled an estimate. The brief shows the revision openly rather than quietly editing the numbers.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: WATCHLIST, upgrading to PURSUE on the first extension signal** (new flavours or formats in the sub-range, a trade push, or retail expansion of the sub-brand). Review in 60 days.

**Hypothesis (corrected):** Because Bluepine Beverages (fictional) launched a caffeinated sub-brand six weeks ago with its own identity, it will likely need extension and architecture design work as the sub-range grows over the next two quarters. The studio's beverage system and sub-brand architecture proof is directly relevant. Confidence: MEDIUM. Falsifier: the sub-range is delisted or folded back into the core brand.

**Correction note (required in the brief):** An earlier draft of this brief described a core brand rebrand. That was wrong. The new wordmark belongs to a sub-brand only; core packaging is unchanged. The error came from grading social screenshots as confirmed. Signal grading in this brief: retailer listings Confirmed, trademark filing Confirmed, social screenshots superseded.

## Critique

Recoveries like this are why the workflow has a verify step and why briefs carry a correction note instead of silent edits. The failure mode was anchoring: the user arrived certain, supplied vivid screenshots, and the first pass inherited the certainty. The save came from a boring habit: checking the full current range somewhere neutral (a retailer listing) before writing the hypothesis. Note also what did not happen: the analyst did not split the difference ("maybe a partial rebrand"). The second signal did not soften the first claim; it replaced it, and the brief says so.
