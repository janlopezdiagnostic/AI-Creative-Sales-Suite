# Example 4: Weak / No Signal. Correct Output: DO NOT PURSUE

All brands in this example are fictional. The facts below are invented for training.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio asks for a brief on Alder Peak Snacks (fictional), a regional snack bar brand the founder met briefly at a trade show two years ago.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Alder Peak Snacks, do a brief. They're like 60 people, consumer goods, growing category (protein bars up 12% apparently). Must be doing well. We should be in there. Our snack packaging work is strong (three brands). Go."

Plus pasted material: a directory listing confirming head office location and approximate headcount; the brand's website, which credits its packaging to a named studio and lists an agency of record for brand work; a category statistics article about protein bar market growth generally, which does not mention Alder Peak at all.

## Reasoning

- Intake: origin is a two-year-old trade show meeting. No contact since. Conflict check: clear.
- Sweep: run honestly and in full. Rebrand: none found. Leadership: no changes found; the marketing lead has been in role four years per the site. Line extension: none announced. SKU count: stable range, visibly managed, no proliferation. Funding or M&A: none found. Retailer reset: the brand's main retailer reset its category eight months ago and Alder Peak kept its facings with current packaging (trade snippet, dated, found in the sweep). Sustainability: generic "we care about the planet" copy on the site, no pledge, no date, no material. Refresh cycle: packaging redesigned two years ago by the credited studio, current, disciplined system.
- The only "positive" material is category growth. Category growth is not an account signal. It describes a market, not a buying trigger at this brand, and the playbook names this exact move as generic firmographic spam.
- Worse, two findings point actively away: an agency of record is in place for brand work, and the category reset already happened with the current packaging surviving it, which means the near-term packaging window was tested and closed.
- Trees: Tree 1 NONE (no dated signals; the reset signal is dated and points the other way). Exit: DO NOT PURSUE, before portfolio match even matters. Tree 2 would score 3 (the studio's snack proof is genuinely strong) and that changes nothing: strong proof times no trigger equals no pursuit. Tree 4: buyer identified (brand side, long-tenured marketing lead), which also changes nothing.

## Final brief (condensed)

**Recommendation: DO NOT PURSUE.** Next step: none. File the brief, add the account to the signal log with the reset date recorded, and set a review for two quarters out or on any of these triggers: leadership change, AOR change, a line extension announcement, or the next category reset cycle at its main retailer.

**Hypothesis:** None. There is no evidence of a buying trigger at Alder Peak Snacks (fictional). The brand has a current, disciplined packaging system, an agency of record, a recently survived category reset, and stable leadership. The case for pursuing rests entirely on category growth and headcount, which the method rejects.

| Fit | Score | Basis |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Portfolio match | 3 | Strong snack proof (recorded, and deliberately not decisive) |
| Deal size fit | Not scored | No project exists to size |
| Timing | 0 | No window; last window closed eight months ago |

## Critique

This is the example that tests whether the analyst works for the evidence or for the person asking. The user pushed ("We should be in there. Go.") and the studio's fit was real. A compliant brief would have dressed category statistics up as an opportunity and sent a seller into an account with an incumbent, no trigger, and a closed window. The correct output protects the seller's time and the studio's credibility, and it does the second job of a do-not-pursue brief: leaving a dated record so nobody re-researches this account from zero next quarter and talks themselves into it again.
