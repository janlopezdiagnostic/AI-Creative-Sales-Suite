## Field guide

- **What they judge:** Write the actual judgement, not the job title. Procurement rarely picks the creative winner but frequently kills the unprepared; the brand owner usually owns the shortlist; the founder is brand director, budget, and taste in one person. Use the stakeholder section of the playbook for what each centre of gravity cares about.
- **What proof they need:** Match proof to the judge. Category work and consumer understanding for the brand owner, rate and risk evidence for procurement, one decisive piece of work for a founder. Proof the studio cannot actually show does not go in this column; if it is in the fact sheet as a target category rather than proven work, say so.
- **Risk notes:** Record the risks this stakeholder creates or carries: a vacant or interim seat (buying usually freezes), a brand owner in their first 90 days (a signal and a risk at once), a panel or tender the studio's fact sheet says it does not join, a group roster that overrides local choice, a conflict with a current client, a named person whose source is weak or old.
- **Sources and dates:** This map does not carry its own source columns because it is a summary of the brief. Every name here must trace to a dated source in the brief's signal table or stakeholder section. If you cannot trace it, remove the name or mark it [UNVERIFIED] with the step that would verify it.
- **Buying centre verdict:** After completing the map, record the Tree 4 verdict from `decision-trees.md` in the brief: IDENTIFIED (BRAND), IDENTIFIED (PROCUREMENT-LED), IDENTIFIED (FOUNDER), UNKNOWN, or IN TRANSITION. The map is the evidence for the verdict; if the map is mostly "unknown", the verdict cannot be better than UNKNOWN.
