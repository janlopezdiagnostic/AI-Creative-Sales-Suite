# Example 5: Spec Work Demand Converted to Paid Discovery

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, project manager 500/day
- Founder rule: free pitch work is capped at 2 days of studio time, and only with a named budget, decision maker, and decision date
- Paid discovery: fixed price, fee fully creditable against the main project if it proceeds within 60 days (studio policy for this example)

## Messy input (RFP extract and covering note)
"Aulden Home, a regional homeware retailer's own brand team, is inviting 5 studios to pitch for a candle and home fragrance range, 'approximately 20 SKUs, maybe more'. Pitch requirement: two creative routes for the hero candle, presented in person in 3 weeks. Budget 'to be confirmed following the pitch'. Decision maker not named. Timeline for the range: 'in stores for autumn'. A note from the founder: 'The brand is big here. This could be huge for us. Should we just do the pitch?'"

## Reasoning
Decision tree 2, step 1: is there a real budget, a named decision maker, and a decision date? Budget unconfirmed, decision maker unnamed, range size unknown ("maybe more"). Three failures. The estimated cost of two routes done properly is 6 to 8 studio days, far over the founder's 2 day cap.
Declining flat would end a genuinely promising relationship. The move is the paid discovery conversion: a 2 week, fixed price phase whose deliverable is exactly what the client needs to run a fair process (audit of the current own brand range, architecture for the 20 plus SKUs, and a scoped, priced plan), offered to them as a better first step than a beauty parade. If they refuse paid discovery and insist on free routes, the answer is a warm no, which also tells the founder what "could be huge" was actually worth.
Note the honesty point: discovery is not a paid pitch. The deliverable is thinking and a plan, which the client keeps whatever they decide. One concept direction may emerge from the architecture work and can be shown as an illustration of the plan, but two finished routes are the project, and the project is not free.

## The maths (discovery phase)
- Own brand and competitor audit: strategist 2 days = 1,600
- Range architecture and hierarchy: creative director 1 day = 950
- Design feasibility and format review (with their candle supplier's constraints): senior designer 1 day = 750
- Scoped plan, timeline, and full project proposal: project manager 0.5 day = 250
- Discovery total: 3,550, fully creditable against the project fee if signed within 60 days

## Output: the reply (client facing, excerpt)
"Thank you for including us. We do not produce free creative routes, and I would rather explain what we do instead, because I think it serves this range better. Your brief has three open questions (final SKU count, architecture across candles and fragrance, budget) that make any route we drew now a guess, and guesses at pitch stage become expensive changes later.
What we propose: a two week discovery phase at 3,550, credited in full against the project if we proceed within 60 days. You receive an audit of your current own brand presentation, a range architecture for the full line, and a fixed, scoped price for the design programme, with dates worked back from the autumn drop. If you then choose another studio, the audit and architecture are yours and were worth buying.
If you still want to run a creative pitch, we will step back from this round with thanks, and we would be glad to be considered for the rollout work."

## What happened (illustrative outcome)
The client bought discovery. The audit found the "20 SKUs" were really 26 across two formats, which repriced every pitch they would have received. The studio won the programme at a fixed fee built on the architecture, with the discovery fee credited. One of the five invited studios did pitch free and was not selected.

## Critique
Strengths: the no is structural, not moralising; it names the client's own unanswered questions as the reason, offers a product instead of an apology, and keeps the door open for rollout work even in refusal. The credit makes discovery close to risk free for a serious client while still filtering out unserious ones, because writing the cheque is the filter.
Watch outs: discovery credit policies differ by studio; if the fee is not credited, the reply must not imply it is. Also, the outcome paragraph is the happy path; the honest base rate is that many RFP issuers will simply move to the next studio on the list, and the founder has to be genuinely at peace with that before sending this reply.
