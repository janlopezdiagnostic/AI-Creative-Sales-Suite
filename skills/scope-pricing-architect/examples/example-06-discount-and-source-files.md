# Example 6: Discount Demand and the Free Source Files Ask

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Included revision rounds: 2 per phase. Deposit 50 percent
- Source files: excluded, priced at 1,500 per design family. Discount policy: scope trades only, rate does not move

## Messy input (client email, after receiving the Better tier proposal)
"Proposal looks great and the team loves the direction. Two things. Finance needs 20 percent off to sign this quarter, scope stays the same. Also we will need all the native files included free, our brand manager insists, she had them free at her last company. If you can do both we sign Friday."

## The proposal they are answering (Better tier, as sent)
Total 10,650, built as: strategy and architecture 1,275; master design 2 families 3,950; adaptations 6 SKUs 2,325; production liaison with their printer 1,150 (artworker 2 days 900 + project manager 0.5 day 250); project management 1,500 (3 days); proofing 450 (artworker 1 day).

## Reasoning
Two separate asks, two separate trees.
Ask one is decision tree 4: a percentage demand on an unchanged scope. The rate does not move. The answer is a trade menu with real numbers, plus an invitation to name a budget if 20 percent is really a ceiling rather than an opening position.
Ask two is decision tree 5: source files were excluded in the proposal and priced at 1,500 per family, 3,000 for this project. "Free at her last company" is edge case 14: acknowledge, do not litigate a stranger's model, restate the priced route, and explain the practical reason once (native files transfer the ability to extend the system, and carry font and asset licences the client would need to hold).
The Friday deadline is a pressure device, friendly or otherwise. The reply is same day and complete, but the structure does not bend for the calendar.

## The maths (trade menu)
- Remove production liaison: minus 1,150. The client's team then manages the printer directly; the studio supplies print ready files and one handover call.
- Reduce artwork rounds from 2 to 1 consolidated round: minus 750 (senior designer 1 day; the round being cut is a master family round, which the senior designer leads. Adaptation rounds are designer and artworker time and price differently.)
- Both trades: 10,650 reduced to 8,750, a 17.8 percent reduction in price with a genuinely smaller scope.
- Note the arithmetic honesty: a full 20 percent on unchanged scope was 2,130 of pure rate. The trades get the client close to their number only by removing work, which is the entire point, and the reply says so in one sentence.

## Output: the reply (client facing, excerpt)
"Glad the direction landed. On the two points, here is what we can do.
Our rate is our rate, so I cannot take 20 percent off the same scope, and I would not trust a studio whose numbers moved that easily. What I can do is resize the scope to a number Finance can sign. Two options: drop our printer liaison (your team runs the printer directly, we supply files and a handover call), which brings it to 9,500; or that plus one consolidated artwork round instead of two, which brings it to 8,750. The full scope stays on the table at 10,650 whenever you want it, including as a phase 2.
On native files: they are not included in our fees, here or in the proposal you have, because they hand over the working system, not just the finished packs, and they carry font and asset licences that sit with the studio and would need to transfer or be re-licensed to your team. They are available at 1,500 per family, 3,000 here, with a handover session so your team gets real value from them. What is included, as standard, is every final production file and a complete print ready set.
Either scope works for a Friday signature at the deposit terms in the proposal. Tell me which structure Finance prefers and I will paper it the same day."

## Outcome (illustrative)
The client chose the 9,500 tier, kept both revision rounds, dropped the liaison, and did not buy the source files. Three months later they bought the liaison back as a change order during a difficult print run, at standard rates, without rancour on either side.

## Critique
Strengths: both asks were answered in full, in one message, with numbers the client could take to Finance; nothing was conceded under the Friday pressure, and the trade menu got within reach of their target while removing real work. The files answer gives the practical reason once and does not repeat it defensively.
Watch outs: the reply assumes the sender has authority for those exact trades; the rate sheet's discount section should name who may offer which trades, because an account exec improvising trades beyond their authority recreates the original problem in a new shape. Also, dropping production liaison is the trade most likely to be regretted by the client, as the outcome shows; document clearly, as the scope did, that printer management moved to their side, or the studio will be blamed for print issues it no longer controls.
