# Example 8: Finished Redesign Converted to a Range Guardianship Retainer

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Minimum booking unit: half day. Overage on retainers billed at the standard day rates above
- Source files: excluded, priced at 1,500 per design family
- Retainer terms for this example: monthly fee invoiced in advance, unused days do not roll over

## Messy input (voice note summary from the founder)
"Northfield Pantry launch went well. The 12 SKU extension is on shelf and selling. Their marketing lead says there will be a steady drip: flavour swaps, two or three new SKUs a quarter, seasonal sleeves, retailer specific artwork, pack copy updates when regulations change. She asked for 'some kind of monthly arrangement so we are not doing paperwork for every little job'. She also asked if the monthly fee could be discounted because it is regular. I do not want to discount the rate, and I do not want us drowning in unscoped little jobs either. Build the retainer scope."

## Reasoning
1. The volume is real and recurring, which is the retainer test in the playbook (Part 2). A monthly block of days, reserved in advance, fits a drip of adaptations and updates better than a pile of small fixed fees and change orders.
2. The rate does not move. Retainer pricing here is the standard day rates, unchanged. What the client buys with regularity is reserved capacity, a named team that already knows the range, and no per job paperwork. The discount ask is answered by structure, not by a cheaper rate (playbook Part 11).
3. The block is sized from the client's own forecast: two or three SKU jobs a quarter plus updates is roughly 8 working days a month across roles, weighted to design and artwork, with a small strategist allocation for range decisions so adaptations do not quietly become unpriced strategy.
4. Guardianship is defined, because the word alone means whatever the client hopes it means. In scope below means: the studio checks new and changed artwork against the range system and guidelines, flags drift, and keeps the range coherent. It does not mean unlimited design, and it does not mean the studio approves regulated content.
5. Unused days do not roll over, and the scope says so plainly. The fee reserves capacity the studio cannot sell to anyone else. The fairness valve is not rollover, it is the quarterly review: if the block is consistently too big or too small, it gets resized at the review with numbers in front of both parties.
6. The exit is written now, while relations are warm: end at a quarter boundary on 30 days written notice. A retainer either party dreads leaving is a retainer that gets resented, and resentment is how ranges drift to other studios.

## The maths (internal, shown here for training)
Monthly block, by role:
- Creative director: 1 day x 950 = 950
- Strategist: 0.5 day x 800 = 400
- Senior designer: 2 days x 750 = 1,500
- Designer: 2 days x 550 = 1,100
- Artworker: 2 days x 450 = 900
- Project manager: 1 day x 500 = 500
- Monthly total: 8.5 days; 950 + 400 + 1,500 + 1,100 + 900 + 500 = 5,350
- Quarterly total: 5,350 x 3 = 16,050
- Annual equivalent at the same block: 5,350 x 12 = 64,200 (shown for planning only, the commitment is quarterly, see Terms)
Overage illustrations, at the standard rates, half day minimum:
- One extra designer day in a heavy month: 1 x 550 = 550
- One extra artworker half day for an urgent retailer file: 0.5 x 450 = 225
- One extra senior designer day plus creative director half day for an unplanned range review: 750 + 475 = 1,225

## Output: retainer scope (client facing excerpt)
**Range guardianship retainer: 5,350 per month**, invoiced monthly in advance. A reserved block of 8.5 studio days per month (creative director 1, strategist 0.5, senior designer 2, designer 2, artworker 2, project manager 1), booked against the Northfield Pantry range.
**In scope, drawn from the block:** new SKU adaptations within the existing 3 design families, up to 2 per month; flavour, size, and colourway variants; seasonal sleeves and retailer specific artwork versions within the existing system; pack copy and regulatory panel updates using your supplied, approved copy; artwork checks on anything produced by third parties against the range guidelines, with drift flagged in writing; one range review call per month; print ready file supply to your printer for work produced under this retainer.
**Out of scope, quoted separately:** a new design family or a range redesign; naming and strategy programmes; photography, illustration, and copywriting; native source files (available at 1,500 per design family); print costs and couriers; work for brands other than Northfield Pantry.
**How the block works:** requests come through your named marketing lead to our project manager, who confirms the estimated days before work starts. A simple usage note goes out with each invoice: days used, by role, and what they produced. Unused days do not roll over; the fee reserves the capacity whether or not the month is busy. If a request would exceed the remaining block, we quote the overage first (standard day rates, half day minimum) and start only on your written approval.
**Quarterly review:** at the end of each quarter we review usage, range plans, and the coming quarter's forecast together, and either side may propose resizing the block for the next quarter. If usage runs under 70 percent of the block for two consecutive months, we will raise resizing at the review ourselves rather than let you pay for capacity you are not using.
**Terms and exit:** initial term two quarters. After the first quarter, either party may end the retainer at the end of a quarter by giving 30 days written notice. Fees are payable for the full notice period, because the capacity stays reserved. On exit we supply print ready files for all completed, paid for work, and a short handover note listing live files and their locations. Fees already paid cover capacity reserved and are not refundable for unused days. Drafting aid, have counsel review before use.

## Critique
Strengths: the discount ask was answered without touching the rate: the client gets certainty, priority, and one monthly invoice instead of per job paperwork, and the studio sells the same days at the same prices with better forecasting. The in and out lists are the real protection; guardianship could have expanded to cover anything with the brand's name on it, and instead it is a countable set of job types with a monthly SKU cap. The quarterly review with the 70 percent trigger does the fairness work that rollover usually pretends to do, and it creates the natural moment to grow the block when the range expands.
Watch outs: retainers fail quietly through unlogged small asks, especially direct messages to designers that bypass the project manager. The usage note only works if every request actually flows through it, and the founder has to enforce that routing kindly but consistently in the first two months. Second watch out: the 2 adaptation cap per month will be tested in launch heavy quarters; the answer is the pre quoted overage route, not quietly absorbing a third and fourth SKU and renegotiating the retainer from a position of grievance at the review.
