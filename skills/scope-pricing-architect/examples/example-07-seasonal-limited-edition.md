# Example 7: Seasonal Limited Edition Packaging, Hard Retailer Deadline

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, strategist 800/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Included revision rounds: 2 per phase. Additional round: priced per phase at quoting
- Rush multiplier: 1.5x on affected phases. Deposit 50 percent. Milestones 50 / 30 / 20
- Source files: excluded, priced at 1,500 per design family
- Late regulatory copy: work to place or replace regulated panel content after the copy freeze date is a change order at the standard day rates

## Messy input (email from the client, verbatim style)
"Hi, for the winter set at our main retailer we want a limited edition range under our Bramblewick Kitchen brand: 4 SKUs (Spiced Plum Jam, Cranberry and Orange Preserve, Ginger Pear Butter, Chestnut and Honey Spread), one seasonal family, same jars and labels as the core range. The retailer has confirmed the seasonal set: packs on shelf 3 November, their planogram locks early October, and our printer needs final files by 26 September, so artwork has to be locked by 19 September, no movement on that. Marketing copy is nearly done. Regulatory copy is the slow bit, our compliance consultant is reviewing the Chestnut and Honey claims and it might land a bit late. Can you plan it so we do not pay rush fees? We would rather hear the real dates now than panic in September."

## Reasoning
1. Deliverables are countable: 4 SKUs, 1 seasonal design family, 1 existing format, dielines already in the studio's hands from the core range. Fixed fee model (decision tree 1, Exit A).
2. The deadline is hard but the calendar is honest. Kickoff in the week of 4 August gives 7 working weeks to the 19 September artwork lock. The studio's standard timeline for a 4 SKU seasonal family is 5 working weeks including 2 revision rounds per phase, so the plan carries roughly 2 weeks of buffer. Rush is not needed, and the scope says so in writing, because the buffer is planned, not luck.
3. The rush question is therefore answered before it is asked: no multiplier in any tier. If the client's own dates slip and compress the remaining timeline below the standard, the rush term exists in the rate sheet and can be elected then, as a visible line, on the affected phases only.
4. The real risk is not the design work, it is the regulatory copy. The plan gives it a date instead of a hope: marketing copy due 22 August, regulatory copy due at the copy freeze on 5 September, two weeks before artwork lock. Panel layout work is scheduled inside that window.
5. Late regulatory copy is handled by a stated term, agreed now while nobody is under pressure: regulated content received after the copy freeze, or changed after it has been placed, is a change order at the standard day rates, and the artwork lock moves day for day by the delay unless the client elects rush. The Chestnut and Honey claims are the client's and their consultant's responsibility; the studio places the approved copy accurately, it does not substantiate it.
6. Limited edition means a defined life. The scope covers this season's set only. A repeat run next year, or extension to further SKUs, is a new scope or a retainer conversation, stated here so it is not argued later.

## The maths (internal, shown here for training)
- Seasonal concept direction, 1 family: senior designer 2 days (1,500) + creative director 0.5 day (475) = 1,975
- Master artwork, lead SKU: senior designer 1 day (750) + artworker 0.5 day (225) = 975
- Adaptations, 3 further SKUs: per SKU designer 0.5 day (275) + artworker 0.25 day (112.50) = 387.50; x 3 = 1,162.50
- Regulatory panel setup and copy placement, 4 SKUs: artworker 1 day = 450
- Print proofing support: artworker 0.5 day = 225
- Project management: 1.5 days = 750
- Better total: 1,975 + 975 + 1,162.50 + 450 + 225 + 750 = 5,537.50
- Good total: Better minus the seasonal concept direction phase (the studio works to the client's written seasonal brief) = 5,537.50 - 1,975 = 3,562.50
- Best total: Better plus limited edition adaptation notes for next season's repeat (senior designer 1 day, 750), a second proofing round (artworker 0.5 day, 225), and a launch week support block (designer 1 day, 550) = 5,537.50 + 750 + 225 + 550 = 7,062.50
- Rush: not applied. Standard timeline fits the deadline with buffer, as set out in Reasoning point 2.

## Output: tiered scope (client facing excerpt)
**Good, 3,562.50.** Master artwork for the lead SKU and adaptations for the remaining 3 SKUs, working to your written seasonal brief and the core range system, regulatory panel setup and copy placement for all 4 SKUs, one proofing round, project management throughout, 2 revision rounds per phase. Choose this only if the seasonal direction is already settled internally; there is no concept phase in this tier.
**Better (recommended), 5,537.50.** Everything in Good, plus a seasonal concept direction phase (one seasonal route for the family, developed and approved before artwork begins) so the limited edition reads as an event rather than a recolour.
**Best, 7,062.50.** Everything in Better, plus written adaptation notes so next season's repeat can be produced quickly, a second proofing round, and a launch week support block for retailer queries and last minute artwork requests.
Not included in any tier: copywriting, claims substantiation and regulatory approval (yours and your consultant's), print costs, photography or illustration commissioning, native source files (available at 1,500 for the seasonal family).
Dates, planned not rushed: kickoff week of 4 August; marketing copy due 22 August; copy freeze, including all regulatory copy, 5 September; artwork lock 19 September; final files to printer 26 September. On this plan no rush fee applies, and none is in the price.
Late copy term: regulatory copy received or changed after the 5 September copy freeze is placed as a change order at our standard day rates (artworker 450/day, designer 550/day), quoted and approved in writing before the work is done. If late copy uses up the buffer, the artwork lock and the printer date move day for day by the same delay, or you may elect rush at 1.5x on the affected phases to hold the original dates. We will tell you which position we are in on the day the copy arrives, not afterwards.
Terms summary: 50 percent deposit, 30 percent at artwork approval, 20 percent before final files. Kill fee per SOW stages. Changes quoted and approved in writing before work starts.
Assumptions: 4 SKUs on the existing core range dieline; jars, labels, and printer unchanged; consolidated feedback from one named decision maker within 3 working days per round.

## SOW excerpt
"1. Deliverables: master artwork for 1 seasonal design family (Bramblewick Kitchen Winter Limited Edition); master artwork for 1 SKU and adapted artwork for 3 further SKUs (4 SKUs total) as listed in Appendix A; regulatory panel layout and placement of client supplied, client approved regulated content on all 4 SKUs; print ready files per SKU to the existing dieline supplied at signing (a supplier dieline change after signing is a change order). 2. Dates: copy freeze 5 September, artwork lock 19 September, files to printer 26 September. 3. Late copy: regulated content supplied or amended after the copy freeze is a change order at the rates in the studio rate sheet, and delivery dates move day for day by the delay unless the client elects rush terms in writing. 4. Revisions: 2 rounds per phase, a round being one consolidated set of feedback from the client's named decision maker."

## Critique
Strengths: the client asked for a plan without rush fees and got one that is checkable: the dates, the buffer, and the standard timeline are all on the page, so "no rush needed" is a conclusion they can verify rather than a promise they have to trust. The late copy risk, which is the most likely way this project goes wrong, is priced and scheduled by a term agreed in August, when agreeing it is cheap, instead of negotiated in September, when it would not be.
Watch outs: the whole plan rests on the copy freeze being treated as real on the client side. If the compliance consultant's copy arrives on 12 September, the term protects the fee but the retailer date may still move, and the studio must send the day for day notice the same day, in writing, while there is still a choice about rush. The second watch out is scope life: a limited edition that sells well generates a repeat conversation, and the Best tier's adaptation notes are what make that repeat profitable rather than a free rerun.
