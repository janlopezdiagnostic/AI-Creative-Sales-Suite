# Example 4: Recovery Case, an Under-Scoped Job Rescued by Change Order

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are calculated from the fictional rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Creative director 950/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Additional revision round at artwork phase: 600 per round. Change order rate: standard day rates
- Source files: excluded by default, buyout priced at 1,500 for a single design family

## How it went wrong (the situation the skill is handed)
Tern and Co, a pet food brand, was quoted 6,000 verbally by a previous account manager for "packaging for the range", understood by the client as 10 SKUs. Nothing was signed beyond a one line email: "Range packaging, 6,000, starting Monday." No revision cap, no SKU appendix, no files clause.
Six weeks in: the range is now 16 SKUs. Artwork is on its fifth round of feedback, arriving from three people separately. The client has just asked for the native files "so our intern can tweak feeding guides". The design team is demoralised and the project has consumed roughly double the days the fee can carry. The founder asks: "Fix this without losing the client."

## Reasoning
1. Do not relitigate the past angrily, and do not absorb the future silently. The rescue has three parts: an honest reset conversation, a written scope from today forward, and change orders for the deltas. The original 6,000 for the original understanding (10 SKUs, reasonable rounds) is honoured; the studio's error was the missing paperwork, and the client should not be punished for it. Everything beyond the original understanding is new scope.
2. The deltas are countable: 6 extra SKUs at adaptation rate, and artwork rounds beyond a fair allowance. Offer a fair allowance of 2 further consolidated rounds included from today, with rounds after that at the rate sheet price; do not bill the past rounds, which were never defined. Bill only forward.
3. The intern request is a source file buyout conversation (decision tree 5), priced at the rate sheet basis, with the licence caveat list. It also signals the client may intend to finish the work in house; that is their right once terms are agreed, and the kill and handover terms should be tidied in the same reset document.
4. Everything goes into one reset SOW the client signs: original scope restated, change order 1 attached, terms from today. One awkward meeting buys a clean project.

## The maths (change order 1)
- Additional SKUs, 6: per SKU designer 0.5 day (275) + artworker 0.25 day (112.50) = 387.50; x 6 = 2,325
- Forward artwork rounds beyond the included allowance: priced at 600 per round, quoted as used (none billed yet)
- Change order 1 total: 2,325. Revised project total: 8,325
- Source file buyout, if the client proceeds: 1,500 (one design family), plus a half day handover session at designer rate 275 if wanted

## Output: reset conversation script and change order excerpt
Script, opening: "I owe you a cleaner setup than the one you got. We quoted the range when it was 10 SKUs and we never wrote down rounds or files, that is on us. Your 6,000 covers that original job and we will honour it. The range is 16 now, and the files question needs an answer in writing. Here is a one page scope for the project as it stands, a change order for the 6 extra SKUs at our standard adaptation rate, with the maths shown line by line from the rate sheet, and the file buyout price if you want your team to handle tweaks. Nothing changes about the creative direction, this is just the paperwork catching up with the project."
Change order 1 (excerpt, using the template):
"What changed: range extended from 10 SKUs to 16 SKUs at client request, confirmed across meetings in weeks 4 to 6. Why outside original scope: the original agreement was for the 10 SKU range as briefed; SKUs 11 to 16 did not exist in that brief. Additional work: artwork adaptation for 6 SKUs as listed. Price: 2,325 at the studio's standard adaptation basis (designer 0.5 day plus artworker 0.25 day per SKU). Schedule effect: final artwork moves from [date] to [date plus 5 working days] if approved this week. If declined: the 10 SKU scope completes to the original plan."

## Critique
Strengths: the rescue protects the relationship by absorbing the studio's own paperwork failure and charging only for genuine client side growth, priced from the studio's standard rate sheet and shown line by line, rather than invented in a tense moment. (Note the discipline: the change order does NOT claim to use "the rate the original price implies." The original 6,000 was never decomposed, so any implied per SKU rate would be fiction; the honest anchor is the published rate sheet.) The forward round cap stops the bleed without billing archaeology.
Watch outs: this works once. If the studio's next quote also goes out as a one line email, no change order system will save it; the real fix is the studio rule that no work starts without the SOW template, which the founder adopted after this project. Also, the buyout may mean the client finishes in house and the final quality wobbles; the handover session offer is the studio's best protection for its own portfolio.
