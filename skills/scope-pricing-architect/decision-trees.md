# Decision Trees

Run these before improvising. Each tree ends in a defined exit. If your situation matches no branch, stop, tell the user what does not fit, and let the user decide.

## Tree 1. Pricing model choice

START: Can the deliverables be counted today (SKUs, formats, rounds, phases)?
- YES → Is the work one defined project, or ongoing volume?
  - One defined project → **FIXED FEE.** Build bottom up from the rate sheet, cap revision rounds, state assumptions. Exit A.
  - Ongoing volume (monthly adaptations, rolling launches) → **RETAINER.** Define the pool, the overage rate, the rollover rule, and a quarterly review. Exit B.
- NO → Is the blocker internal to the client (undecided range, undecided architecture, disputed direction)?
  - YES → **PAID DISCOVERY.** Fixed price phase whose deliverable is the audit, the architecture, and the scoped plan. Then re-run this tree with real numbers. Exit C.
  - NO, the work itself is genuinely exploratory or embedded (the client needs a design team, not a deliverable) → **DAY RATE.** Minimum booking unit, approved weekly or monthly cap, weekly reporting of days used. Exit D.
- At any node: is the timeline compressed below the studio's standard for this scope?
  - YES → Apply the rush multiplier from the rate sheet to the affected phases, shown as its own line, whichever model you chose.
- Exit rule: state the chosen model and the reason in one line at the top of the scope.

## Tree 2. Spec work demand → paid discovery conversion

START: The client asks for free creative (concepts, a route, "just show us one pack") before committing.
1. Is this a formal pitch with a real budget, a named decision maker, and a decision date?
   - NO → Decline the free creative, warmly, in one sentence. Offer paid discovery (step 3). Exit if refused: walk away; a client who needs free work to choose will need free work to continue.
   - YES → Continue to 2.
2. What is the studio's cost of pitching (days at rate sheet rates)?
   - If the pitch cost is more than a small, founder-approved percentage of the project value → Offer paid discovery instead (step 3). If refused, decline the pitch and keep the relationship door open.
   - If within the approved limit → Pitch, but scope the pitch itself: state what will be shown (one route, concept level, watermarked or presented not sent), protect IP in the pitch terms (drafting aid, counsel review), and diary the decision date. No decision by the date → Tree 5 style follow up, once, then close the file.
3. Paid discovery offer structure: fixed price, 2 to 4 weeks, deliverable is audit + architecture + scoped plan, fee creditable against the full project if the studio chooses to offer the credit. Frame it honestly: "You are buying the thinking that makes the full price accurate. If you take the plan elsewhere, it was still worth having."
Exit: discovery signed, pitch run inside limits, or a clean, warm no.

## Tree 3. Scope creep mid-project → change order

START: New work is requested that is not in the deliverables inventory.
1. Is it genuinely tiny (under half a day) AND the first such ask on this project AND strategically worth goodwill?
   - YES → Do it, and record it in the project log as a goodwill item with its estimated value. The record matters: it is evidence of pattern if asks continue, and of generosity when the account is reviewed. Exit.
   - NO → Continue.
2. Prepare the change order (template) with price AND schedule effect. Send it before any work starts.
3. Client response:
   - Approved → Schedule the work, update the project total, continue. Exit.
   - Declined → Offer the swap: remove something of similar value from the remaining scope. If they swap, document the revised scope. If they decline both, the original scope continues unchanged and the record notes the decision. Exit.
   - "Just do it, we will sort the paperwork later" → Decline politely and hold. Offer to start on written email approval with the change order to follow the same day. If they will not put it in an email, they have answered the question. Exit.
   - Pushback that the work "was obviously included" → Point to the SOW deliverables section, quote the relevant line, stay factual and friendly. If the studio genuinely implied it, concede gracefully this once, note it, and fix the template wording so the ambiguity cannot recur. Exit.

## Tree 4. Discount demand → scope trade

START: The client asks for a lower price on an unchanged scope.
1. Is the ask a number ("10 percent off") or a budget ("we have X, total")?
   - A budget → Build the scope that honestly fits X and present it as the Good tier, full tiers visible. Exit.
   - A number → Continue to 2.
2. Ask what is driving it, once, and listen: budget ceiling, procurement policy, comparison quote, or testing.
   - Comparison quote → Do not chase it. Walk the deliverables list, point to what the comparison likely excludes (rounds, production support, source files), and offer the matching reduced scope at the matching price. Exit.
   - Procurement policy → Go to Tree 4 step 3.
   - Testing → Hold the rate. Offer a scope trade. Exit.
3. Offer the trade menu: fewer SKUs in phase 1 (rest as a priced phase 2), fewer revision rounds, no production liaison, longer timeline (removes any rush line), source files removed if they were in. Each trade has a price effect. The rate does not move.
4. If a non-scope concession is genuinely needed to close, trade it, never give it: faster payment terms, deposit raised, case study permission, a phase 2 commitment, an introduction. Get the trade in the same document.
5. If the client insists on rate movement with no trade, the decision is the founder's, not the sender's. Escalate with the numbers and a recommendation. Exit.

## Tree 5. Source file buyout demand

START: The client asks for native source files.
1. Was source file inclusion agreed in the SOW?
   - YES, included or priced → Supply exactly what was agreed, on the agreed payment condition (usually full payment received). Check third party licences (fonts, stock, plugins) and list what the client must license themselves. Exit.
   - NO → Continue.
2. What do they actually need them for?
   - In-house team will extend the range → Price the buyout from the rate sheet basis (percentage of design fee or per family fee), plus a handover session at day rate, plus the licence caveat list. Exit via written agreement (drafting aid, counsel review).
   - Another studio or freelancer will continue → Same pricing. Do not punish the choice, and do not discount out of disappointment. Offer to remain available for guidelines or review work. Exit.
   - "Just in case" archival → Offer the cheaper honest alternative: final production files plus a full set of print ready PDFs and a documented file archive list, at no or small cost, with source files priced if ever needed later. Many clients accept once they see the difference. Exit.
   - They claim ownership as a right ("we paid for the design") → Stay calm, point to the SOW files clause agreed at signing, offer the priced route, and loop in counsel if they press a legal claim. Do not argue law you are not qualified to argue. Exit.
3. In all branches: never hand over source files against an unpaid final invoice. Files move on payment, as the SOW states.
