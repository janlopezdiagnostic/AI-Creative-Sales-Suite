# Reply Triage Template

Fill in one row per reply, on the day the reply arrives. Any reply stops the follow-up sequence first; triage decides what happens instead. The assistant drafts replies and log entries; the human reviews and sends every reply. Nothing here is automated, and no reply category licenses inventing facts, results, or permissions to rescue a conversation.

## Categories (use exactly these labels)

- **Positive:** interest, a meeting accepted, a request for the relevant example or a call.
- **Brush-off:** a polite deflection that is not a yes. Name the type in the row: "send portfolio", "we have an agency", "not now" with no date, "too expensive" with no conversation, or other (describe it).
- **Hostile:** anger, a spam complaint, a threat to report, or an instruction never to contact again. A "remove me" or unsubscribe is logged here for handling purposes and also triggers the opt-out stop rule immediately.
- **Referral:** the replier points to another person, inside or outside the brand, as the right contact.
- **Out of office:** an auto-reply or a human reply saying the buyer is away, has left, or is on leave.

## Draft reply slot

Budget: under 80 words for most categories, under 50 for hostile and brush-off replies. Shorter is almost always better after a no.

> [Draft reply here. One idea only. No new claims: anything about capability or results must trace to the brief or the case study library, at or below its permission level.]

## Category playbooks (one line each, detail lives in edge-cases.md)

- **Positive:** Reply fast, offer two concrete times, attach nothing unless they asked for the specific example, and name that example when you send it.
- **Brush-off, "send portfolio":** Send one matched example, not the portfolio: the single library entry the opener cited, in its permitted form, with one line on why it matches their situation.
- **Brush-off, "we have an agency":** Acknowledge it without disparaging the incumbent, and narrow to work an AOR commonly does not hold (extensions, regional adaptation, seasonal) only if the brief supports that gap.
- **Brush-off, "not now":** Thank them, ask one question only if it is genuinely open (what would change the timing), otherwise close politely and set the watchlist review trigger from the brief.
- **Hostile:** One short apology, confirm removal, log the opt-out, stop. No justification, no second message, no drafting around it.
- **Referral:** Thank the referrer, ask permission to use their name with the new contact, and route the new contact through a fresh brief check before any outreach.
- **Out of office:** If they are away, pause and restart on the stated return date only if the trigger is still live. If they have left the company, treat it as a buyer-change case and re-brief the account.

## Weekly sweep

Once a week, check: every reply from the last seven days has a row, every stopped sequence is actually stopped in the sending tool the human uses, and every opt-out appears in the CRM. An untriaged reply older than two business days is a process failure, not a backlog.
