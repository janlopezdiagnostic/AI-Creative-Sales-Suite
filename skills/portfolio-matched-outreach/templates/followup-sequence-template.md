# Follow-Up Sequence Template: Three Bumps

A sequence is three bumps maximum after the opener. Each bump exists only because it adds new value the buyer did not have before. A bump with no new value is not sent: "just bumping", "circling back", and "floating this to the top of your inbox" are not value, and a sequence built on them is stopped, not padded out.

All bumps are drafts only. The human reviews and sends each one. The assistant never sends, schedules, or enrols anyone in an automated sequence.

## Bump 1: Teardown insight

- **Send window:** [4 to 5 business days after the opener, unless a reply or an opt-out arrived first]
- **New value added (required):** [One specific, true observation about their current range, shelf presence, or pack architecture that the seller can stand behind. Source it: shelf photo, range page, or retailer listing, with date.]
- **Draft slot (40 to 70 words):**

> Following up with something specific: [the observation, in one or two sentences]. [One line on what it usually costs or risks if left alone, only if evidenced.] Happy to share the one-page teardown note if useful. [CASE STUDY: entry ID] is the closest example of how we handled the same pattern.

- **Do not send if:** [The observation turns out to be wrong or already fixed / a reply arrived / the buyer opted out]

## Bump 2: Relevant news

- **Send window:** [5 to 7 business days after Bump 1]
- **New value added (required):** [News the buyer plausibly has not connected to their range yet: a dated retailer reset announcement, a labelling or EPR rule change covering their formats, a competitor move reported in trade press. Source and date required.]
- **Draft slot (40 to 70 words):**

> One thing worth flagging: [the news, source, date]. For [Brand] that likely means [the concrete packaging implication, stated as an implication, not a certainty]. We handled the same implication on [CASE STUDY: entry ID, named only within its permission level]. Want the short note on how we sequenced it?

- **Do not send if:** [The news does not actually cover their formats or market / the implication is speculation presented as fact / any stop rule has fired]

## Bump 3: Seasonal hook or break-up

- **Send window:** [7 to 10 business days after Bump 2]
- **New value added (required):** [Either a real seasonal anchor (the next reset, seasonal range, or planning cycle, with its date) or an honest close: one line that makes replying easy and makes silence a clean ending.]
- **Draft slot (40 to 70 words):**

> Last note from me on this. [Seasonal anchor with date, if used: for example, seasonal artwork usually locks two to three quarters before shelf, so a spring range decision lands around (month).] If the timing is wrong, say the word and I will close the file until [the next named trigger]. If it is live, a 20 minute call is the next step.

- **Do not send if:** [No real seasonal anchor exists and the break-up would repeat Bump 2 / any stop rule has fired]

## Stop rules (apply to the whole sequence, no exceptions)

- Any reply, of any tone, stops the sequence. Replies are handled through the reply triage template, not by sending the next bump.
- An unsubscribe, opt-out, or "remove me" stops everything immediately, is logged in the CRM the same day, and no further draft is prepared for that contact.
- An out-of-office reply pauses the sequence. Restart only when the buyer is back, and only if the original trigger is still live (see edge cases).
- A conflict or do-not-contact flag discovered at any point stops the sequence, even mid-draft.
- Three bumps with no reply is the end. The account returns to the watchlist with a review trigger named in the research brief. No fourth bump is drafted to "try once more".
