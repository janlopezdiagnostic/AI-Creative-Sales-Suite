# Warm Intro Ask Template

Use this when the research brief names a mutual connection between the studio and the target buyer. A warm intro is a favour, so the ask is built to cost the connector almost nothing: they read one short message, forward one paragraph, and are done. Everything below is drafted by the assistant and sent, if at all, by the human. The skill never contacts the connector or the buyer directly.

## Part 1: The ask to the mutual connection (short message or email)

Budget: 100 to 140 words. Send through whatever channel the user already talks to this person on. Do not open a new channel just for the ask.

Placeholders:

- [CONNECTOR: first name]
- [BUYER: name and role at the target brand, as sourced in the brief]
- [SIGNAL: what changed at the target, source and date]
- [CASE STUDY: library entry ID, named only within its permission level]

> Hi [CONNECTOR], quick favour to ask, and a genuine no is completely fine.
>
> I am hoping to reach [BUYER] at [Brand]. The reason is specific: [SIGNAL]. We have done the comparable work [CASE STUDY, short form] and I think there is a real fit, which the paragraph below explains in a form you can forward as it stands.
>
> If you are comfortable forwarding it, thank you. If the timing or the relationship makes it awkward, no explanation needed and no follow-up from me on it.

Rules for Part 1:

- Name the real reason for the intro. "Networking" or "picking their brains" is not a reason, and connectors can tell.
- One ask, one buyer, one brand. Never bundle two intros into one message.
- Never offer the connector anything in return beyond thanks, and never imply the buyer has agreed to anything.

## Part 2: The forwardable blurb (connector forwards this verbatim)

Budget: one paragraph, 60 to 90 words. Written in the studio's voice, addressed to the buyer, and complete on its own: if the connector adds nothing but "passing this on", the blurb still works.

> Hi [BUYER first name], [Studio name] is a [positioning in a few words, from the fact sheet] studio. We are reaching out because [SIGNAL: the specific trigger, short form]. For [comparable client description, CASE STUDY ID, permission respected] we [the work, format and scale], and [approved outcome wording, or omit]. If [the specific work at Brand] is live, a 20 minute call would be the next step. Contact: [seller name, email, as the user supplies them].

## Part 3: The easy-out (include in Part 1, never buried)

One line, verbatim or close to it:

> If you would rather not, just say so and that is the end of it, no hard feelings and no chasing.

An intro ask without an easy-out is pressure, and pressure is how connectors stop answering. The easy-out is not decoration: if the connector uses it, the matter is closed (see edge cases) and the account goes back to the cold or watchlist route from the brief.

## Part 4: Follow-through (send after the connector acts, either way)

Budget: under 60 words. Send within two business days of learning the outcome.

If the intro was made:

> Thanks again for forwarding that, [CONNECTOR]. We are speaking with [BUYER] on [date, if set]. I will let you know how it lands, briefly, and only once. Appreciate it.

If the intro was declined, or nothing came of it:

> Thanks for considering it, [CONNECTOR]. No further asks from me on this one. I will mention it only if there is ever a result worth reporting back.

Rules for Part 4:

- Keep the connector posted exactly once on the outcome, as promised. Silence after a favour reads as extraction.
- Never report back anything confidential from the buyer conversation. One line on status, nothing on budget, internal politics, or names beyond what the connector already knows.
- Log the ask, the outcome, and the follow-through date in the CRM (drafted by the assistant, confirmed by the human before any connected-mode write).

## Filled example snippet (all names fictional)

**Part 2 blurb, for Northbeam Pantry (fictional):**

> Hi Priya, Studio Northlight (fictional) is a packaging studio known for range architecture in food and drink. We are reaching out because Northbeam Pantry announced an 8-SKU autumn extension (trade press, 2026-09-08), and extensions are where tiering usually breaks. For a comparable pantry brand [CASE STUDY: CS-07, anonymised] we rebuilt tiering across 18 SKUs, and the range kept its full listing through its retailer review. If the extension artwork is still open, a 20 minute call would be the next step. Contact: Alex, Studio Northlight.
