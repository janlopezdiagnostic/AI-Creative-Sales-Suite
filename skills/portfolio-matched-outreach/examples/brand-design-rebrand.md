# Example 4: Brand Design, Rebrand Signal, Founder Buyer

All brands, studios, and people in this example are fictional and invented for training. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended.

**Scenario:** A brand design and identity studio, Common Practice (fictional), wants outreach for Northlight Home (fictional), a direct-to-consumer homeware brand that has just hired its first brand lead and signalled a rebrand.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Northlight hired a brand lead, ex agency, and their CEO basically said rebrand in a podcast. Perfect for us, our identity work for founders is our best stuff. Write the set, founder to founder tone, do not sound corporate."

Plus pasted material: the research brief (condensed): Recommendation PURSUE NOW. Signals: first Head of Brand appointed, Lena Ortiz (fictional), started 2026-09-01, external hire from an agency (company announcement, 2026-09-01, confirmed); CEO discussed refreshing the brand for retail expansion in a podcast interview (2026-10-05, reported, single source, wording was directional, not a formal rebrand announcement). Hypothesis: the brand will scope identity and system work in the next one to two quarters as it prepares for retail. Buyer: Founder and CEO, Daniel North (fictional), is the decision maker, with the new Head of Brand as the working contact. Brief grades buyer as FOUNDER.

Plus the case-study library (condensed): Entry A, full identity and brand system for a founder-led consumer brand, category: consumer goods, format: identity, guidelines, packaging system, buyer type: founder, outcome: new identity launched and rolled out across DTC and retail, permission: named client and launch citable. Entry B, identity refresh for a founder-led food brand, buyer type: founder, outcome: refresh shipped, permission: named client citable in general terms. Entry C, corporate identity for a services firm, buyer type: marketing director, permission: citable, but buyer and category mismatch, not used.

## Reasoning

- Match scoring, on the six axes in the playbook (each 0 to 3, total out of 18), scored on Entry A: Category proximity 2, consumer goods, adjacent to homeware rather than identical. Format and deliverable match 3, identity and system work is exactly the hypothesised need. Buyer-type match 3, Entry A was bought by a founder, matching the founder buyer in the brief. Outcome relevance 2, Entry A's identity launched and rolled out across DTC and retail and that is citable, but no retail performance result is recorded. Recency 2, no completion date is recorded in the condensed entry. Proof asset quality 2, the system work exists to show, though its assets are not itemised in the entry. Total 14 of 18, grade Strong, at the bottom of the band.
- Overall: the match grades Strong, with honest caution on the trigger. The leadership signal is strong and recent, but the rebrand language itself is reported and directional, not confirmed. The copy must therefore not state a rebrand as fact. It can cite the confirmed hire and reference the retail expansion context as reported, without upgrading the podcast comment into an announcement.
- Tone decision: founder buyers respond to direct, peer-level copy and penalise corporate process language. The set below is plain, short, and specific. No flattery about the brand, no vision talk, no deck offer in the opener.
- Channel decision: email to the founder, with the Head of Brand named in reasoning as the follow-up contact if the founder forwards it. The ask is a short conversation, not a credentials presentation.

## Output: outreach set for human review (drafts only, human sends)

**Email opener (72 words)**
Subject: Northlight brand, next phase

Hi Daniel,

Noted Lena Ortiz joining as first Head of Brand in September, alongside your comments about preparing the brand for retail. A first brand hire usually means identity and system decisions that have sat with the founder now need to scale.

We built a full identity and system for a founder-led consumer brand that launched across DTC and retail.

Worth a 20-minute conversation about what retail readiness tends to surface first?

Best,
Jonas Feld, Founder, Common Practice

**Bump 1, day 5: teardown insight**
Subject: One retail readiness note

Hi Daniel, one pattern from identity work ahead of retail: packaging and shelf presence expose every inconsistency in a DTC-first system, type scale, colour, and naming, all at once. In the Entry A rollout, we audited the system against a retail fixture early, before the retail launch. Happy to share what that audit covers, in one page.

Note for the human sender: in the sent version, replace the words Entry A with the actual case-study client name as permitted by the library. The draft above keeps the placeholder so the permission check stays visible in review.

**Bump 2, day 12: new signal**
Subject: Retail timing

Hi Daniel, [NEW SIGNAL: what has changed since the opener, with its source and date, goes here]. If identity decisions are being sequenced now, the order matters: architecture and naming before artwork, or artwork gets rebuilt. A short sequencing chat may be useful even if you work with another studio.

Note for the human sender: a follow-up may only use a signal the seller actually has, sourced and dated the way a brief signal is. The brief for this account holds no second signal, so this bump is drafted around a placeholder. Put a real signal in before sending, or skip this bump and go to Bump 3.

**Bump 3, day 20: clean break-up**
Subject: Should I step back?

Hi Daniel, last note from me. If the brand work is already scoped with someone, understood. If it is still open and a founder-bought identity system is a useful reference point, we are a short call away. Either way, the first brand hire is a good sign, and I wish you well with the retail step.

## Critique

What this example does right: it matches on the axis that matters most for this buyer, which is buyer type, and it handles a reported signal honestly instead of announcing a rebrand the brand has not announced. The founder tone is direct without being familiar. What to watch: founder outreach fails when it criticises the current brand to manufacture need. This set names a scaling problem, not a quality problem, and that distinction matters. Also watch the placeholder handling in bump 1: any draft that reaches review with a library label still in the copy must be fixed by the human before sending, never sent as is.
