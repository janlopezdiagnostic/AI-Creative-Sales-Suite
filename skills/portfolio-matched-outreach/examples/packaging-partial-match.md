# Example 2: Packaging, Partial Match Handled Honestly

All brands, studios, and people in this example are fictional and invented for training. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio, Fieldnote Studio (fictional), wants outreach for Bramblewick Foods (fictional), a chilled ready-meals brand that needs artwork-only support. The studio's strongest proof is in beverages and structural design, not in this exact category or task.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Bramblewick looks perfect, they are growing fast and their packs look tired. We have beverage work that won shelf standout and structural work everyone loves, surely that transfers? Write the strongest version, do not undersell us."

Plus pasted material: the research brief (condensed): Recommendation PURSUE, with a caution. Signals: Bramblewick will add 4 chilled ready-meal SKUs for an autumn retailer reset (retailer range review note, 2026-09-22, confirmed); current packs date from 2022 (brand site, observed). Hypothesis: the brand needs fast, accurate artwork extensions within its existing system, not a redesign. Buyer: Brand Manager, Tom Ellery (fictional), brand buyer role. No structural change is mentioned in any source.

Plus the case-study library (condensed): Entry A, beverage cans redesign, 8 SKUs, category: beverages, format: cans, buyer type: founder, outcome: shelf standout work, permission: named client citable, no sales outcome citable. Entry B, structural packaging for a snacks brand, format: carton structure and dieline, buyer type: brand director, outcome: structure shipped, permission: named client citable in general terms. Entry C, chilled food artwork, 2 SKUs for a small deli client, format: sleeves, buyer type: brand manager, outcome: artwork delivered on deadline, permission: work describable, client name not citable. No ready-meals case study exists.

## Reasoning

- Match scoring, on the six axes in the playbook (each 0 to 3, total out of 18), scored on Entry C, the only honestly adjacent entry: Category proximity 2, chilled deli food sits in the same fixture as chilled ready meals, though it is not the same subcategory. Format and deliverable match 2, Entry C is sleeve artwork for chilled food, which is the artwork-only kind of work the brief asks for; Entries A and B score lower on this axis because they are a redesign and a structural job. Buyer-type match 3, Entry C was bought by a brand manager, Tom Ellery's role. Outcome relevance 1, Entry C's recorded outcome is only that artwork was delivered on deadline, with no result a buyer can weigh. Recency 1, no completion date is recorded for Entry C. Proof asset quality 1, the work is describable only, the client is not citable, and no shareable assets are recorded. Total 10 of 18.
- Overall: partial match (9 to 13). This is not a do-not-send case, because Entry C is honestly adjacent and citable within its limits, but it is not a strong-match case either. The user instruction to not undersell is noted and set aside where it conflicts with the library: stretched claims are not allowed.
- Handling rules applied: lead with observation, not proof. Soften proof to exactly what the library supports. Say what the work was and was not. Do not claim beverage shelf results transfer to ready meals, do not imply structural expertise solves an artwork-only need, and do not name the Entry C client.
- Channel decision: email, observation-led. The opener must earn attention from the quality of the observation because the proof line alone will not.

## Output: outreach set for human review (drafts only, human sends)

**Email opener (79 words)**
Subject: Bramblewick autumn SKUs

Hi Tom,

Noted the 4 new chilled SKUs landing for the autumn reset (retailer note, September). Adding SKUs inside a 2022 system is usually an artwork discipline problem: holding range architecture and blocking tight while the new variants slot in, under a fixed lock date.

Our closest work is chilled sleeve artwork delivered on deadline for a deli brand, plus broader range systems in other categories.

Worth a short call to see if that artwork experience is useful here?

Thanks,
Alex Mercer, Founder, Fieldnote Studio

**Bump 1, day 5: teardown insight**
Subject: A note on adding SKUs to an older system

Hi Tom, one pattern from artwork extension work: the risk is rarely the new pack, it is drift in the old ones when files are rebuilt under time pressure. A quick architecture check, colour bands, tiering, and variant order, before artwork starts tends to prevent that. Happy to share a one-page checklist we use for extensions, no obligation.

**Bump 2, day 11: relevant news**
Subject: Autumn reset and artwork lock

Hi Tom, [NEW SIGNAL: what has changed since the opener, with its source and date, goes here]. If a second check on files before lock would help, we can do that as a small, standalone piece.

Note for the human sender: a follow-up may only use a signal the seller actually has, sourced and dated the way a brief signal is. The brief for this account holds no second signal, so this bump is drafted around a placeholder. Put a real signal in before sending, or skip this bump and go to Bump 3.

**Bump 3, day 19: seasonal hook and break-up**
Subject: Closing the loop before autumn

Hi Tom, I will leave it here. If the autumn SKUs are covered, that is the main thing. If a future extension needs artwork support inside an existing system, that narrow job is the one our chilled sleeve work actually speaks to. Happy to be considered then, and good luck with the reset.

## Critique

What this example does right: it refuses the user's invitation to oversell, names the adjacency plainly in reasoning, and writes copy a buyer can trust because it does not pretend beverages equal ready meals. Entry C is used within its permission limit, describable but unnamed. What to watch: partial-match outreach fails when the softened proof still smuggles in an implication, for example calling structural work relevant to an artwork-only brief. If no honestly adjacent entry had existed at all, this set should not have been written and the do-not-send route would apply instead.
