# Example 3: Fashion, Seasonal Moment, LinkedIn as Primary Channel

All brands, studios, and people in this example are fictional and invented for training. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended.

**Scenario:** A creative studio, Atelier North (fictional), wants outreach for a fashion label, Solene Studio (fictional, a label name, not the creative studio), ahead of a seasonal wholesale moment. The Account Research Brief recommends PURSUE NOW with a relationship-opening angle.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Solene just showed, wholesale is expanding, they need lookbooks for AW selling. Our fashion work is strong. Email feels wrong, founder lives on LinkedIn and Instagram. Can we do LinkedIn properly, not a salesy InMail essay?"

Plus pasted material: the research brief (condensed): Recommendation PURSUE NOW (relationship-opening only). Signals: two department store accounts start with the AW27 season (founder interview, trade press, 2026-09-18, confirmed); SS27 collection shown at fashion week (coverage, 2026-09-30, confirmed). Hypothesis: the label needs wholesale selling materials and a campaign that works in partner channels, briefed ahead of the AW27 selling window early next year. Buyer: Founder, Mara Solene (fictional), founder buyer.

Plus the case-study library (condensed): Entry A, lookbook and campaign for a womenswear label at an adjacent tier, category: fashion, format: lookbook, line sheets, campaign assets, buyer type: founder, outcome: wholesale materials shipped for a selling season, permission: named client and scope citable, no sales outcome citable. Entry B, retail packaging for the same label, shopping bags and boxes, permission: describable only. Entry C, beauty campaign, category mismatch, not used.

## Reasoning

- Match scoring: Category match 3, Entry A is fashion at an adjacent tier. Format match 3, lookbook and line sheets are exactly the AW27 wholesale need in the brief. Buyer match 3, founder to founder pattern matches Entry A. Timing match 3, the brief names the AW27 window explicitly; SS27 is closed and is not pitched.
- Channel decision: LinkedIn DM is primary. Reasoning: the founder is publicly active there, the brief supports a relationship opener rather than a formal pitch, and the user observation about channel fit is consistent with the sources. Email is not used as primary here. All follow-ups stay on LinkedIn and stay short, because long DMs read as pitches on that channel.
- Discipline adapted to channel: opener still follows trigger, why it matters, one proof line, one ask, but in about 60 words. Each follow-up is two to three sentences maximum and still adds new value. No connection-request note that pitches; the pitch lives only after connection.
- Traceability: every claim traces to the brief or Entry A. No wholesale growth numbers are invented, no department store names are added, and Entry B is mentioned only in describable terms if needed, which the set below does not need.

## Output: outreach set for human review (drafts only, human sends)

**LinkedIn connection note (no pitch)**
Hi Mara, I follow Solene Studio and the wholesale expansion story with interest. I run Atelier North, we make lookbooks and campaigns for independent labels. Would value connecting ahead of the AW season.

**LinkedIn DM opener, on connection (61 words)**
Hi Mara, thanks for connecting. Saw the interview on the two department store accounts starting with AW27 (September). Wholesale lookbooks have a harder job than consumer ones: buyers skim, so line sheets and imagery have to sell the range fast. We shipped lookbook and campaign materials for a label at a similar tier for its selling season. Open to a brief chat before AW27 briefing starts?

**Follow-up 1, day 5: teardown insight, LinkedIn**
Quick note from that lookbook work: the spreads buyers actually used were the simplest ones, one look, clear fabric and price architecture, no lifestyle clutter. Happy to send a two-page sample spread if a concrete reference helps more than a deck.

**Follow-up 2, day 12: new signal, LinkedIn**
Noticed one of the department store partners published its AW27 buying calendar this week, which tends to pull wholesale materials deadlines forward. If your lookbook timing has shifted too, a short planning chat now may be worth it.

**Follow-up 3, day 20: seasonal hook and clean break-up, LinkedIn**
I will leave it here so I am not filling your inbox before market week. If AW27 materials are covered, all good. If a lookbook or line sheet need opens up this cycle, that specific work is our strongest fashion proof. Good luck with the season.

## Critique

What this example does right: it respects the channel instead of pasting an email into LinkedIn, keeps every message short enough to read on a phone, and still preserves the follow-up logic: insight, new signal, seasonal break-up. It also respects the calendar: it pitches AW27, not the already closed SS27. What to watch: LinkedIn tempts writers into flattery openers about loving a collection. That is banned here for the same reason as in email. The trigger must stay factual and sourced, even in 60 words, and any claim the brief does not support stays out however friendly the channel feels.
