# Example 6: Do Not Send, Pursue-Worthy Brief, No Honest Proof

All brands, studios, and people in this example are fictional and invented for training. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio, Fieldnote Studio (fictional), has a strong research brief for a target brand, but its case-study library contains no matching or honestly adjacent work it is allowed to cite. The user is pushing to send anyway.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Brief on Verdant Baby Foods is pursue now, signals are great, new range, new buyer. Our library is thin there but we have done food, we have done kids products separately, and the baby food case from two years ago, can we not just combine them into a story? Just write something confident, we need pipeline."

Plus pasted material: the research brief (condensed): Recommendation PURSUE NOW on signals alone. Signals: Verdant Baby Foods (fictional) will launch a 7-SKU organic toddler meals range into two national retailers in summer 2027 (trade press, 2026-10-15, confirmed); new Brand Director, Claire Dubois (fictional), started 2026-08-20 (company announcement, confirmed). Hypothesis: the brand needs a packaging system for a regulated, trust-sensitive category it is entering for the first time. Buyer: Brand Director, brand buyer.

Plus the case-study library (condensed): Entry A, adult ready-meals packaging, category: food, format: trays and sleeves, buyer type: brand manager, permission: named client citable. Entry B, children's toy packaging, category: toys, format: cartons, buyer type: founder, permission: named client citable. Entry C, baby food packaging concept work for a startup, category: baby food, format: pouches, buyer type: founder, outcome: concept only, never shipped, never launched, permission: INTERNAL ONLY, must not be cited externally in any form. No shipped baby food or toddler food case study exists. No regulated infant or toddler food experience is documented.

## Reasoning

- Brief check: the brief is genuinely pursue-worthy. Two confirmed signals, a dated launch, a new buyer in the receptivity window, a clear hypothesis. On signals alone, this looks like Example 1. That is exactly why this example exists: signal strength does not create proof.
- Match scoring: Category match 0 for citable work. Entry C is the only same-category entry and it is internal only and unshipped, so it scores zero for outreach purposes. Format match 1, trays, sleeves, and cartons are not pouches for toddler meals. Buyer match 2 on Entry A only, which does not rescue a category failure in a trust-sensitive, regulated category. Task match 0, neither citable entry involved regulated food claims, age grading, or retailer trust requirements for this category.
- The user proposal to combine food plus kids into a baby food story is a stretched claim and is refused. Combining two adjacencies into an implied specialism the studio does not have is fabrication by assembly. In this category the risk is higher, not lower: a buyer who discovers the gap in a first meeting will not rebook.
- Permission check is decisive on its own: internal-only means internal only. Referencing Entry C obliquely, for example as work for a baby food startup we cannot name, still breaches the permission and still cites an unshipped concept as proof.
- Decision: DO NOT SEND. No outreach copy is produced in this example, deliberately. Producing even a soft draft would invite the human to send it under pipeline pressure.

## Output: do-not-send memo (no outreach copy)

**Decision: DO NOT SEND outreach to Verdant Baby Foods (fictional) at this time.**

Reason, in one line: the brief is strong and the proof is absent, and in a regulated, trust-sensitive category an observation-led opener without honest proof damages credibility more than silence does.

**Options, in order of preference:**

1. Build proof first. Seek a smaller, honestly matched entry point in an adjacent, citable category the studio can win and ship, for example an adult food extension where Entry A is a true match, and revisit baby and toddler food only after shipped, citable work exists. Timeline: quarters, not weeks.
2. Wait for a better trigger and a better angle. Stay on watchlist for this account, monitor for a need that matches citable work, such as a non-food brand or gifting format where Entry B is honestly relevant. No outreach until the match changes.
3. Walk away from this launch window. Accept that summer 2027 toddler meals will be designed by a studio with shipped category proof, log the loss of window as a library gap, and direct pipeline effort to accounts where the library already matches.

**What gets logged:** account name, brief date and recommendation, match scores as above, the internal-only status of Entry C, the decision and date, the user push to send and the reason it was declined, and a review trigger: new shipped, citable case study in food for children, or a materially different need at the account. The account goes to the signal log as DO NOT CONTACT until the review trigger fires.

**What the human should do this week instead:** pick two accounts from the pipeline where category, format, and buyer all score 2 or above on citable work, and build outreach sets for those.

## Critique

What this example does right: it separates two judgements the user had merged, is the account attractive (yes) and can we credibly write to it (no), and it gives the human useful next actions instead of just a refusal. The logging step matters: without it, the same account will be re-pitched internally in a month with the same thin library. What to watch: pipeline pressure is the normal cause of generic outreach. Generic outreach to a well-researched account is worse than no outreach, because it burns a named buyer who may be right for the studio later, once real proof exists.
