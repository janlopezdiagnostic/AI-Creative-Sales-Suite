# Example 7: Recovery, Rewriting a Weak First-Draft Opener

All brands, studios, and people in this example are fictional and invented for training. Any resemblance to real companies is unintended.

**Scenario:** A packaging studio, Fieldnote Studio (fictional), has a good brief and a good library match for a target, but the user's own first-draft opener breaks most of the opener rules. This example diagnoses the draft and rewrites the full set.

## Raw input (as received, messy)

> "Target is Juniper Table Sauces, brief says pursue, library has the hot sauce work. I drafted an opener, can you polish it? Make it sound premium. Draft below, I think it just needs tightening."

Plus pasted material: the research brief (condensed): Recommendation PURSUE. Signals: Juniper Table Sauces (fictional) will launch a 4-SKU limited hot sauce range for a summer retailer feature (retailer feature list, 2026-10-08, confirmed). Buyer: Head of Brand, Alex Rowe (fictional), brand buyer. Hypothesis: the brand needs limited-edition packaging that stands apart from the core range without breaking its architecture, on a fixed feature date.

Plus the case-study library (condensed): Entry A, hot sauce limited edition, 3 SKUs, category: hot sauces, format: bottles and labels, buyer type: head of brand, outcome: range shipped for a retailer feature, permission: named client citable, outcome citable only as shipped for the feature, no sales figures citable. Entry B, core sauce range, 18 SKUs, permission: named client citable in general terms.

Plus the user's first draft (quoted in full, 115 words):

> "Hi Alex, I hope you are well! I have long admired Juniper Table and your incredible, visionary work building such an iconic, beloved brand, truly one of the most exciting brands in food right now. We are an award-winning, full-service, world-class packaging studio passionate about helping ambitious brands win, and we increased sales by 47 percent for our last hot sauce client and would love to do the same for you. Our capabilities include strategy, structural, artwork, repro, and much more, and I have attached our full 40-page deck. Could we have a quick 45-minute intro call next Tuesday at 10 or Wednesday at 2 to run through everything we do? Looking forward to connecting!"

## Reasoning

- Diagnosis, flaw by flaw against the rules. One, flattery opener: admired, incredible, visionary, iconic, beloved. Banned, and it tells the buyer nothing. Two, no trigger: the draft never mentions the summer retailer feature, the 4-SKU limited range, or any dated signal from the brief, so it could be sent to anyone. Three, over-claim: increased sales by 47 percent appears nowhere in the library. Entry A permits only shipped for the feature. The figure is invented for outreach purposes and must be removed, not softened. Four, AI slop and capability listing: award-winning, full-service, world-class, passionate about helping ambitious brands win, plus a five-item capabilities list. No specifics, no proof. Five, length and ask: 115 words, over the 100-word limit, with a heavy ask, a 45-minute call, two forced times, and a 40-page deck attachment the buyer never requested. Six, no matched proof line in permitted form: the real proof, a 3-SKU hot sauce limited edition shipped for a retailer feature for a head of brand, is present in the library and absent from the draft.
- Match check (for the rewrite), on the six axes in the playbook (each 0 to 3, total out of 18), scored on Entry A: Category proximity 3, Format and deliverable match 3, Buyer-type match 3, Outcome relevance 2 (the range shipped for a retailer feature, citable only in those words, with no figures), Recency 2 (no completion date is recorded in the condensed entry), Proof asset quality 2 (assets are not itemised in the entry). Total 15 of 18, grade Strong. The underlying opportunity is strong. The draft was the only weak element, which is why this is a rewrite case and not a do-not-send case.
- Handling of the over-claim: replaced with the exact permitted wording, shipped for a retailer feature. If the human wants a sales outcome in copy, it must be added to the library first with a source and a permission level, then cited. Until then it is either omitted or marked [TO CONFIRM] and held from sending. In the set below it is omitted, because the shipped-for-feature proof is strong enough alone.

## Output: outreach set for human review (drafts only, human sends)

**Email opener, rewritten (74 words)**
Subject: Juniper summer feature range

Hi Alex,

Saw Juniper Table on the retailer feature list for a 4-SKU limited hot sauce range this summer (October list). Limited ranges have to stand apart from the core packs without breaking the architecture buyers already recognise, on a date that does not move.

We designed a 3-SKU hot sauce limited edition, for a head of brand, that shipped for a retailer feature.

Worth a 20-minute call to compare notes on that balance?

Thanks,
Alex Mercer, Founder, Fieldnote Studio

**Bump 1, day 4: teardown insight**
Subject: Limited editions and the core range

Hi Alex, one note from the limited-edition work: the shipped range kept one core architecture element completely unchanged, usually the label structure, and let colour and finish do all the limited work. Changing both at once risks the limited packs reading as a different range at the fixture. Happy to share a one-page comparison if useful.

**Bump 2, day 11: new signal**
Subject: Feature date and artwork lock

Hi Alex, [NEW SIGNAL: what has changed since the opener, with its source and date, goes here]. Limited ranges are tighter than core launches because there is no second wave to fix errors in, so sequencing finish and colour decisions early was the lesson from the shipped range above. A short sequencing conversation may help, whoever designs it.

Note for the human sender: a follow-up may only use a signal the seller actually has, sourced and dated the way a brief signal is. The brief for this account holds no second signal, so this bump is drafted around a placeholder. Put a real signal in before sending, or skip this bump and go to Bump 3.

**Bump 3, day 18: clean break-up**
Subject: Closing the file on the summer range

Hi Alex, last note. If the summer range is already in design, understood, and good luck with the feature. If it is still open, the matched limited-edition work is the right starting point for a conversation. I will close the file here either way.

## Critique

What this example does right: it names every flaw in the original draft against a specific rule, rather than calling the draft weak in general, so the human learns the standard. The rewrite proves the point that a strong library match needs fewer words, not more: the permitted proof is shorter and more convincing than the invented percentage. What to watch: polishing a weak draft without diagnosing the over-claim is the dangerous shortcut. A tightened version of the 47 percent line would still be an invented figure. Polishing does not fix that. Recovery always starts with the evidence check, then the writing.
