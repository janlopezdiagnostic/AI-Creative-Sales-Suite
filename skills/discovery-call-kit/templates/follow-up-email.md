# Template: Follow-Up Email (drafted for the seller's approval, never sent by the skill)

Send within one working day of the call. Short. Its jobs: prove you listened, lock the facts in writing, and hold the next step's date.

---

Subject: [Project name in the buyer's words] : recap and next step

Hi [name],

Thanks for the time [today/yesterday]. To make sure I captured it properly:

- What is driving this: [trigger, paraphrased from the call, in near-verbatim language].
- The shape as we discussed: [deliverables with counts, e.g. packaging for 9 core SKUs, identity excluded].
- Timing: [required date and what created it; artwork lock or next milestone as calculated].
- [Only if true and agreed: Budget/process line, e.g. "You and [name] will review the proposal; [procurement step] follows."]

Two things from my side:
[If a seller ballpark needs unwinding: "The figure I mentioned on the call assumed [scope assumption]. Please treat it as indicative only; the scoped proposal will price what we actually listed together." Otherwise omit.]
[If information is owed: "To scope this properly I still need [SKU list / dieline status / the name of X]. If you can send that by [date], I can hold the proposal date below."]

Next step as agreed: [scoped proposal / paid discovery document / second call with named attendees] by [date].

If I have misread anything above, correct me; I would rather be corrected now than quote on a misunderstanding.

[Sign-off]

---

Drafting rules the skill follows: every factual sentence must trace to the notes or transcript; nothing the buyer did not say appears in the recap (a recap that smuggles in a budget or an agreement manufactures consent, and buyers notice). No new numbers, dates, or deliverables debut in this email, except the proposal or discovery date the seller is committing to. If no next step was agreed on the call, the email proposes one specific step with a date; it does not ask "what would you like to do next?"
