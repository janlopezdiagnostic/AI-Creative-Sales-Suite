# Example 8: A Fashion Seasonal Call

All brands, people, and details in this example are fictional and invented for training.

## Input (prep request with two facts)

Independent fashion label (fictional), 40 styles a season, sells DTC and through two wholesale accounts. The founder-designer wants "packaging and unboxing that feels like the clothes" ahead of the AW season: rigid boxes, tissue, garment bags, lookbook inserts. Wholesale buyers have started asking about shelf and delivery presentation. Call is with the founder-designer and her operations manager.

## Skill output (prep highlights + likely extraction shape)

**Mode: PREP. Vertical notes applied: fashion calendars run the room. AW selling happens months before AW shipping; if this call is happening in spring, the work is feasible; in late summer, it is a rush conversation wearing a normal conversation's clothes. Confirm where in the season cycle the label sits in the first five minutes.**

**Questions that matter most for this buyer pair:** What exactly ships in what: which pieces get rigid boxes (cost per unit will kill full-range rigid quickly, tiering is the design question: hero pieces boxed, rest in garment bags)? Wholesale presentation requirements from the two accounts (their specs override taste)? Volumes per season and reorder pattern (seasonal packaging dies on minimum order quantities; a beautiful box at 300 units is a different product from the same box at 5,000)? Who prints and who holds stock of the packaging itself between seasons?

**Creative expectations, fashion dialect:** references will be other labels, not packaging case studies; ask whose unboxing made her jealous. The founder-designer judges like a designer: bring materials, not mockups; a substrate conversation (board weights, uncoated stocks, foil vs deboss) builds more trust with this buyer than any deck.

**Commercial shape to establish:** seasonal cadence means this is a retainer-shaped relationship wearing a project costume (two seasons a year, plus wholesale specials). Do not force that conversation on call one; plant it: "Most labels we work with end up wanting the seasonal cycle handled as a rhythm rather than a rescue. Let us do this season well and talk about the pattern after." Budget: founder-stated, test against unit economics: packaging spend per garment that the margin can carry is a number she will know to the cent.

**Likely verdict shape (recorded after the fictional call):** Fit 3, Budget 2 (stated range, tied to unit economics, credible), Timing 2 or 3 depending on the season answer, Access 3 (both decision halves in the room: taste and operations). Pursue to proposal if the season window allows production; paid discovery first if volumes and tiering are still fluid, because the tiering decision is the scope.

## Critique

The packaging vertical layer does quiet work here: the buyer's map still holds (founder is taste and budget in one person), but the deal shape, the calendar risk, and the proof types are fashion's, not CPG's. A core-skill-only prep would have asked about retailer resets. This one asks about minimum order quantities and wholesale presentation specs, which is what the call will actually be decided on.
