# Example 7: Recovery, a Bad First Call Salvaged

All brands, people, and details in this example are fictional and invented for training.

## Input (the damage report)

First call with a regional dairy brand (fictional) went wrong in the classic ways. The seller presented the portfolio deck for 30 of the 40 minutes, learned almost nothing, and when the buyer said "and roughly what would something like this cost?", answered "for a range like yours, usually around thirty" before catching themselves. The buyer ended warmly vague: "Send us something." The seller asks: "Have I blown it? I know nothing about their budget, their process, or even the real SKU count."

## Skill output (recovery plan)

**Mode: RECOVERY (STRUCTURE applied after the fact). Diagnosis first: not blown, but the ballpark is live ammunition and "send something" is a trapdoor. Recovery has three moves, in order, inside 48 hours.**

**Move 1: The repair email (drafted for approval).** Purpose: convert the vague ending into a real second conversation and re-anchor the number to scope before it hardens. Draft: "Thanks for the time today. I talked too much about us and learned too little about the range, so two things. First, the figure I mentioned was wrong to give you without a scope: it assumed a refresh of existing structures and a SKU count I never confirmed, and your range may be bigger than the one I priced in my head. Please treat it as withdrawn until we have counted the work properly. Second, can we take 25 minutes next week to do the part I skipped: what is driving the change, which SKUs and formats are really in play, and how a decision gets made on your side? I will send three questions in advance so it is useful even if we never work together." The withdrawal is honest, early, and cheap. Every week it waits, it gets more expensive.

**Move 2: The second call, run properly.** 25 minutes, the three questions sent in advance are the agenda: trigger and deadline driver; deliverables count (SKUs, formats, languages); decision process and who else is involved. Proof stays in the bag; it has been seen. The close is a scoped proposal date or a paid discovery, agreed on the call.

**Move 3: Score what you actually have.** Until the second call happens: Fit 2 (category plausible, proof generic), Budget 1 (the only number in play is the seller's own, recorded as seller talk per Tree 2), Timing 1 (no driver named), Access 1 (one contact, role in the process unknown). Verdict: **NURTURE-leaning, pending the second call**; the extraction records the first call as a presentation, not a discovery, so nobody upstairs mistakes it for progress.

## Critique

Recovery works here because the mistakes were process mistakes, not character ones, and because the repair is fast and candid. Two lessons the skill writes into the seller's prep for next time: the ballpark escaped because the prep pack had not pre-scripted the price-question pivot, and the deck ran long because no one had agreed the call's purpose in the first two minutes. Both are prep failures, both are fixable, and neither requires the seller to be a different person.
