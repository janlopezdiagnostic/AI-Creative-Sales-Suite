# Example 4: The Procurement-Flavoured Call

All brands, people, and details in this example are fictional and invented for training.

## Input (prep request)

The studio's first meeting with a large food group turns out to be with a procurement category manager, not the brand team whose range redesign was announced in the trade press. The seller asks: "Should I just run my normal discovery? I have 45 minutes."

## Skill output (adjusted prep, condensed)

**Mode: PREP, buyer type switched to procurement. This is not a creative discovery call. Adjust accordingly.**

**What this call is actually for:** Procurement is building or refreshing a design services panel ahead of the announced programme. Your deliverables from the 45 minutes are: (1) the process map (panel or direct award, evaluation criteria and weightings, onboarding requirements, standard terms including payment days and IP position), (2) the name and role of the business sponsor for the redesign programme, and a route to them, (3) where rate benchmarking will bite (they will compare your day rates against artwork-only suppliers if the phases are not separated; hand over rates structured by phase, strategy, structural, artwork, production support, never as one blended number).

**Do not:** present the portfolio as a deck tour (offer two relevant case studies verbally and leave the credentials document), discuss creative ideas for their brands, quote a project price for the redesign (you have no brief; any number given here will be logged as your price for it), or agree to exclusivity, most-favoured pricing, or terms on the call.

**Questions that earn their place:** "Who owns the redesign programme on the brand side, and how will they meet the studios on the panel?" "What does a studio have to be true about for you to put them forward?" "When the last panel ran, what made the business unhappy?" That last question usually produces the real evaluation criteria, unguarded.

**Close:** "The right next conversation is with [sponsor] about the programme itself. Can you introduce us, or shall I write to them and copy you?" A procurement contact who facilitates that introduction is an ally for years. One who blocks it while demanding rates is telling you the panel is a price exercise; the scorecard will say so.

**Extraction note (for after):** Access scores on the sponsor, not on procurement. Until the sponsor is met, this account's verdict cannot exceed paid discovery, whatever procurement's warmth. Budget field records the panel terms (rate structure, payment days as stated) and the project budget remains `[NOT STATED]`.

## Critique

The failure this example prevents is category error: running a taste-discovery playbook at a process buyer, and leaving with rates on file, no sponsor, and the illusion of progress. The prep accepts procurement's agenda as legitimate, extracts the commercial frame, and protects the only asset that matters, the route to the person who owns the work.
