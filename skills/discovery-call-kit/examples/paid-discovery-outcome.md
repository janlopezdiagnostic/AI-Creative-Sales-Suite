# Example 5: The Call That Should End in Paid Discovery

All brands, people, and details in this example are fictional and invented for training.

## Input (mid-call facts, seller messaging between meetings)

"Halfway through. Brand director, mid-size personal care. Range refresh is real, retailer pressure is real. But: SKU count 'somewhere between 12 and 30', no idea who prints two of the formats, budget 'will be released once we see a proposal', and she wants concepts in the proposal to 'sell it internally'. She is friendly and senior. What do I do with the last 20 minutes?"

## Skill output (live structure advice)

**Mode: STRUCTURE. Do not aim for a proposal close. Aim for a paid discovery close.**

**Why:** Three of the four qualification dimensions cannot be scored from what exists. Fit is real (refresh, right category) but scope volume is unknown by a factor of 2.5, the production chain is partly unknown, and the budget mechanism is backwards (money released by a proposal means the proposal is doing the internal selling, which is how studios end up writing business cases for free). Concepts in the proposal completes the pattern: your unpaid creative becomes her internal pitch deck.

**The close to run:** Name the situation as a compliment to the project, not a complaint about the process: "This is a real programme and I do not want to price it on guesses, because the guesses are where the surprises live. Here is what I suggest. A fixed-fee discovery, two weeks: we audit the range as it stands, confirm the SKU and format list with your supply chain team, verify the print routes for the two unknown formats, and come back with a scoped programme, real numbers, and one design territory explored so you have something true to sell internally. The fee is credited in full against the programme if it proceeds within 60 days. You get a decision-grade document whether or not you pick us."

**Key mechanics:** territory explored, not concepts for the range: one territory shows thinking without handing over the answer. Fee sized to be signable at her level (the access problem dissolves if she can approve the discovery herself; ask directly: "What can you sign without going upstairs?"). Date the discovery start on the call.

**If she refuses paid discovery but still wants concepts:** the tree is unambiguous. Offer the scoped proposal without concepts, priced on stated assumptions with the volume risk visible, or decline. Friendly and senior is exactly the profile that extracts the most free work when the system allows it.

## Extraction note (after she accepted, in this fictional case)

Verdict recorded as **PAID DISCOVERY FIRST** with the discovery scoped: range audit, volumes confirmation, print-route verification, one territory. Budget for the programme remains `[NOT STATED]` and that is now acceptable, because discovery exists to convert the unknown into a costed scope, after which the budget conversation happens against real numbers. Scores: Fit 3, Budget 1, Timing 2, Access 2 (she can sign discovery; programme signer unknown, named as a discovery task).

## Critique

The instinct this example corrects is "senior and friendly, so send the big proposal". Seniority plus vagueness is not a qualified deal; it is an expensive one. Paid discovery is not a consolation prize here, it is the product that fits the actual state of knowledge, and it gets the studio paid to learn what a proposal would have had to guess.
