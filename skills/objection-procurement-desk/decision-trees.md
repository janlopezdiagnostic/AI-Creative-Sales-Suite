# Decision Trees

Run these before improvising. Each tree ends in a defined exit. If your situation matches no branch, stop, tell the user what does not fit, and let the user decide. Rates, floors, and term limits referenced throughout live in the studio rate and rules sheet.

## Tree 1. Objection triage

START: Pushback has arrived on a live proposal. Take it in the client's order; if several objections arrive together, run this tree per objection.
1. Capture it verbatim. Who said it (taste buyer, finance, procurement, gatekeeper relaying), in what channel, with what exact wording?
2. Classify it using the playbook library: price, in-house alternative, incumbent comparison, rate benchmarking, speed, internal approval, free creative, terms, IP or files, exclusivity.
3. Diagnose the real issue. Assign one primary diagnosis and write down the evidence:
   - **Price:** a budget, a ceiling, or category norms genuinely bind.
   - **Process:** an organisational system requires something (policy reduction, panel, payment run, approval chain).
   - **Risk:** confidence is the gap; price is the respectable vocabulary for it.
   - **Politics:** someone needs a win, a defence, or a record.
   - Evidence thin or contradictory → the response's job is a diagnostic question, not a concession. Exit to Tree 1 step 5 with a question draft.
4. Authority check: is the likely response inside the authority the rate sheet gives the sender?
   - NO, or unsure → Escalate first (playbook Part 4 format), respond after the decision. Exit.
   - YES → Continue.
5. Draft the response using the playbook structure for that objection and diagnosis: acknowledge, isolate, diagnostic question if needed, trade or structure, next step. Then:
   - User reviews and confirms → send. Log the exchange the same day (Tree 5 documentation step happens for every exit of this tree). Exit.
   - The pushback does not match any library entry → Say so to the user, propose the closest structure and why, and let the user choose. Do not force a fit. Exit.

## Tree 2. Discount demand → scope trade or walk

START: The client asks for a lower price, in any phrasing: a percentage, a budget figure, a "sharpen your pencil", a finance requirement.
1. Is the ask a budget ("we have X") or a percentage on an unchanged scope?
   - A budget → Design the scope that honestly fits X, using the Scope and Pricing Architect. Present it as the Good tier with the fuller tiers visible. The rate never moves. Exit.
   - A percentage or general pressure → Continue.
2. Ask the isolating question once: "If the number worked, is everything else agreed?" and, if relevant, "Is the percentage a policy figure or a ceiling?"
   - The answer reveals a non-price blocker (risk, scope doubt, internal politics) → Return to Tree 1 with the real objection. Exit.
   - Price is genuinely the blocker → Continue.
3. Offer the trade menu: fewer SKUs or deliverables in phase 1 (rest priced as phase 2), fewer revision rounds, no production liaison, longer timeline removing any rush line, source files removed if they were in, payment timing improvements from the client side. Each trade carries its price effect from the original scope build. The rate does not move.
   - Client accepts a trade → Confirm in writing same day, reissue the scope summary with the traded structure, log it. Exit.
   - Client declines trades but names a real budget → Back to step 1, budget branch. Exit.
   - Client insists on rate movement with no trade → Continue to 4.
4. Floor check: would accepting take the deal below the founder's floor or move the rate itself?
   - YES → This is not the sender's decision. Escalate with numbers and a recommendation. Founder options: authorise a documented exception (recorded in the log with the reason), or walk (Tree 5). Exit.
   - NO, a trade within authority exists that has not been offered → Offer it. One. Not the whole menu at once. Exit.
5. If the client will accept neither structure nor trade and the founder will not authorise an exception → Tree 5. Exit.

## Tree 3. Procurement process: join or decline

START: A procurement process has appeared: panel application, RFP, framework invitation, or onboarding demand. Run the triage scorecard (templates/rfp-triage-scorecard.md) as you go.
1. Is there a brand-side sponsor: a named person inside who wants this studio and can say so?
   - NO → Is there named, dated, real work behind the process (a project, a programme, a reset), verifiable from the sponsor-less material provided?
     - NO → **Decline, warmly, now.** One paragraph: thank them, state capacity is committed to existing clients, offer to be contacted when a specific project exists. Log the decline and the reason. Exit.
     - YES → Continue, but cap the investment at the triage threshold and note the missing sponsor as the top risk.
   - YES → Continue.
2. Score the process: budget named or discoverable; access to the decision maker for questions; creative demanded at submission (a heavy negative); timeline plausibility; incumbent position; admin cost (paperwork hours, insurance upgrades, portal terms) against realistic first year value.
   - Score below the studio's threshold (set in the scorecard) → **Decline or negotiate the process.** Decline warmly as above, or, where the relationship justifies it, propose the alternative that fixes the failing line: a paid discovery instead of free creative, a meeting instead of a paper only round. If the alternative is refused, decline. Exit.
   - Score at or above threshold → Continue.
3. Before investing: state the maximum bid cost (days at rate sheet rates) the studio authorises, get founder sign-off if it exceeds the authority limit, and diary the decision date.
4. Participate inside the cap: answer what is asked, in the format asked, using deliverables language; file the rate card with its cover note; file the terms response during onboarding, not later.
   - Process demands something outside the rate sheet mid-flight (free creative beyond the cap, terms beyond limits, exclusivity) → Stop. Escalate. The options are: negotiate the demand, or withdraw cleanly before the expensive stage. Sunk cost is not a reason to continue. Exit.
   - Decision arrives: win → move to onboarding rules and Tree 4 for any terms conflicts. Loss → one follow up asking for feedback, log what is learned, close the file. Exit.

## Tree 4. Terms conflict: resolve or escalate

START: Client terms conflict with the rate sheet: payment days, kill fees, liability, IP defaults, termination, or portal terms presented as non-negotiable.
1. List every conflicting clause separately. Never negotiate "the terms" as a lump; lumps get accepted by silence.
2. For each clause, classify:
   - **Acceptable:** inside the rate sheet's limits, or a cost the studio knowingly carries for this client tier. Accept explicitly, in writing, clause by clause.
   - **Tradable:** outside preference but inside founder authority to trade for value (volume, commitment, faster elements elsewhere, deposit size). Prepare the trade; do not offer it free.
   - **Red line:** outside the sheet's stated limits (payment days beyond the cap, uncapped liability, assignment of all concepts, removal of kill protection, exclusivity). Not the sender's call under any pressure.
3. Any red line clauses present?
   - YES → Escalate the full clause list with classification and a recommendation before responding. Founder decides: documented exception, counter, or Tree 5. Exit.
   - NO → Continue.
4. Draft the terms response (templates/terms-response.md): accepts named, counters named with the studio's structure (smaller milestones, PO before start, balance before file release, pause rights), trades attached to tradable clauses.
   - Client accepts or counters inside limits → Confirm the final set in writing, all clauses, one document. Flag for counsel review if the document is contract shaped. Log it. Exit.
   - Client states "non-negotiable, portal standard" → Ask, once, for the exceptions route and who approves exceptions; large organisations almost always have one. If refused and red lines remain → Escalate; founder chooses documented exception or Tree 5. Exit.
5. Standing rule for every exit: work does not start until terms are agreed in writing and the purchase order position is clear. A terms conflict discovered at invoice stage is a documentation failure, and it is logged as one.

## Tree 5. Walk-away criteria

START: A walk-away is being considered, by either side, or Tree 2 or Tree 4 has routed here. Walking away is a decision with a structure, not a mood.
1. Confirm the trigger is real. At least one must hold:
   - The only acceptable version for the client prices below the founder's floor or moves the rate with no trade, and no exception is authorised.
   - Terms red lines stand after the exceptions route has been tried (Tree 4).
   - The process requires investment beyond the authorised cap with no sponsor (Tree 3).
   - The buyer demands confidential information (another client's terms), unpaid creative as a condition of consideration after the paid alternative was refused, or conduct that would breach the studio's own standing rules.
   - Exclusivity is demanded in a form the founder will not sign (broad perimeter, open ended, uncompensated).
   - None holds → This is frustration, not a walk-away. Return to Tree 1, re-diagnose, and respond to the actual objection. Exit.
2. Founder decision, on the record: walk, or authorise the specific exception that resolves the trigger, with its reason documented in the log. The desk never walks a deal, or saves one below the floor, on its own authority.
3. Draft the walk-away (templates/walk-away-email.md): short, warm, final. Thanks for the specific thing that was good; the reason in one honest sentence (fit of structure or terms, never blame and never a lecture); the door left genuinely open with the condition that would change the answer ("if a phase 1 at a smaller scope ever fits, we would be glad to"); no last minute discount, no sting, no over-explaining.
   - User reviews and confirms → send. Exit to 4.
4. Document and close: complete the negotiation log (the ask, the diagnosis, the trades offered, the trigger, the decision, who made it), update the CRM to closed with the reason in factual language, and set a review date if the door was left open on a real condition (a new budget cycle, a reset, a leadership change). A walked deal with a review date is pipeline; a walked deal without one is just a memory. Exit.
