# Example 6: A Negotiation That Ends in a Good Walk-Away

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Discount authority: scope trades only, AE level. Founder floor on this deal: the full scope holds its rate; scope may shrink to a stated minimum structure (strategy, masters, one round) below which the studio will not deliver its name on the work. Payment days cap 30.

## Situation, as the negotiation log shows it
Kestrel Home Goods (fictional, venture backed homewares brand, 14 SKUs of storage and kitchen products). Proposal: 21,500 for brand refresh applied to packaging, strategy, masters, adaptations, guidelines.
- Day 1: Proposal sent. Brand lead enthusiastic on a call.
- Day 5: Brand lead: Finance (meaning the lead founder) needs "a partner rate". Asked for 15 percent off, same scope. Desk responded per tree 2: isolating question confirmed everything else was agreed; trade menu offered (phase the guidelines, one round at artwork, no liaison), worth 2,900 in scope, reaching 18,600.
- Day 9: Client declined trades: "The scope is right, the price is wrong." Counter restated: full scope, 15,100 (30 percent off), net-60, and "we will be a great case study, the range is going into two national retailers".
- Day 10: Escalation brief to the founder: ask now combines a 30 percent rate cut (below floor in effect, rate movement not scope), net-60 (beyond cap), and payment in exposure (case study value). Recommendation: walk, unless the founder wants a documented exception. Founder decision, logged: no exception. The brand's structure (full scope, deep rate cut, slow payment, reputational consideration) is three red lines arriving as one offer; any one of them might have been a conversation.
- Day 10: Walk-away sent (below). Day 12: a gracious reply from the brand lead ("understand, Finance held firm"). Log closed with review date set at their next budget cycle.

## Output: the walk-away (client facing)
Subject: Kestrel packaging programme: a straight answer

[Brand lead's name],

Thank you for the speed and honesty on your side, and to [founder's name] for looking at it properly. I have taken a careful run at whether we can make your structure work, and I owe you a straight answer rather than a slow negotiation.

We cannot do the full programme at 15,100 on 60 day terms. The honest reason is arithmetic, not positioning: at that structure we would be cutting the very things that make the work succeed at retail, rounds, production care, and senior time, and shipping you a range neither of us would be proud of. A smaller scope at a fair structure is something we would genuinely enjoy, and the phased version from my earlier email stands whenever it is useful.

If next year's budget cycle gives the programme more room, I would be glad to pick this up; the thinking in the proposal was built for your range and it keeps. Wishing you and the team a strong launch into [the two retailers, named in their email]. If a second pair of eyes on the production side would ever help informally, you know where I am.

[AE name]

## Outcome (illustrative)
Nothing immediate, which is what a walk-away usually produces, plus a log entry and a review date. At the next budget cycle the brand returned with a new finance lead, a realistic budget, and the phased structure from day 5 as their own opening suggestion. The eventual project signed at full rates on standard terms. The studio is explicit with itself about the lesson: this outcome is a pleasant possibility, never the plan. The walk-away was correct on day 10 even if the phone had never rung again.

## Critique
Strengths: three red lines were treated as one decision, not haggled one by one into three slow concessions; the email's reason was structural and true; the reopening condition (budget cycle, phased structure) was real, which is why the return conversation could start from it. Internally, the escalation brief meant the founder decided in ten minutes from a page, not from a forwarded thread.
Watch outs: the offer of informal production advice at the end is a judgment call; here it was safe because the relationship was warm and the AE meant it in the small sense. On a colder account it risks reopening free consulting (edge case 2). Cut it unless it is genuinely meant and genuinely small.
