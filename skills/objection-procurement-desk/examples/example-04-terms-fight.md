# Example 4: The Terms Fight (Net-90, IP Defaults, Kill Removal)

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. All contract language discussion is drafting aid, have counsel review before use.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Deposit 50 percent, milestones 50/30/20, invoice terms 14 days. Payment days cap: 30, founder exception only with the cash cost stated. Kill fees by stage. Source files excluded, priced per family. Liability position: cap at fees paid for the project, counsel set.

## Situation
After selection by the brand team at Northgate Provisions (fictional grocery group), onboarding sends the studio the group's standard supplier terms as a portal document "for acceptance". Extracts: payment 90 days from invoice; "all work product, including concepts, drafts and preliminary materials, is assigned to the Company"; termination for convenience at any time with payment only for "satisfactory work received"; liability uncapped; and a clause granting rates "no less favourable than those offered to any other customer".

## Reasoning
Classify: terms conflict, run decision tree 4. Nothing here is answered in a chatty email; it gets a clause table.
Clause classification:
1. Net-90: red line (beyond the 30 day cap). Tradable only as a founder exception with the financing cost stated in numbers: on this project's fee profile, 90 day terms across the programme is a real, computable cost, and milestones must shrink exposure (higher deposit is not available inside their portal pattern, so: balance before file release becomes load bearing, and phased starts mean no phase begins while the prior phase's invoice ages).
2. IP assignment of concepts and drafts: red line. It converts every unused route into their property, which is the studio's working capital, and it conflicts with the SOW the brand team already agreed in substance. Counter: assignment of final approved artwork for the agreed use on full payment; concepts and working files remain studio property; source files priced as in the proposal.
3. Termination with payment only for "satisfactory work": red line. "Satisfactory" undefined means every kill is an argument. Counter: the SOW's stage based kill fees, work completed payable at the stage rates.
4. Uncapped liability: red line, escalated to founder and counsel regardless of the client's size.
5. Most favoured pricing: red line on confidentiality grounds (eval 13's rule applied to contract language): the studio cannot warrant comparisons against terms it does not disclose.
Escalation: the full clause table went to the founder before any response, with the recommendation to counter all five, accept the rest of the document explicitly, and let counsel check the counter language. Founder approved counters, declined any net-90 exception beyond 45 days with restructured milestones (a documented exception, cost stated, logged).

## Output: covering email (client facing, the clause table was attached)
"Thank you for the onboarding documents. We have completed our review and the attached response sets out our position clause by clause. In summary: the large majority of your standard terms are fine with us as drafted. Five points need adjustment, each with our reasoning and proposed wording in the attachment.
Payment: our standard is 14 days and our ceiling is 30; we can work at 45 with the milestone structure in the attachment (deposit on signing, phase payments, balance before final file release), which keeps both sides' exposure sensible. Beyond that we would be financing the programme, which a studio our size cannot honestly do.
Intellectual property: final approved artwork assigns to you on full payment, for the agreed use, which matches the proposal your brand team accepted. Concepts, drafts, and working files remain ours; that is what allows us to price the design work itself as we do. Source files for the finished range are available at the price in the proposal.
Termination and liability follow the same pattern: our stage based kill fees from the proposal, and a liability cap proportionate to the fees, both standard for work of this size and both in the attachment.
On the most favoured pricing clause: we cannot sign it, for a reason that protects you as much as us. Our pricing to every client is confidential, including yours. We will never quote your terms to another client, and so we cannot warrant comparisons against anyone else's.
Everything is drafted to be signable. If your team wants to talk any point through, I am available this week, and our respective legal contacts can close the wording quickly once the commercial points are agreed. Drafting aid on our side, as flagged in the attachment; we are ready to complete onboarding as soon as these five points land."

## Outcome (illustrative)
The group accepted the IP, kill, and liability counters with minor wording from their counsel, declined 45 days initially, then accepted them when the brand team's launch date made the alternative (a new supplier onboarding) worse than the exception. The most favoured clause was deleted and replaced with a rate card review date. Onboarding took eleven days longer than the portal's estimate and the relationship started with the terms actually agreed, which the studio's log notes is the entire point.

## Critique
Strengths: clause by clause handling meant nothing was accepted by silence, and each refusal arrived with its reasoning and a signable alternative, which is what lets a procurement counterpart say yes. The confidentiality framing of the MFN refusal converted a defensive no into a client benefit.
Watch outs: the 45 day exception is exactly the kind that quietly becomes the house standard if it is not recorded as an exception with its reason; it was logged in the exceptions register, and the rate sheet was not edited. Also, eleven days of onboarding delay is a real cost on a dated launch; the desk should diary onboarding lead time into future proposals to groups like this rather than discovering it per deal.
