# Example 1: Price Pushback from a Brand Director

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. Prices are taken from the fictional proposal and rate sheet shown in this example, not offered as market rates.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. Currency USD, prices exclude tax.
- Deposit 50 percent. Invoice terms 14 days. Source files excluded, priced at 1,500 per family
- Discount authority: the account executive may offer scope trades only. Rate movement requires the founder
- Floor on this proposal: the founder has set a minimum structure of the Better tier minus the production liaison, rate unchanged

## The proposal they are answering (Better tier, as sent)
Range refresh for Alder & Vine Pantry (fictional), 10 SKUs, 2 families. Total 14,800: strategy and architecture 1,900; master design 2 families 4,600; adaptations 8 SKUs 3,900; production liaison 1,600; project management 1,900; proofing 900. Two revision rounds per phase. Reset deadline: artwork lock in 9 weeks.

## Messy input (email, three days after the proposal)
"Hi, thanks for this, the team is excited about the direction and nobody is questioning the work. The honest problem is Finance. They have put a ceiling on design spend this quarter and your number is about 20 percent over what they will release. I do not want to lose the reset over this. Can you meet me at 11,800 and we sign this week? I have gone to bat for you internally already."

## Reasoning
Classify: price objection, stated as a flat percentage gap (14,800 to 11,800 is 3,000, almost exactly 20 percent).
Diagnose: the wording carries three signals. "Nobody is questioning the work" suggests value is accepted. "Finance has a ceiling" is a budget claim, not a policy percentage. "I have gone to bat for you" is a buyer who has spent internal capital and now needs a deliverable win. Primary diagnosis: Politics with a real budget underneath. She needs something to take back to Finance; the worst answer is a bare no, the second worst is the discount she asked for.
Authority check: 11,800 on the same scope is a rate cut of 20 percent, outside the AE's authority and below the founder's floor structure. Option A (the proposal minus the production liaison) lands exactly on the founder's floor, so the AE may offer it as it stands. Option B trades further, so it goes below the floor structure and is not the AE's to offer alone. The AE escalated Option B before replying, with the numbers and the reasoning, and the founder approved it as a one-off on one condition: the two deferred adaptations are priced openly in the reply at 975, billed when they are done, not quietly folded away. The approvals are logged; the client hears none of that machinery, just the two structures.
Trades available from the proposal as built: remove production liaison (minus 1,600, client runs the printer with a handover call), move proofing to one round (minus 450 of the 900), phase the adaptations: 6 SKUs now, 2 after the reset (975 deferred, billed at the same rates when those adaptations are done). Full trade stack reaches 11,775 now, close to her number, with genuinely less scope. Also offer the internal selling help: a one page summary for Finance showing the reset date consequence.

## Output: the reply (client facing)
"Thank you for being straight about where the constraint sits, and for going to bat. I cannot take 20 percent off the same scope, and I would not trust a studio whose price moved that easily. But if 11,800 is a real ceiling, here is the scope that honestly fits it, and it still hits your reset.
Option A, 13,200: everything in the proposal except production liaison. Your team runs the printer day to day; we supply print ready files, one handover call, and we stay on the hook for file quality. Option B, 11,775 now: Option A, plus proofing at one round instead of two, and two of the eight adaptations land just after the reset instead of before it. Those two adaptations are billed at 975 when they are done, at the same rates, so Option B is 11,775 now and 975 later, not 11,775 all in. The six that matter for the reset shelves are all in.
If it helps with Finance, I can send a one page version of either option showing the fee against the reset date and what delay costs the launch. The full scope also stays available at 14,800 if the ceiling turns out to be quarterly rather than absolute. Tell me which structure to paper and I will have it back to you the same day."

## Outcome (illustrative)
Finance released 13,200. The client chose Option A, kept both proofing rounds and all 10 SKUs for the reset, and ran the printer themselves with two support calls that were logged and later referenced when the liaison was bought back as a change order on the next range.

## Critique
Strengths: the reply treated the buyer as an ally with a problem, gave her two defensible structures instead of a refusal, and tested the ceiling honestly (designing to 11,800 rather than calling her bluff). The one page offer armed her for the internal fight, which was the real negotiation.
Watch outs: the phasing trade (SKUs after the reset) only works because the reset genuinely shelves six first; on a single date launch it would have been a fake trade. Also note the reply never mentioned the founder, the floor, or authority limits. The client heard one confident voice, which is the point of the escalation system behind it.
