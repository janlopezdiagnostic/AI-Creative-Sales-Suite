# Example 8: Fashion Client Pushback, Fees, Usage Rights, and Page Creep

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative. This example runs the desk in the fashion vertical layer: the client is a fashion label, and the pushback is about creative fees, photography usage rights, and lookbook scope two weeks before market week.

## The studio (fictional rate sheet excerpt)
Maren Studio (fictional creative studio selling campaign and lookbook design to fashion labels). Currency USD, prices exclude tax. Quoted scope on the table: a 56 page lookbook and a campaign edit of 10 hero images, at the quoted fee.
- Creative director 950/day, senior designer 750/day, designer 550/day, artworker 450/day, project manager 500/day
- Lookbook scope is priced per designed spread range with a stated page count; additional pages are a change order at the per-page rate in the scope
- Photography and talent usage is licensed, never assigned: priced by term, territory, and media from the usage table in the rate sheet; "in perpetuity, all media, worldwide" is the most expensive line on that table, not a courtesy
- Rush multiplier 1.5x inside 10 working days. Market week dates are fixed by the client calendar and stated in the SOW

## Messy input (email from the label's head of brand)
"Loved the AW lookbook direction. Three things before we sign. Our finance director needs 30 percent off the fee, same scope, she says design fees have come down everywhere. We also need the campaign photography usage in perpetuity, all media, worldwide, included in the fee, our last studio just bundled it. And the wholesale team has asked for 15 more pages in the lookbook (more looks came in), still for the same market week date, two weeks from now. Can you confirm all three today?"

## Reasoning
Classify: three asks bundled into one warm email; separate them per tree 1.
1. Thirty percent off, same scope. Classify: price. Diagnose: process, not value. The finance director is running a standard opening; "design fees have come down everywhere" is an unverifiable market claim, answered the way this desk always answers borrowed benchmarks: walk the deliverables, offer scope trades, never move the rate.
2. Perpetuity, all media, worldwide usage, included. Classify: rights. Diagnose: risk and inexperience more than greed. Usage is a priced asset with a term, territory, and media structure (fashion layer); bundling it free would give away the most valuable line in the deal and would also misprice the photographer and talent licences the studio must clear. The answer prices the ladder: the usage the launch actually needs (one season, named territories, named media) is modest; perpetuity worldwide is available, at the table price, so the client can see what they are really asking for.
3. Fifteen more pages, same immovable date. Classify: scope plus timeline. Diagnose: genuine operational need (more looks arrived), wrong mechanism. Pages are countable, so this is arithmetic: a change order at the per-page rate, plus the rush multiplier the rate sheet sets inside 10 working days, quoted alongside the alternative of a wholesale supplement PDF the studio can produce inside the date at standard rates. Market week cannot move, so the choice is presented as scope vs format, never as absorbing the pages silently.
Response shape: one message, each ask answered in full, warm because this is a good client at the worst week of their season, structured because "confirm today" is the Friday deadline pattern the desk does not bend for.

## Output: the reply (client facing, excerpt)
"Glad the direction landed. Let me take the three points in order, because each has a different answer.
On the fee: our rates are our rates, and 30 percent off the same scope is not something I can do, or something I would trust from a studio that could. What I can do is resize: the lookbook at 40 pages instead of 56, or the campaign edit at 6 hero images instead of 10, both priced properly below. The full scope stays at the quoted fee whenever finance wants it.
On usage: photography usage is licensed, not bundled, because it is not ours to give away free: the photographer and talent licences price by term, territory, and media. The usage your launch actually needs, one AW season, [territories], wholesale and DTC channels, is [price from the usage table] and is probably what your last studio bundled. Perpetuity, all media, worldwide is [price from the usage table]. It is on the table if you want it; seeing both numbers is the honest way to choose.
On the extra pages: 15 more pages is a change order at the per-page rate in the scope, and because we are inside 10 working days of market week, the rate sheet's rush terms apply to the added pages only, not the whole job. Priced both ways on the attached note. If the pages matter more than the print, the faster route is a wholesale supplement PDF at standard rates, which your team can also send digitally after the showroom appointments.
Tell me which combination you want and I will paper it today."

## Outcome (illustrative)
The label took the full 56 page scope at the quoted fee, bought the one season usage package, and chose the change order for the added pages at the rush rate after seeing the supplement option. Finance's 30 percent was never mentioned again once the usage ladder showed where the real money in the deal sat.

## Critique
Strengths: the desk rules travelled into the fashion layer intact: classify and separate bundled asks, trade scope never rate, price rights as a ladder instead of refusing them, and convert deadline pressure into a format choice with real numbers. The usage answer also protected the studio legally: clearing perpetuity worldwide on a finance director's say-so, without the underlying photographer and talent licences allowing it, would have been a breach presented as a favour.
Watch outs: the per-page and usage numbers must come from the studio's real rate sheet and usage table; an invented usage price quoted in market week becomes the precedent every future season negotiates against. Also note the reply's discipline about what it did not do: it never criticised the client's previous studio, whose "bundled" usage was almost certainly narrower than the client remembered.
