# Example 5: RFP Triage, a Polite Decline

All brands, people, and prices in this example are fictional and illustrative.

## The studio (fictional policy excerpt)
Hartwell Studio. RFP threshold: 15 of 24 on the triage scorecard, and no automatic fails. Authorised bid cost above 2 days requires founder sign-off. The studio does not produce free creative at submission stage under any score.

## Messy input (the RFP, as received, summarised faithfully by the AE)
From the procurement portal of Bellweather Group (fictional, national retailer): "Invitation to tender: own label packaging design services." Requirements: full creative routes for two own label ranges (a route per range, print ready standard visuals), a rate schedule, company accounts for two years, insurance at levels listed, references. Submission in 12 working days. Evaluation "by the buying team". No budget indicated anywhere. Questions in writing only, answered to all bidders. The incumbent supplier is named in the portal footer as the portal's case study. No brand or design contact is named; the buying team's decision date is "in due course".

## Reasoning
Run the scorecard (templates/rfp-triage-scorecard.md):
1. Sponsor: 0. Procurement only, no named human beyond the portal.
2. Real work: 1. Own label design is real work in general, but no named project, range specifics, or volumes are given.
3. Budget: 0. Absent entirely.
4. Access to decision maker: 0. Written questions to all bidders only.
5. Creative demanded: 0. Full routes at submission, free.
6. Timeline: 0. Twelve working days for two ranges of routes plus full compliance paperwork, decision "in due course".
7. Incumbent position: 0. The incumbent is being showcased on the same portal.
8. Fit: 2. Own label food packaging is squarely the studio's portfolio.
9. First year value vs bid cost: 0. Bid cost conservatively 6 to 8 days of senior time (two routes, compliance, accounts pack) against unknown, unstated value.
10. Terms preview: 1. Unknown; portal terms unseen at triage.
11. Admin cost: 1. Heavy pack, partly reusable.
12. Strategic value: 1. A national own label roster place would be useful if real.
Total: 6 of 24, against a threshold of 15. Automatic fails also trigger: free creative at submission AND no budget AND no sponsor (all three), and the contact cannot name who decides beyond "the buying team".
Decision: decline, warmly, the same week. Not a negotiation of the process, because no line of the process is fixable by the studio from outside a portal with no named human. Log the decline, the score, and the two facts that would change the answer (a named brand side sponsor, or creative removed from the submission stage).

## Output: the decline (sent through the portal message function and by email to the procurement address)
"Thank you for the invitation to tender for own label packaging design services. We have reviewed the requirements carefully, and we are going to decline on this occasion.
Two reasons, honestly stated. First, capacity: our current clients have our design team committed through this quarter, and a submission of this size done properly would need senior time we cannot honestly promise inside the window. Second, method: we do not produce creative routes ahead of an engagement. It is a standing policy that protects our paying clients' place in the queue, and it would apply equally to work we did for you.
If a specific own label project comes up where credentials, approach, and a paid first phase would be a workable format, we would be glad to be considered, and our portfolio in this category is strong. Our details will remain on the portal for that route.
Thank you again for thinking of us."

## Outcome (illustrative)
No reply, which is the expected outcome of a portal decline and not a failure of it. The studio spent 40 minutes on the triage and decline instead of 7 days on a bid. Five months later a named category manager from the same group emailed about a specific seasonal range, referencing the decline ("you were the only studio that explained itself"), and that conversation started with a sponsor, a budget, and no free creative requested.

## Critique
Strengths: the triage was scored, not felt, which is what makes a decline defensible inside the studio when the logo is famous. The decline gave the two reasons that are both true and reputationally safe, and left a specific, plausible route back that did in fact get used.
Watch outs: the capacity reason must stay honest. If the studio had been empty that quarter, the decline would have rested on method alone, and that is sufficient; inventing a capacity reason because it sounds softer is the kind of small fiction that tends to surface later.
