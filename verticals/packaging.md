# Vertical Layer: Packaging

Load this file alongside any core skill when the studio sells packaging design or the target client buys it. It overrides generic examples with packaging reality. Where this file and a core skill disagree, this file wins for packaging work.

## The buyer map

- **Brand / marketing director (the taste buyer).** Owns shelf impact and brand consistency. Judges your portfolio first. Cares about: differentiation at 3 feet and at thumbnail size, range architecture, speed to shelf.
- **Founder (DTC and challenger brands).** Taste and budget in one person. Fast decisions, price-sensitive, relationship-driven. Watch for unpaid "quick concepts" asks.
- **Procurement / sourcing (larger brands).** Enters after the taste buyer is sold. Buys by rate benchmarking and panel processes. Needs: rate card logic, vendor onboarding docs, insurance and compliance paperwork. Never let procurement be the first conversation.
- **Production / print partners.** Not buyers, but they kill or bless feasibility. A studio that speaks production fluently (dielines, substrates, print processes, colour limits) wins trust with every other stakeholder.

## Deal shapes

- **Line extension:** new SKUs into an existing range. Fast, lower fee, the classic foot in the door. Price per SKU with a base architecture fee, never pure per-SKU with no base.
- **Range redesign / refresh:** the flagship deal. Structural + artwork + guidelines. Multi-stakeholder, 2-6 month cycle.
- **New brand / NPD launch:** strategy, naming sometimes, structural, artwork, production support. Highest fee, highest scope risk: lock the SKU count and the revision rounds.
- **Retailer-driven work:** exclusive ranges, seasonal packs, value-tier lines. Deadline is the retailer's reset date and it does not move.
- **Retainer shapes:** range guardianship (ongoing SKU extensions, seasonal variants, artwork updates), NPD pipeline support. The retainer pitch lands right after a successful launch, timed to the client's planning cycle.

## Calendars and timing signals

- **Retail resets / range reviews:** major grocers and mass retailers reset categories on fixed cycles, commonly spring and autumn planogram changes; brands pitch months ahead. A reset announcement is a research signal with a built-in deadline: work backwards, artwork lock is usually 8-12 weeks before shelf date `[VERIFY PER RETAILER]`.
- **Seasonal:** holiday, back-to-school, summer. Seasonal packaging starts 2-3 quarters before it shelves.
- **Regulatory clocks:** labelling and packaging rules create forced redesigns: extended producer responsibility (EPR) fees and reporting (EU PPWR phasing in from 2025-2026; US state EPR laws in CA, CO, OR, MN, ME, WA and others rolling out), recyclability labelling, nutrition/health claim rules, deposit schemes. A compliance deadline is the least ignorable sales trigger in this vertical: the work is mandatory and dated. Verify the specific rule and date for the client's market before citing it in outreach.
- **Trade moments:** packaging awards, Pack Expo and similar shows, and category reviews concentrate buying attention. Watch for new CMO / head-of-brand hires: the first 90 days (a practitioner rule of thumb, not a measured constant) is the highest-receptivity window in B2B creative sales.

## Vocabulary the skill must use correctly

Dieline, structural design, substrate, print process (flexo, litho, gravure, digital), colour separations, spot colour vs CMYK, varnishes and finishes, cutter guide, artwork vs repro, range architecture, tiering (good/better/best on shelf), shelf standout / blocking, own-label vs branded, SKU rationalisation, PPWR, EPR, How2Recycle labelling.

## Proof types that land

Before/after shelf photography, range architecture diagrams, sales or distribution outcomes where the client permits citing them, print-production competence (show a dieline, talk substrates), speed to shelf on a retailer deadline. Never cite client results you cannot source; mark anything the studio claims but has not documented as `[TO CONFIRM]` in drafts.

## Production and pre-press reality (where naive scopes bleed)

Design adaptation and mechanical artworking are different jobs and price separately. A scope that says "artwork" without naming the pre-press layer is under-scoped. The layer includes: repro and trapping, colour separations and drawdowns, wet proofs vs digital proofs (named, and who pays), cutter guide / dieline sign-off responsibility, plate or cylinder charges (usually the printer's, passed through or the client's direct, but the SOW must say which), barcode creation and verification, and 3D pack renders or physical comps when a client needs to sell the range internally. The Scope and Pricing Architect's rate sheet template carries fields for each of these; if the studio's sheet leaves them blank, they come back as `[TO CONFIRM]`, which is the correct behaviour, not a gap in the output.

## Pricing norms (structure, not numbers)

Numbers always come from the studio's own rate sheet. Structures that are normal here: per-project fixed fee by phase (strategy / structural / artwork / production support priced separately), per-SKU artwork fees on top of a range architecture base, print/production support by day, usage and source-file terms stated explicitly (native artwork and dieline files are deliverables only if priced; structural IP and cutter ownership stated in the SOW), revision rounds capped per phase with artwork amends beyond cap billed per round or per hour per the rate sheet.

## Packaging-specific traps (feed these into the core skills' edge cases)

1. The SKU count grows mid-project ("just two more flavours") without a change order.
2. The client assumes native/source files are included; the studio assumed the opposite.
3. Regulatory copy changes arrive after artwork lock and get framed as "minor amends."
4. The retailer moves the reset date forward; rush terms were never agreed.
5. Procurement benchmarks the studio's day rate against freelance artworkers, ignoring strategy and structural phases.
6. Sustainability claims in the pitch that the substrate cannot support (greenwashing risk, legal exposure for the client): never propose a claim the materials cannot back.
