# Vertical Layer: Brand & Product Design

Load this file alongside any core skill when the studio sells brand identity, brand systems, or brand/product design for digital products. It overrides generic examples with brand-design reality. Where this file and a core skill disagree, this file wins for brand and product design work.

## The buyer map

- **CMO / VP brand (the mandate buyer).** Owns the brand as a business asset and the rebrand decision itself. Judges strategic sharpness first, then portfolio. Cares about: board and CEO credibility, differentiation, internal adoption, and a rollout that does not embarrass anyone.
- **Founder (startups and scale-ups).** Taste, budget, and final sign-off in one person. Often buying the brand they could not afford at founding. Fast decisions, strong opinions, relationship-driven. Watch for strategy being skipped in favour of jumping straight to a logo.
- **Product marketing lead.** The day-to-day owner on digital product work: launches, naming, messaging, in-product brand. Cares about shipping dates and sales enablement, and will quietly expand a brand scope into launch support if nobody prices it.
- **Head of design / design director (client side).** The professional peer. Judges craft and systems thinking hardest, and decides whether your studio's work will survive contact with their in-house team. Sell them governance and handoff quality, not just the launch moment.
- **Procurement.** Arrives at larger companies once the mandate is set. Rate benchmarks and panel logic; keep the strategic conversation with the mandate buyer.

## Deal shapes

- **Full rebrand:** strategy, naming sometimes, identity system, guidelines, rollout support. The flagship deal: multi-stakeholder, 3-9 month cycle, priced by phase with rollout as its own priced phase, never a free tail.
- **Identity system (new brand):** strategy lite, identity, core applications, starter guidelines. The scale-up staple after a funding round.
- **Design system & guidelines build:** tokens, component libraries, brand portals, governance rules. Systems work, sold on durability, priced separately from identity creation because the buyer and the success measure are different.
- **Brand for digital products:** product identity, UI brand layer, design language for apps and platforms. Bridges brand and product teams; scope must name which surfaces are in (app, web, marketing site, product UI) and which are explicitly not.
- **Campaign vs AOR:** campaign identity and key visuals are projects with a season's life; agency-of-record and brand guardianship retainers are the compounding form: ongoing governance, new applications, internal training, rollout waves. The retainer pitch lands after launch, when the client discovers how many touchpoints they actually own.

## Calendars and timing signals

- **Funding rounds:** an announced raise is the classic trigger: the identity bought at seed no longer fits the company being built. The buying window opens at announcement and the work is usually wanted before the next raise or a major launch `[VERIFY PER CLIENT]`.
- **Product launches:** a flagship launch, a new product line, or entry into a new category forces brand decisions on a date set by engineering and sales, not by the brand team. Work backwards from launch: identity decisions need lock months ahead if physical or retail touchpoints are involved.
- **Leadership changes:** new CMO or VP brand is the highest-receptivity window in this vertical too: the first 90 days, strongest roughly days 14 to 90 (a practitioner rule of thumb, not a measured constant). A new leader inherits a brand they did not build and is licensed to question it.
- **Pre-IPO and M&A:** pre-IPO companies tidy the brand for scrutiny; acquirers and the acquired both face brand architecture decisions (endorse, merge, retire) on integration timelines set by the deal, not by preference. Announcements are public signals; the real buying window is the integration planning phase after close.
- **Fiscal planning:** enterprise brand budgets are set in annual planning cycles; a rebrand proposed after budgets lock usually waits a year or gets descoped. Ask when their planning cycle closes.

## Vocabulary the skill must use correctly

Identity system, brand architecture (branded house, house of brands, endorsed, hybrid), naming architecture, design tokens, brand guidelines, governance, rollout, touchpoint, brand portal, asset management, lockup, sub-brand, endorsement, migration path, trademark clearance, design language system.

## Proof types that land

Before/after identity comparisons with the business reason stated, architecture diagrams that untangle a messy portfolio, rollout case studies (how the system reached every touchpoint without breaking), governance proof (a guidelines system a client's in-house team actually uses a year later), token and component libraries shown working in the client's own stack. Never cite client results you cannot source; mark anything the studio claims but has not documented as `[TO CONFIRM]` in drafts.

## Production and handoff reality (where naive scopes bleed)

An identity is not finished at the logo reveal. The bleed points: asset production across dozens of formats, guideline documentation at a depth an in-house junior can execute, token and component handoff into the client's Figma and code stack, font and image licensing transferred correctly, and training the people who will live in the system. Price documentation and handoff as named deliverables with named depths (a 12-page starter guide and a governed brand portal are different products at different prices). Trademark clearance and legal sequencing sit with the client's counsel; the scope should name that dependency, because a naming or identity direction that fails clearance late is a schedule event, not a design revision.

## Pricing norms (structure, not numbers)

Numbers always come from the studio's own rate sheet. Structures that are normal here: fixed fee by phase (strategy / identity / system & guidelines / rollout support priced as separate phases with separate approvals), naming as its own priced workstream, guidelines depth tiered, rollout support by wave or by day, token and component library build priced separately from identity creation, licensing and source-file terms stated explicitly (native files and working files are deliverables only if priced), and guardianship retainers priced monthly against a defined service menu, not open-ended availability.

## Brand & product design traps (feed these into the core skills' edge cases)

1. The rebrand scope says "identity" and the client hears every touchpoint: signage, fleet, uniforms, app, wayfinding, templates. If the touchpoint inventory is not an appendix, the scope is fiction.
2. Guidelines nobody maintains: a static PDF delivered into a company with no brand owner decays within a year. Sell governance (an owner, an update cadence, a portal) or say plainly the document is a snapshot.
3. Token and system handoff to an in-house team with no training, no documentation depth, and no support window: the system forks within two quarters and the studio gets blamed for the fork.
4. Trademark and legal sequencing ignored until the launch date is set: a name or mark that fails clearance late forces a rushed rename that nobody scoped or priced.
5. Strategy sold, then skipped under time pressure, with the identity judged later against a strategy nobody agreed: keep the strategy approval as a signed gate before design begins.
6. The founder or CMO who approved the direction leaves mid-rollout; their successor reopens decisions. Get approvals in writing per phase, from the role, not just the person.
