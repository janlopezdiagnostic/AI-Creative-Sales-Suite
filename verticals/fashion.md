# Vertical Layer: Fashion

Load this file alongside any core skill when the studio sells creative and design services to fashion labels, or the target client buys them. It overrides generic examples with fashion reality. Where this file and a core skill disagree, this file wins for fashion work.

## The buyer map

- **Creative director (the taste buyer).** Owns the visual world of the label. Judges your eye before your process, and your portfolio's cultural fluency before either. Cares about: image quality, casting and styling credibility, and whether you understand the references without having them explained.
- **Head of brand / marketing director.** Owns campaigns, launches, and the calendar. The commercial counterpart to the creative director: cares about sell-through, launch impact, and assets delivered in time to matter.
- **Wholesale director.** Buys showroom and selling tools: lookbooks, line sheets, order forms, showroom graphics. Judges on whether accounts reorder and whether the tools make selling easier in a 20-minute appointment.
- **Founder (independent labels).** Taste, budget, and survival in one person. Seasonal cash flow is real: the same founder is generous after a good season and frozen before market week. Time your asks to their cash calendar, not yours.

## Deal shapes

- **Seasonal campaign & lookbook:** the recurring core deal. Concept, shoot production partnership, lookbook design, campaign assets sized for every channel. Twice a year minimum (SS and AW), plus pre-collections or capsules at larger labels.
- **Collection launch identity:** naming, graphics, and the visual system for a new line, diffusion label, or collaboration. One-off, high visibility, often the entry deal.
- **Showroom & wholesale tools:** line sheets, lookbooks for buyers, order systems, showroom and trade show graphics. Unglamorous, recurring, and sticky: whoever makes the selling tools is hard to replace.
- **DTC site refresh:** e-commerce design and campaign landing systems. Sold on conversion and drop performance; scope must separate the site system from the per-drop asset production, which is its own recurring line.
- **Licensing collaborations:** capsule collections and licensed product lines with partners (retailers, artists, other brands). Approval chains double: two brand owners sign off everything, and usage rights are negotiated per partner.

## Calendars and timing signals

- **SS/AW calendar:** the industry runs two main seasons, spring/summer and autumn/winter, with selling and delivery months apart. Creative work for a season starts two to three quarters before product reaches stores; by the time a collection is visible publicly, the next season's creative is already in production `[VERIFY PER LABEL]`.
- **Market weeks:** wholesale selling concentrates around the fashion weeks and market periods in New York, London, Milan, and Paris, plus regional showroom weeks. Lookbooks and line sheets must exist before market week opens; a late line sheet is not late, it is worthless. Work backwards from the selling date, not the launch date.
- **Sample lead times:** creative decisions wait on physical samples. Shoot dates are set by when samples land, and samples slip. Build the schedule around sample delivery as an explicit dependency with a stated fallback (previous season product, showroom samples, or reshoot windows).
- **Drop culture:** DTC and streetwear-influenced labels release in drops, not seasons: shorter cycles, higher frequency, asset production as a standing rhythm rather than a campaign event. Price drop support as a retainer or a per-drop rate, never inside a seasonal campaign fee.
- **Leadership and funding signals:** a new creative director appointment resets a label's whole visual world and its supplier list, and the buying window is the first 90 days, strongest roughly days 14 to 90 (a practitioner rule of thumb, not a measured constant). New investment or a new retail account (a first major wholesale door) forces wholesale tools and campaign upgrades on the retailer's timeline.

## Vocabulary the skill must use correctly

Lookbook, line sheet, tech pack (know what it is, a garment construction specification, and that it belongs to product teams, not to this sale), range plan, sell-in vs sell-through, market week, capsule, drop, pre-collection, carryover, door (a retail stockist), showroom, campaign vs e-commerce imagery, usage buyout.

## Proof types that land

Campaign and lookbook work the buyer recognises culturally, sell-through or reorder outcomes where the client permits citing them, wholesale tools that real buyers used in market week, drop assets that shipped on schedule across a season, fluency in the label's reference world shown in the work itself. Never cite client results you cannot source; mark anything the studio claims but has not documented as `[TO CONFIRM]` in drafts.

## Production and rights reality (where naive scopes bleed)

Fashion creative is photography-led, and photography is rights-managed territory. Every shoot scope must state: usage term (how long), territory, media (print, digital, OOH, social, wholesale use), and exclusivity, for photography, talent, and styling where applicable. A rate agreed for one season's campaign in one territory does not quietly cover next season in another. Retouching rounds, selects counts, and who holds the raw files belong in the scope. Sample logistics (shipping, customs carnets for international shoots, insurance) are real costs that naive scopes absorb silently; name who carries them.

## Pricing norms (structure, not numbers)

Numbers always come from the studio's own rate sheet. Structures that are normal here: per-season campaign fee by phase (concept / production support / design & asset production priced separately), lookbook priced per edition with page or look counts named, line sheets and wholesale tools priced per season as a package, usage and buyout terms stated explicitly per shoot (term, territory, media) and priced on top of production, retouching rounds capped with extra rounds billed per rate sheet, drop support as a monthly retainer or per-drop fee, and rush terms agreed before market week because the deadline cannot move.

## Fashion-specific traps (feed these into the core skills' edge cases)

1. The seasonal deadline cannot move: market week opens on its date whether the line sheet is ready or not. Any scope without a sample-delivery dependency and a lateness clause is a promise to absorb the client's delays.
2. Sample-stage scope changes: the collection itself changes while creative is in production (looks added, key pieces dropped), and the lookbook page count and shoot plan silently follow it. Price per look and per page so changes price themselves.
3. Image usage rights agreed loosely ("for the campaign") then used across seasons, territories, and a wholesale partner's channels. Usage is a priced term with four parts (term, territory, media, exclusivity), never a vibe.
4. Wholesale vs DTC buyer conflicts: the wholesale director wants price-forward selling tools; the DTC lead wants brand heat and no visible discounting. One asset set rarely serves both; scope the two outputs separately or name the compromise in writing.
5. The founder's cash calendar ignored: fees fall due in the label's leanest pre-market month and payment stalls, not from bad faith but from seasonality. Align the payment schedule to sell-in and delivery cash points at signing.
6. Creative director turnover mid-season: the incoming director disowns the outgoing director's campaign direction. Approvals in writing per phase, and a kill/re-scope clause that survives the personnel change.
