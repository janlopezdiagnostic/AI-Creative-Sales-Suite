# Skill: Rough Notes to Bid (Contractors & Home Services) - Lite

Turn rough site notes or a voice-memo transcript into a professional, itemized client proposal. Lite version: core workflow only. The Pro Pack adds the full exclusion-clause library by trade, change-order and follow-up sequences, and printable/Google Docs formats.

## Setup (once)

Fill in and keep with this skill:

```
Business name:
Trade(s):
License # (required on proposals/bids in some states - check yours):
Standard markup / margin approach (e.g. materials at cost +X%, labor rate):
Payment schedule default (e.g. 30% deposit, 40% at midpoint, 30% on completion):
Estimate validity window (days):
Warranty you actually offer:
```

## Rules

1. **Never invent a price.** Use only numbers in the notes or the fact sheet. Missing number = `[TO CONFIRM]` in the output, listed at the top.
2. **Separate scope into three buckets:** included, excluded, and optional add-ons. Never bury an exclusion inside a paragraph.
3. **Flag risk items from the notes** (rot, access problems, permits, utility lines, material volatility) as explicit assumptions or exclusions, in plain language a homeowner understands.
4. **Plain language, no sales hype.** Homeowners sign what they understand.
5. **Drafting aid only.** The client-facing proposal carries no AI disclaimer. Instead, the output's operator-only section (item 1 below) opens with: "Draft generated with AI assistance. Review all scope, pricing, and terms before sending. Not legal advice." Also flag in that section whether your state requires a right-to-cancel notice for contracts signed at the client's home.

## Workflow

Input: pasted notes or a voice-memo transcript of the site visit.
Output, in order:

1. **Questions / to-confirm list** (anything missing that changes price or scope)
2. **Proposal**: client + job summary, itemized scope with line prices where known, timeline estimate, payment schedule
3. **Exclusions & assumptions** (bulleted, plain language)
4. **Optional add-ons** with prices if known
5. **Change-order paragraph**: how changes get priced and approved in writing before work proceeds
6. **Follow-up message**: a short, friendly text/email the contractor can send with the proposal
